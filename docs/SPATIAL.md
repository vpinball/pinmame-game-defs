# Spatial placement

On-demand companion to `docs/INSTRUCTIONS.md`. Read it before step 5 of the per-game workflow, and before placing any device from a table or a factory drawing; it is not loaded by default.
The runbook wins where the two disagree.

**Belongs here:** how to take, measure, normalize, project and validate device placements from retained tables and factory drawings.

**Does not belong here:** the coordinate convention and spatial-record requirements every session needs (`docs/INSTRUCTIONS.md`), or one machine's placements (its spatial report).

## Rules

When a factory drawing is the only defensible coordinate source, make the measurement reproducible: retain a tight native-resolution crop, identify the exact full-page render and its pixel dimensions, record the playfield-frame corners, state whether each measured point is a device symbol, physical socket, remote callout balloon, or leader endpoint, list the source pixel centers and normalization formula, and round only to the precision the scan supports. Reconcile the manual frame against at least one exact retained-table control point before mixing manual- and VPX-derived placements in one normalized space. A callout's leader tip marks the part the callout names, not a point on it: when a modelled table object lies on or beside that part within the fit's accuracy, the drawing is consistent with the object and does not overrule it. A drawing inset can have its own, non-uniform scale; fit it separately from the main drawing, and treat a point far outside its control cluster as a coarse cross-check, never as a coordinate. Placement provenance must name both the coordinate source and any manual/script source needed to justify a shared anchor.

A retained table object's stored centre is not always a playfield coordinate: collidable primitives are sometimes stored as a local offset with the geometry baked into the mesh. Use the centre of the world-space mesh bounds from `vpxtool export obj --units vpu` for such objects, record the export and its hash, and never take a primitive's `position` field on trust.

A VPX `Flasher` object is usually a glow or reflection sprite, often parked on a cabinet side wall or shared between outputs, and is not evidence of a socket; place a flasher on a script-driven `Light` at a modelled dome or lens, or on a factory drawing. When a primitive's geometry is baked into its mesh, check the mesh-derived position against independently positioned control points in the same table before relying on it.

Take a placement's table object from what the script actually binds, never from its name alone. `vpmMapLights` drives a light from its `TimerInterval`, and only if the light is in the mapped collection, so a light named for one lamp can carry another lamp's number or none. A switch can be bound through a differently named spinner or kicker while a same-numbered trigger has no handler at all, and a lamp-named light can be lit from a solenoid callback. Record any such table defect on the device. When a factory drawing reconciles placements, read its control pixels on the drawing itself and keep the crops. Control pixels that reproduce a frame formula applied to the table coordinates, with sub-pixel residuals, are circular: they reconcile nothing and must not be cited as a check.

Map lamps to physical bulbs, not primitives or lightmap helpers. A lamp or flasher placement names one bulb object's own center: never a centroid of several objects, a reflection, bloom or `F_refl`-style sprite, an insert overlay, or a glow the table paints on the target the bulb lights. When the manual's location drawing puts the bulb somewhere other than the script-bound light, measure it on the drawing and prefer a table object at that spot. Reconcile shared RGB channels and co-located emitters explicitly. For GI, distinguish playfield bulbs, rear-panel bulbs, and coin-door/cabinet bulbs; playfield/rear placements and cabinet quantity may need different treatment. Cabinet/backbox displays require controlled `not_applicable` spatial evidence with PinMAME core and manual/human-review provenance. A display physically fitted to the playfield uses a located display placement only when the manual establishes its physical fitment, PinMAME identifies its callback, and a VPX object or reviewed measurement supports its centre; an emulator rendering flag does not determine cabinet location.

## Lessons

Each lesson ends with the lines of `docs/archive/current-state-ledger-until-2026-10-01.md` that
record the case behind it, and with `verified:` where it was checked against the repository code or
pinned PinMAME source on 2026-10-01. A lesson added later names the commit or knowledge note that
produced it instead.

- **Callout limit.** Fit each drawing on validated placements. A table object matches its callout only if every read is within the fit's leave-one-out error and within 0.07 normalized; otherwise the placement stays `observed`. When outlying controls inflate leave-one-out, 0.07 alone decides. Projections are exempt. The 0.07 is a per-curator convention, so state it in the spatial report. (archive L270, L662; verified: curate_indianapolis_500.py, curate_twilight_zone.py)
- **Callout check.** `tools/drawing_callouts.py` makes the callout limit reproducible from a committed seed: every callout an independent reader transcribed on each drawing without table data, the verifier's corrections with their reasons, and per-page jet-bumper and flipper-pivot controls. Placements and callouts of one label pair one-to-one, nearest first, so one drawn callout never validates two bulbs. Readers stop at crossings and take balloon centres for leader ends, so overlay every page before accepting it. Never check a placement measured on the same drawing, and when a documented finding says a leader ends on another part, exclude that placement instead of letting distance decide. Its control fit needs at least four controls spanning half the playfield height; where balloons hide the flipper pivots, read another crisp mechanism feature such as an inlane rollover slot. The callout fit covers the whole page and each leave-one-out offset is that placement's own prediction error, so the controls' extent does not bound which placements this check can validate; the extrapolation rule below governs drawings reconciled through controls alone. (2026-10-01 callout checks in the Guns N' Roses, WHO dunnit, World Poker Tour and Taxi spatial reports)
- **A corroborating fit does not move the table's coordinate.** When a drawing fit checks table placements, a pass confirms count, side and order and the coordinate still comes from the table; a drawing supplies a coordinate only through the reproducible-measurement rule above. A fit is visual reconciliation, not proof, since the table author may have traced the same manual. Leader-line counts give bulb quantity. (archive L118, L662, L898)
- **Frames and controls.** Give each page its own fit; never reuse another page's frame. Reject any control whose bounding box spans much of the table. Mark a placement outside the controls' extent as extrapolated and keep it `observed`. (archive L118, L450, L771)
- **Several tables.** Validate a position only when every retained factory-layout table agrees within 0.025 normalized, with each table's object chosen through its own script binding. A table derived from, or ancestral to, another retained table can only supplement. (archive L662, L1038; verified: lotr_spatial_resolve.py)
- **Mechanism projections.** Project a sensor inside a mechanism onto that mechanism's own object; the sensors of one rotating figure may share a coordinate. Children of a tilting group share a local coordinate, so use a playfield-fixed object at each one's spot instead. An emulator that derives a mechanism's switches from a position counter says nothing about the physical sensors: place fitted sensors from their own geometry when the table or a drawing provides it, and project onto the mechanism's fixed object only when no usable sensor geometry exists. (archive L640, L683, L695, L794)
- **Placeholders and helpers.** Look for placeholder rows (unplaced lamps lined up along an edge) and `is_visible=false` animation helpers. If same-named mechanism objects still disagree once the helpers are dropped, omit `spatial` rather than pick or average. (archive L651, L823, L827)
- **Picking the bulb.** Collapse render doubles to the member with `show_bulb_mesh`, then to the smallest falloff radius. When no light shows its own bulb and the table models its bulbs in one baked mesh, split that mesh's world-space OBJ export into connected pieces and count one emitter per piece a light sits on; a distance threshold alone left Tales from the Crypt's glow helpers, 12-24 units from their bulbs, as extra emitters (commit "defs: apply the Tales from the Crypt review findings"). Place a Flupper dome on its `FlasherbaseN`. A table's GI grouping is not the machine's wiring, so keep those placements `observed`, without a quantity. (archive L390, L594, L718)
- **Existence and placement are separate.** A `used` device may have no `spatial`, which keeps the record partial. A WPC-95 flasher wired to both a playfield bulb and an insert-panel bulb counts both in `quantity` but places only the playfield bulb. (archive L390, L763; verified: validation.py)
- **Out-of-range objects.** `extract_spatial_candidates` drops them silently, so check the raw extraction. Exclude a stray member when the device has other placements. Clamp a sole object to the edge, with its raw value disclosed, only when the manual puts the device at that edge. Omit `spatial` when the object lies off the playfield plane. (archive L606, L640, L683; verified: spatial.py)
- **A symbol diagram cannot validate.** A lamp drawing that shows only lamp symbols inside an outline, with no flipper or other mechanism feature, has no independent control fit, so `tools/drawing_callouts.py` cannot check it and its lamps stay `observed`; the outline's corners are not controls when they do not match the table bounds. Where a callout leader runs into a drawn flasher symbol, the symbol's centre is the part. (spatial report `reports/spatial/bally/safe-cracker-1996.md`)

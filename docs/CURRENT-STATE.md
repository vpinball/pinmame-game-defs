Williams Medieval Madness (`williams.medieval-madness.1997`) was promoted on 2026-08-05 and is the first author-ready Williams WPC machine. It introduced the `pinmame.wpc-95` controller profile, which every later WPC-95 game should reuse instead of `pinmame.wpc-alpha`.

Bally Attack From Mars (`bally.attack-from-mars.1995`) was promoted on 2026-08-05 as the second WPC-95 machine, incorporating and superseding external PR #2. It is the first definition to enumerate PinMAME's two auxiliary lamp columns: `afmGameData` declares `lampCol = 2`, and the sixteen saucer-L.E.D. addresses 91-98 and 101-108 are shifted in serially by public solenoids 37 and 38. Reuse that pattern for any other game whose `core_tGameData` declares extra lamp columns, and note that WPC-95 auxiliary outputs 37-40 are mirrored at 41-44 while `custSol` duplicates appear from 51 upward. Its curation also recorded a genuine pinned-PinMAME defect worth remembering: `afm.c` names the two loop gates the opposite way round from the manual and from both retained known-working scripts, and is internally inconsistent because its `/* 33 */` and `/* 34 */` comments contradict its own `WPC_FLIPPERCOIL95` bit reads. Verify emulator-side left/right naming against the printed table before trusting it.

Bally Centaur (`bally.centaur.1981`) is claimed as maintainer-local pending work and is in progress on branch `centaur-1981`, worktree `<working-root>/worktrees/pinmame-game-defs-centaur`. It remains `partial`. Identity, the full 48-position switch matrix, solenoid semantics, the display inventory and provenance are now validated; `coverage.missing` names what is not: input and output enumeration, input/output semantics, mechanism inventory and behaviour, polarity, variant differences, provenance, recreation notes, spatial placement, and one unresolved conflict on continuous output 1. Two independent high-tier reviews (a cross-provider `gpt-5.6-sol` review and an `opus` review) were run against the contribution; both independently reproduced the self-test mapping and every `by35.json` address rule from pinned source, and their findings are integrated. `tools/curate_centaur.py` regenerates the definition byte-for-byte from `tools/curate_centaur.json` and has a `--check` drift gate.

Centaur introduced `controllers/pinmame/by35.json`, the Bally MPU AS-2518-35 profile that every later BY35-driver machine should reuse. Its rules are: public momentary solenoid = PIA1:B selector + 1, so 1-15 with no address 16 because selector 15 is the idle state; four continuous outputs at 17-20; a six-column switch matrix published as sequential positions 1-48, not column-times-ten notation, with the sixth column strobed from PIA1:B bit 7 and the three diagnostic contacts at -7 to -5; and a lamp strobe that skips address 15 and advances two matrix columns per data bit, so 16, 32, 48, 64, 80, 96, 112 and 128 are unaddressable decoder slots rather than unused lamps. A non-zero `lampCol` adds a second lamp board at 65-127.

The lesson worth carrying to every other Bally and Stern BY35 machine is that a manual's printed **"Self Test #"** column is a test *order*, not a public address, and the two are unrelated. The manual itself says the number the ROM displays during its solenoid test is the number assigned to that solenoid, so the LibPinMAME harness resolves the mapping directly by pairing each pulsed address with the displayed number. For Centaur that exposed three traps: printed 14 and 15 are transposed against public 14 and 15, public 7 is the outhole kicker and not the ball release both retained scripts call it, and public 17 is a continuously asserted line that the printed table does not account for, whose function is recorded as an unresolved conflict because PinMAME's LISY bridge treats Centaur's continuous bit 0 as a strobe while `by35.c` takes the sixth switch column from bit 7. Two general lessons come out of that. A continuous output that is held never pulses, so never read the absence of a pulse as proof that such an address is unused. And on any six-column BY35 game one of the four continuous solenoid addresses carries a switch strobe rather than a coil, so treat a permanently asserted address in 17-20 as a candidate strobe and resolve it per game. It also exposed two defects in the legacy import, a missing public solenoid 4 that shifted the right four-bank labels and a completely absent magnet. Do not assume the printed and public numbering agree on any BY35 game; run the self test.

Its remaining blockers are the mechanism set (inline drop bank, right four-bank with four individual down coils plus one reset, ORBS bank, trough and sub-trough ball path, upper-right magnet), per-switch polarity, the lamp inventory, the thirty-two unenumerated MPU option switches, and spatial placement. The retained manual scan contains no lamp identification table, and only sixteen of the auxiliary lamp board's reserved addresses have been observed in use, so the lamp set is genuinely open. Figure V on printed page 18 places every switch and solenoid and states which are off the playfield, so it is the reconciliation reference when placements are authored.

The physical family is `centaur` (Bally, 1981) with `centaura` and `centaurb`, the 2004 and 2008 Bally/Oliver free-play ROMs, which are later firmware for the same physical machine rather than new games.

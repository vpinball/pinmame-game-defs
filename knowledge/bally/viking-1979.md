## Identity and variants

This is Bally model 1198, four-player Viking, manufactured July 1980. The prior OPDB note confused these dates. `machine.year` is corrected to 1980 under the physical-release rule; stable ID `bally.viking.1979`, OPDB `G57kN-MQ71K` and file names remain unchanged. This avoids an unnecessary ID migration. Native PinMAME root `viking` already says 1980.

The only pinned clone is `vikingb`, Oliver's 2004 seven-digit conversion. It uses the same GEN_BY35 controller, SNDBRD_BY51 sound, lampCol 0 and playfield I/O. Original four player displays have six digits at starts 2/10/18/26; conversion has seven at 1/9/17/25. Credits and ball/match remain two digits at 35/38. Six public segment callbacks represent five physical modules; PinMAME's synthetic DMD rendering is not a seventh physical display. Conversion firmware date does not create a new physical machine.

## Drop banks and passive scoring parts

The left inline assembly ASE-2993-6 has four targets A (top/rear, switch 1), B (2), C (3), D (bottom/front, 4); the ball first reaches D. Each fallen target holds its own contact until common reset 1 (AN-26-1200). Falling targets successively expose the next, then red standup 5. Multipliers advance 2X/3X/4X/5X. Completing all four lights 25,000 outlanes and changes the rollover-button arrows; the exposed standup awards Special then 50,000. Preserve target-down contact state until reset and return all contacts to open after the reset action; a jam leaves a held/stuck contact visible in diagnostics.

The right assembly ASE-3038-1 has target 1 (top, switch 19), target 2 (center, 18), target 3 (bottom, 17). Public 2 (NO-26-1900) resets the whole bank; independent coils 3/4/5 (CJ-31-2000) release the corresponding target latch down, which closes 19/18/17. Switch 33 is a separate rebound behind the bank, not another target and not a duplicate of the center target. The bank's lit values advance 2000/4000/6000/8000/10000; the 1-2-3 order pays tenfold. Gameplay completing 19, 18, 17 reset the bank. Wire every trip coil to its target even though the retained table leaves callbacks 3/4/5 empty; the same table also omits rebound 33. These are table defects, not unresolved claims about fitted hardware. Factory pulse durations are controller-owned; no unmeasured timing constants are prescribed.

Four thumper bumpers use switches 40/39/38/37 and coils 10/11/12/13 respectively. Right switch 35 fires coil 15; left switch 36 fires coil 14. Each gameplay closure independently fired its matching output. Recreate skirt/rod and sling-rubber contact motion mechanically; speed, force and animation are table tuning. All six relationships are explicitly pulse relationships, not inferences from proximity.

The single passive spinner 14/ASE-2250-68 supplies one closure per revolution and a one-way gate. It begins at 10 points, rises to 100 after 10 revolutions and 1000 after 20. A warning sound precedes decay; spinning during that warning retains the tier. Standups 5/25 and rollover 15 are distinct contacts. Two passive upper-arch gates and the two passive flipper-feed gates have no CPU outputs; preserve their one-way paths and table-owned stop/friction behavior. The cabinet flipper buttons and physical dual-winding/EOS circuits are gated by K1 relay 19; EOS does not become another game-matrix input.

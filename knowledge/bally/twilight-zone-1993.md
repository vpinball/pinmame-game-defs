# Twilight Zone (Bally, 1993)

Coverage: **partial - complete physical I/O inventory, WPC-Fliptronic bindings, semantic naming, mechanism causality, 32-driver variant boundary, and recreation behavior validated, with no conflicts; one physical device, the mixed G.I. string 02 ("Mini-playfield & Insert"), has no defensible spatial placement**

## Identity and evidence precedence

This is the Bally physical product released April 1993, IPDB 2684. It covers the `tz_*` clone tree rooted at `tz_92`: 32 driver rows spanning Bally production revisions (9.2 through L-5, H-7/H-8, I-7/I-8, D-1 through D-4, IFPA tournament revisions, the LA-9 PAPA tournament build, five prototype revisions P-3/P-3D/P-4/P-5/PA-1/PA-2) and seven FreeWPC community-firmware revisions. Every one of these is a firmware revision for the same physical machine; none is a virtual-only or unrelated driver.

Evidence precedence for this definition: the retained known-working VPX script ("Twilight Zone (Bally 1993) 2.4.5.vpx", by Skitso, based on Ninuzzu's original) is runtime and mechanism-causality ground truth; the Bally operations manual (16-50020-101) controls physical construction, part numbers, polarity, quantities, and device presence; pinned PinMAME controls controller generation, public address topology, and mechanism-table position ranges; the retained VPX geometry supplies normalized coordinates. Two scans of the manual are cited: the Internet Archive scan, image-only, whose tables were transcribed into `external:pinmame-review-artifacts/twilight-zone-1993/manual-transcription.md`, and the complete 164-page copy from IPDB machine 2684 (with Manual Amendment 16-50020-AMD-1), whose pages used here are committed as excerpts. A second table, ninuzzu's January 2020 VPX recreation, is the ancestor of the 2.4.5 lineage and so not an independent table; it supplies the objects the 2.4.5 table dropped (the second sockets of four flasher circuits and the switch walls of 45/46), normalized by its own 1093 x 2162 bounds. Sixty same-named switch, kicker, bumper, gate and spinner objects of the two tables agree to a median 0.004 normalized.

**Retained-scan limitation.** The Internet Archive scan carries only odd-numbered printed pages (for example PDF 40 = 2-9, 58 = 2-45, 64 = 2-57), so every even page is absent, including the Switch Matrix wiring page (2-50, which also carries the first half of the Switch Locations table, items 1-33: dedicated switches 1-8 and matrix 11-33), the Solenoid/Flasher Table wiring page (2-52), and the Lamp Matrix wiring page (2-54). The complete IPDB copy carries all of them. This definition uses it for the clock drives, the knocker's backbox wiring, the G.I. strings' playfield/backbox split, the flasher socket counts, the mini-playfield switch drawing, the switch-matrix column and row wiring of every used matrix switch, including the clock optos' column 9, and the part numbers of switches 11-33 (page 2-50); the lamp matrix and the other outputs' wire colours and connector pins have not been transferred yet. Page 2-50 prints matrix position 24 "Always Closed" (switch list part "----"): it is recorded as a constant, always-closed reference contact, which pinned `wpc.c` also closes at machine init, so a host never drives it. It prints item 27 "Ball Shooter", which now labels switch 27; the retained script's "Shooter Lane" is kept as an alias. Manual Amendment 16-50020-AMD-1 changes switch 61's part from the printed 5647-12693-32 to 5647-12693-57, the Ball Popper Assembly's optos (page 2-25, switch 74, Gumball Popper) from A-14231/A-14232 to A-16908/A-16909, and names switch 57's part A-16535 "Ramp Prox Opto sensor Assembly" (page 2-47, item 12a); the definition records all three, and switch 57 is typed as a proximity sensor (`switch_type` other). Its page 2-20 entry changes the flipper end-of-stroke switch part to SW-1A-194, a part the definition does not record.

## Controller platform and address topology

`GEN_WPCFLIPTRON` (`PINMAME_HARDWARE_GEN_WPCFLIPTRON = 0x8`, pinned PinMAME's own comment: "Fliptronic flippers, Addams Family 2/92 - Twilight Zone 5/93") with `wpc_dispDMD`. The controller profile is the new `pinmame.wpc-fliptronic`, introduced by this definition for every later pre-WPC-95 Fliptronic-generation game to reuse.

- Switches: dedicated coin-door 1-8, matrix 11-88 as drive column then return row, one custom switch column at 121-128 (`CORE_CUSTSWCOL`, internal column 12, here the eight clock-position optos, which the manual prints as a "9th column" numbered 91-98), Fliptronic 111-118. Twilight Zone genuinely has **four** flippers (`FLIP_SW(FLIP_L|FLIP_U)|FLIP_SOL(FLIP_L|FLIP_U)`), confirmed by the manual's printed "Flipper Coils" list (FL-15411 orange lower left/right, FL-11753 yellow upper left, FL-11722 green upper right) -- unlike Monster Bash, the upper Fliptronic circuits here are real hardware, not repurposed.
- Solenoids: standard drivers 1-16 fitted (10-14 handled by native VPX slingshot/bumper physics, not scripted), 17-20 flashers, 21/23-27 magnets/motor/diverter (22 not fitted), 28 flasher, Fliptronic upper-flipper circuits 33-36, Fliptronic lower-flipper circuits 45-48, custom board outputs 51-58 (five flashers 51-55 plus the clock motor pair 56/57 and clock strobe 58), and a software-only fake gumball-release state at 59. PinMAME state channels 29-32; simulator-only 49 and reserved 50.
- Lamps: full 8x8 matrix 11-88, every position fitted (no gaps).
- GI: five strings on public addresses 0-4.

**This generation has no LPDC board.** Unlike WPC-95, addresses 37-44 are not a duplicated general-purpose output range here: pinned PinMAME's `core_getSol` dispatch only serves that branch for `GEN_WPC95`/`GEN_WPC95DCS`/`GEN_ALLS11`, and Twilight Zone's own `tz_getSol` hook does not claim any part of it either, so 37-44 are simply unused address space on this machine. The manual's printed auxiliary-board callout numbers 37-44 for the custom solenoid board are a *different* numbering scheme from the public PinMAME address space for the same physical outputs -- see below.

**Printed auxiliary-board item numbers are not public addresses.** The manual's Solenoid/Flasher Locations page numbers the custom board's outputs 37-44 (matching the board's own silkscreen), but PinMAME publishes the same eight physical outputs at 51-58. The retained script's own comments bridge the two schemes explicitly: `SolModCallback(51)` through `(55)` are annotated `'(37)` through `'(41)`, and the commented-out clock lines read `'(42) Clock Reverse'`, `'(43) Clock Forward'`, `'(44) Clock Switch Strobe'` against `SolCallback(56)`/`(57)`/`(58)`. The printed number is preserved as a `manual.address` alias on each device; the binding uses the true public address.

## Ball path, trough, and shooter

Twilight Zone has no manual plunger. A ball drains through the Outhole (18) into the trough, walking Far Left Trough (25) -> Left Trough (17) -> Center Trough (16) -> Right Trough (15) at the eject end. Solenoid 9 (Ball Release) ejects the ball on switch 15 into the shooter lane, where it rests on Auto-Fire Kicker opto 72; solenoid 3 fires it onto the playfield. Switch 26 (Trough Proximity) detects the Powerball by type rather than presence. The retained script sets and clears it from the ball resting in the right-trough eject kicker (`sw15_hit`/`sw15_unhit`), and the manual's Main Playfield Switch Locations drawing prints callout 26 beside 15 at the trough's eject end, so its spatial record is a documented projection onto that eject position.

## Gumball machine

A ball diverted by solenoid 6 (Gumball Diverter) enters the gumball lane (switch 51, Gumball Popper Lane) and is caught by the Gumball Popper opto (switch 74), which solenoid 4 kicks into the gumball wheel. A real gumball then enters the delivery chute (switch 87, Gumball Enter opto) and travels to the exit (switch 56, Gumball Exit) where the player receives it. Solenoid 24 turns the internal gumball motor. Switch 55 (Gumball Geneva) senses the motor's geneva-gear position, but pinned PinMAME's `tz_handleMech` derives it synthetically from an internal position counter (`locals.gumPos`), and the retained table has no switch object for it. The table pulses it from script instead: `SolGumballMotor` schedules `SolGumRelease` 1.7 s after solenoid 24 turns on, and `SolGumRelease` calls `vpmtimer.PulseSw 55` (script.vbs line 2401). The switch sits on the underside beneath the gumball machine (manual dagger footnote; callout 55 is printed inside the gumball-machine outline), so it and the gumball motor (solenoid 24, callout 24 in the same outline) are projected onto the gumball machine assembly, represented by the pivot of the table's gumball-globe primitive. `tz_getSol` also exposes a fake `CORE_CUSTSOLNO(9)` "gumball release" state (public 59) used only to simplify emulator-side sequencing; the retained script comments it out as "unreliable with SolModCallbacks", so it is declared `virtual`, not a real coil.

## Clock mechanism

A bidirectional DC gearmotor drives a physical analog clock hand. The clock test text on page 1-18 names the pieces: the clock "is operated through a D.C. Motor Control Board, pt #A-16120, (located UNDER the playfield at approximately the same position as the clock) and solenoid drives 42 and 43 (located on the 8-Driver Board in the backbox). With only drive 43 turned ON, the clock moves forward. With only drive 42 turned ON, the clock moves in reverse. With both drives ON, or both drives OFF, the clock is stopped." Drives 42 and 43 are the 8-Driver Board's own numbers for public solenoids 56 and 57, so public 56 is **Clock Reverse** and public 57 **Clock Forward**, as the Solenoid/Flasher Table (2-52) and the Solenoid/Flasher Locations page (2-53) also print. The position optos are strobed by solenoid 58 (A-16100, Clock Switch Strobe). Eight opto sensors report hand position: Minute board (A-16220) at public 121-124, Hour board (A-16219) at public 125-128. Pages 1-18 and 2-50 print them as a "9th column" of the switch matrix, numbered 91-98, but `tz.c:175-182` defines them as `CORE_CUSTSWNO(1,1..8)`, and `core.h:347` places that first custom column at 121-128 (`CORE_CUSTSWCOL = CORE_STDSWCOLS = 12`); `tz_swRowRead` (`tz.c:586-589`) returns that internal column when the 9th column is enabled, and `tzGameData`'s 0xff `Cust` mask entry inverts it. A recreation drives 121-128; a write to 91-98 lands in a column the ROM never reads. The printed numbers are kept as `manual.address` aliases, as on Indiana Jones and Star Trek: The Next Generation. Column 9 is the Gray-White wire from J5-1 of the 8-Driver Board A-16100, driven by its Q1 and Q12 (page 1-18; page 2-50 prints `*J5-1`, and page 2-52 prints the same Gry-Wht `*J5-1` on the Clock Switch Strobe, public 58); the rows are the normal White row wires J208.

The ROM's own clock test agrees (T.16 on the 9.2 ROM, which inserts T.14 LAMP ROW/COLUMN and T.15 DIP SW. TEST before it; `evidence/runtime/wpc-fliptronic/twilight-zone-clock-test.json`). While the test reports CLOCK STOPPED, the ROM holds both 56 and 57 on. Each operation is measured over the 3.94 s from the snapshot before its start press to the snapshot after its stop press (the two presses are 3.47 s apart). While it runs CLOCK FWD. SLOW it keeps 57 on and drops 56 ten times, 0.17 s in all; FWD. FAST drops 56 23 times, 0.55 s in all. CLOCK REV. SLOW keeps 56 on and drops 57 eleven times, 0.22 s in all; REV. FAST drops 57 23 times, 0.61 s in all. So the intervals with one drive alone are 57-only going forward and 56-only in reverse, and the fast modes spend more time on one drive. The run had the clock model off, so it shows which public output the ROM drives for each named operation, not which way a physical motor turns; a recreation that runs the clock from the two public outputs moves it forward while only 57 is on, in reverse while only 56 is on, and holds it otherwise.

Pinned PinMAME models the clock itself. `tz.c:601-616` declares `mechClock = {sClockRev, sClockFwd, MECH_TWODIRSOL|MECH_FAST, ...}` with its own table of hour and minute opto ranges, and `init_tz` registers it with `mech_add(0, &mechClock)` (`tz.c:624`); it runs whenever mechanics handling bit 0 is on (`mech.c:88-107`), which the retained table sets (`Controller.HandleMechanics = 1`) before reading the model's position through `Controller.GetMech(0)` to turn its hands. The `#if 0` block in `tz_handleMech` (`tz.c:638-661`) is a separate, older tick model and is disabled. `mech.c:140-145` reads `sol1 = sClockRev` (public 57) and `sol2 = sClockFwd` (public 56) and sets `dir = (sol==1)-(sol==2)`, so the live model runs forward on 57 alone and back on 56 alone, as the manual and the ROM say. Only `tz.c`'s `#define` names (`sClockFwd` = 56, `sClockRev` = 57, `tz.c:210-211`) read backwards, a PinMAME naming defect rather than a disagreement about the machine; the former `conflict.clock-motor-direction-naming` was withdrawn on 2026-09-26 for that reason.

A second run of the same clock test with that model on (`evidence/runtime/wpc-fliptronic/twilight-zone-clock-test-mech.json`, `--handle-mechanics 1`) shows it. The ROM holds the same outputs as in the model-off run, and its display now tracks the modelled clock: 0:00 at 12 HOUR on opening, 0:15 after CLOCK FWD. SLOW, 0:30 and then 1:00 at 1 HOUR during CLOCK FWD. FAST, still 1:00 after CLOCK REV. SLOW, and 12:45 (still 1 HOUR), then 12:30, 12:15 and 12:00 at 12 HOUR during CLOCK REV. FAST, with the minute boxes 00/15/30/45 and the four hour-opto boxes changing as the time does. The frames show a model moving the way the ROM's operation names say for the outputs it holds, not a physical clock.

## Magnets

Three eddy-current magnets: Left Magnet (solenoid 21, switch 83), Lower Right Magnet (solenoid 23, switch 81), and Upper Right Magnet (solenoid 22, switch 82). The third magnet is **not fitted** on this physical machine: both its solenoid and switch print "Not Used" with no part number at all in the manual, the strongest "not fitted" signature the manual uses anywhere. The retained script's own comment on the ROM's `SolUpperRightMagnet` callback reads `(*) only in prototype, supported by rom 9.4` -- the ROM can drive a third magnet, but no manual/schematic evidence of a production variant that actually carries it was found. `tz_inportData`'s "Third Magnet" toggle belongs to PinMAME's own internal text-mode ball-tracking simulator (`sim.c`), not a documented physical DIP option, and must not be read as evidence of a fitted variant.

## Mini-playfield

A raised second playfield reached through the Mini-Playfield Enter opto (switch 44). Two eddy-current magnets (solenoid 25 Left, solenoid 26 Right) manipulate the ball around the Camera/Mini-Playfield Top Hole (switch 42 -- the retained script's own comment calls it "camera / upper playfield", and the manual's plain label is preferred here as the physical construction authority), the Player Piano standup target (switch 43), and the Mini-Playfield Top/Exit optos (switches 75/76). Switches 45/46 (Mini-Playfield Left/Right) are two contacts each: page 2-51 prints "Mini-playfield Left (2)" and "Mini-playfield Right (2)", and the page 2-50 drawing "MINI-PLAYFIELD, TOP AND BOTTOM RAMP SWITCH LOCATIONS" draws one balloon of each on its own side rail and one on the bottom rail. The 2020 ninuzzu table models them as invisible hit walls `sw45`/`sw45a` and `sw46`/`sw46a`, whose handlers pulse 45 and 46; each switch is placed on its two walls. The retained 2.4.5 script keeps the same handlers, but its extraction has no such objects, so that table never asserts either switch. The drawing is a perspective detail with remote callout balloons, so it corroborates the count, the side and the order of the contacts, not their coordinates, which come from the 2020 table: through an affine fit on six validated mini-playfield switches (leave-one-out RMS 0.066) the four balloons land 0.024 to 0.047 from the walls. The pinned fits pass a placement at an offset no larger than the fit's largest leave-one-out error and under 0.07; outlying controls inflate the first bound (callout 18 at 0.067 on the overlay fit, balloon 53 at 0.112 on the 2-50 fit), so the 0.07 cap, the bar the Indianapolis 500 record uses, is what decides.

## Three-position ball lock

Lower (switch 88, plain switch part), Center (switch 84, opto), and Upper (switch 85, opto) lock positions; solenoid 15 (Lock Release) kicks locked balls back into play.

## Slot machine, jet bumpers, and slingshots

A ball entering the slot machine crosses proximity switch 57 (underside of the playfield) and rests on kickout opto 58; solenoid 1 returns it to play. Three A-9415-2 jet bumpers and two slingshots (Left/Right, switches 34/35, solenoids 11/10) are handled by native VPX physics in the retained script rather than scripted `SolCallback` entries. The manual's location drawings put switch 31 and coil 13 on the left bumper, switch 32 and coil 14 on the upper-right bumper, and switch 33 and coil 12 on the lower bumper, and the retained script binds `Bumper1`/`Bumper2`/`Bumper3` (left/upper-right/lower) to switches 31/32/33 in the same order. `tz.c` names the switches only `swJet1`-`swJet3`; an earlier revision of this definition labelled 31-33 Lower/Left/Right by analogy with the coil order, which no source supports. Which coil the ROM fires for which switch is not asserted beyond this co-location. The retained script's slingshot handlers also pulse switches 42 and 41 besides 34 and 35, a defect in the consumed table rather than a machine behavior.

**A printed label correction.** `tz.c` unambiguously defines `#define swLSling 34` and `#define swRSling 35` (34 = Left, 35 = Right); the legacy migrated stub this definition replaces had these reversed. The manual's own Switch Locations page (item 34 = "Left Slingshot", item 35 = "Right Slingshot") and the retained table's `Wall.LeftSlingShot`/`Wall.RightSlingShot` geometry (x=0.296 and x=0.676 respectively, x=0 being the left edge) both independently confirm `tz.c` is correct.

## Flippers

Two lower Fliptronic flippers (FL-15411 orange, solenoids 45-48) and two genuine upper Fliptronic flippers (FL-11753 yellow upper-left solenoids 35/36, FL-11722 green upper-right solenoids 33/34), confirmed by the manual's printed Flipper Coils list. `Const UseSolenoids = 2` in the retained script means the ROM drives the coils directly (fast flips). `WPC_FLIPPERS` unconditionally complements the flipper switch column for this hardware generation exactly as `WPC_FLIPPERSW95` does for WPC-95, so the public state of 111-118 is already normalized.

## Driver variants

All 32 catalog rows trace `tz_92` and share `tzGameData`; none is virtual-only. `tz_94ch`/`tz_94h` are the pair the retained script's `cGameName` selects between (Romset 0/1); `tz_l2` is the revision both retained VPX script corpora (`vpxtable_scripts` and `vpx-standalone-scripts`) independently target. FreeWPC revisions `tz_f10` through `tz_f100` are community firmware for the same physical hardware. No edition/family split applies -- Twilight Zone shipped as a single physical configuration, not Pro/Premium/LE variants.

## Spatial state

A `not_applicable` spatial record is used only for a device with no playfield position: the coin-door, cabinet-button, and backbox devices the manual places off the playfield (dedicated switches 1-8, start/tilt/slam/coin-door/buy-in switches, the four Fliptronic button inputs, buy-in and credit button lamps 87/88, the knocker, GI 3 "Insert Main"), unused addresses, PinMAME state channels, DIP switches, the clock strobe (public 58), a control-signal line with no device of its own, and the four flipper end-of-stroke contacts, which follow the repository-wide convention for an internal part of the flipper assembly (the flipper position is carried by the flipper coils). Every other physical device is either placed on a retained table's own object for it, or on a documented projection onto its own mechanism (trough, bumpers, slingshots, flippers, lock, slot kickout, gumball machine, clock), with the reason recorded on the device. The right ramp diverter (solenoid 5) is placed on the pivot of its blade primitive `BM_RDiv`, which the script rotates, not on the invisible `DivTrig`/`DivWall` helpers; the page 2-53 callout-05 arrow ends 0.016 from that pivot. The clock drives 56/57 are projected onto the clock axis because the gearmotor is mounted on the clock (page 1-51); their control board A-16120 sits under the playfield near the clock (page 1-18).

Lamps 11-18 and 21-28 carry the printed suffix "(Door)". That names the playfield's central door-panel insert group, not the cabinet coin door: the Lamp Locations drawing prints those callouts on the door panel in the middle of the playfield, and the retained script maps Light objects l11-l28 to them. The drawing prints one of that group's callouts as "31" where the table lists 21, a misprint recorded in the committed drawing excerpt.

The knocker (solenoid 7) is backbox hardware: page 2-52 prints its connections J130-8 and J107-3 and its coil under the Backbox columns, and the Backbox Assembly page 2-4 lists the B-10686-1 Knocker & Bracket Assy. inside the backbox. Page 2-52's column headings do not match its cells on any fitted coil row 01-28, this one included: each row prints its power driver board drive connection (a J130, J127, J125 or J124 pin, which the connector list on page 3-33 names as that solenoid's drive) under Voltage Connections and its J107 or J109 supply pin under Drive Connections, just as the G.I. rows print return and feed the other way round. So J130-8 is the knocker's drive connection and J107-3 its supply. That list calls J130-8 "Sol 7 to playfield coil", the same generic wording it uses for every J130 pin.

Flasher circuits 18, 19, 20 and public 55 (item 41) each have two sockets. The first socket keeps the 2.4.5 table's script-bound Light (f18, f19, f20, f41); the second is the 2020 table's Light for the socket the 2.4.5 table dropped: f18c right of centre on the main playfield, f19a near the top of the mini-playfield, and f20c and f41c under the door panel's Gum and Ball inserts (of each co-located pair, the Light with the smaller falloff). Least-squares affine fits of the page 2-53 drawings on already-validated placements put the new sockets 0.005 to 0.040 from their fitted callouts (leave-one-out RMS 0.019 for the main-playfield drawing and 0.034 for the ramp and mini-playfield overlay, which f19a's 0.040 exceeds; retained in `vpx-geometry-2026-09-26.txt`). The pinned fits pass a placement at an offset no larger than the fit's largest leave-one-out error and under 0.07; outlying controls inflate the first bound (callout 18 at 0.067 on the overlay fit, balloon 53 at 0.112 on the 2-50 fit), so the 0.07 cap, the bar the Indianapolis 500 record uses, is what decides. The drawings corroborate the count and where each socket sits; the coordinates come from the tables. Both scripts carry the comment "(**) - the additional GUM and BALL flashers were removed ro reduce cost" [sic] against circuits 20 and 41; that is an unsourced claim about later production, and the manual prints two sockets.

G.I. string 03 ("Clock & Insert", public G.I. 2) is placed as two co-located projections onto the clock axis: the Clock Assembly A-16124 (pages 2-32/2-33) carries two sockets on its mounting bracket, one bulb with a red sleeve and one with a yellow sleeve. Page 2-33 draws the two sockets side by side on bracket 24, about an inch apart, inside the clock housing's footprint; the co-located projection hides that offset, and because the axis is not a bulb object both placements are recorded as observed, not validated. They are, with the unplaced G.I. string 02, the spatial records that are not validated. Its insert-board bulbs are backbox hardware. The 2020 table's GIClock collection, dimmed from G.I. string index 2, holds four glow sprites at two positions beside the clock, which corroborates two emitters there but gives no socket positions. G.I. string 04 ("Insert Main", public G.I. 3) is wired to the backbox only.

G.I. string 02 ("Mini-playfield & Insert", public G.I. 1) is the one physical device with no spatial record. Its wiring reaches both the playfield and the backbox insert (page 2-52; page 3-33). The Mini-Playfield Assembly A-16806 (pages 2-40/2-41) shows a #555 socket (item 10, yellow sleeve item 49) in the Street Light and item 10's leader to a second socket in its back view, with no printed quantity, and no page enumerates the string's bulbs. The 2.4.5 script binds a single light (l101), and the 2020 table's GIMinipf collection includes Light20 and Light43, which sit on the main-playfield jet bumpers, so its membership is an author's choice rather than a socket list. A continuity or bulb survey of G.I. string 02 on a physical machine would settle it.

The 2026-09-25 spatial repair also corrected placements that had been recorded as validated on the wrong object: switch 47, switch 74, solenoid 15, solenoid 24, lower-flipper coils 45 and 48, and the clock outputs 56-58.

## Promotion decision

Identity, controller platform, address enumeration, semantic naming, mechanism inventory/behavior, variant coverage, and recreation knowledge are complete, and the definition carries no conflicts. Promotion to `author_ready` is refused only because G.I. string 02 has no defensible playfield placement (see Spatial state), so `coverage.missing` is `["spatial_placement"]`. See `reports/spatial/bally/twilight-zone-1993.md` for the complete spatial audit.

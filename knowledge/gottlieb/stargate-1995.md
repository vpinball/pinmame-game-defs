# Stargate (Gottlieb, 1995)

Coverage: **partial - every controller address enumerated and named from the ROM's own service tests, mechanisms,
variants and recreation behaviour recorded; two requirements stay open: polarity (no source shows the active level of
the glider's GLIDER LEFT switch, 20) and spatial placement (every playfield position comes from one community table,
with no factory drawing and no independent table to check it).**

The definition is generated by `tools/curate_stargate.py`; the spatial review is `reports/spatial/gottlieb/stargate-1995.md`
and the runtime evidence is derived by `tools/stargate_runtime_evidence.py` from harness runs the scenarios in
`tools/harness-scenarios/gts3/` drive. The platform is described once in `controllers/pinmame/gts3.json`.

## Identity and variants

Stargate is Premier Technology's model 742, sold under the Gottlieb trade name from March 1995 (IPDB 2847, OPDB
`G50pv-MdE6R`; 3,600 units). It runs on Gottlieb System 3 with a 128x32 dot-matrix display. IPDB lists an English and a
Spanish operations manual but marks both "Availability limited by copyright" and hosts neither, and no other
attributable copy was found, so no manual, schematic or location drawing is retained. Everything a manual would normally
settle about names comes instead from the ROM's own service tests, which name every switch, lamp, coil and auxiliary
driver.

All six pinned drivers are declared with the same `INITGAME2(<set>, DMD, FLIP8182, 4, SNDBRD_GTS3, 5)` line and play the
same sound ROMs:

| Driver | Firmware | Compatibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `stargat5` | Revision 5 game ROM with the rev. 3 display ROM; boot screen Game #742/5; the driver both retained tables load | identical |
| `stargat4` | Revision 4 game ROM ("fixes at least the 'Beeping and Garbled DMD' issue") with the rev. 3 display ROM | identical |
| `stargat3`, `stargat2`, `stargat1` | Revisions 3, 2 and 1 | identical |
| `stargate` | The clone tree's root, catalogued without a revision number | identical |

Every set's Lamp Matrix, Relay & Solenoid, Aux Driver and Switch Edges tests name every address exactly as `stargat5`'s
do (the `comparison` note in each `evidence/runtime/gts3/stargate-<set>-service-tests.json`), so the revisions differ in
rules and display code only.

The IPDB page also pictures a prototype whitewood with a recessed lower playfield and borrowed Rescue 911 and Street
Fighter II parts that had no ROMs of its own; it is not the production machine and no driver corresponds to it.

## Controller facts that matter to a recreation

- **Numbering.** Switches and lamps use Gottlieb's decimal numbers: tens digit the strobe, units digit the return or row,
  units 0-7 only. The Lamp Matrix and Switch Edges tests write 100-117 as A0-B7. Lamp numbers start at 0.
- **Flipper buttons.** Drive the left button at public 143 and the right at 141 (PinMAME's flipper column for System 3).
  `core_updateSw` copies them into the matrix switches the ROM reads, 81 (LEFT FLIPPER) and 82 (RIGHT FLIPPER), on every
  update, so a write to 81 or 82 is overwritten. While the GAME OVER RELAY (32) is energized the presses also publish the
  synthetic flipper outputs 45/46 (right) and 47/48 (left). There are three flippers; the upper right one is on the
  right button.
- **Cabinet test inputs.** -8 is the Test button, -7 Tilt, -6 Slam Tilt. -5 tells the ROM whether the optional
  communications adapter (printer) is present; leave it at 0.
- **Cabinet switches in the matrix.** 0-3 are coin chutes 1-4, 4 the credit (Start) button, 5 the Tournament button inside
  the coin door, 6 the coin-door switch, closed (1) while the door is shut. The game plays with 6 at either level. Two
  coins on chute 1 make a credit at factory settings.
- **Polarity.** No inverted-switch mask exists and the CPU reads the complement of the matrix, so public 1 is a closed
  contact. Every switch whose active level the ROM's behaviour or the known-working script shows reads 1 when actuated,
  so its contact rests open (`normally_closed: false`), optos included. GLIDER LEFT (MOTOR) (20) is the exception left
  open: no table writes it and no run exercised it.
- **Outputs.** Solenoids 1-32 are the ROM's drivers 0-31 plus one. 21 and 25 are not used. 26 is the lightbox (backbox)
  illumination relay A, 31 the tilt relay T that switches the playfield G.I. (G.I. off while energized), and 32 the game-over
  relay Q. 22 drives the backbox rope lights around Ra. There is no G.I. lamp channel.
- **Auxiliary flashers.** `hw.lampCol = 5` adds eight auxiliary-board outputs at lamps 120-127: six playfield flashers
  (REBELLION, TOP LEFT, LEFT STARGATE, RIGHT STARGATE, TOP RIGHT, BOTTOM RIGHT) and two lightbox flashers (RA'S EYES,
  WHITE RAILS).
- **CPU-fired coils.** The pop bumpers, kicking rubbers and kicking targets (coils 1-7) are fired by the CPU from their
  switches (10-16); a consumer drives only the switch. PinMAME smooths System 3 solenoids over four frames, so a short coil
  pulse can be missing from the binary solenoid stream; in the gameplay run pulses of 11 and 12 never showed 2 or 3, which
  that smoothing could explain.

## Mechanisms and behaviour

- **Ball handling.** Four balls: three in the trough and one resting in the outhole, which is how the VPW table starts
  (24 and 34 closed). A drained ball closes the outhole switch (24) and the ROM kicks it into the trough with the Outhole
  coil (29), about every 0.7 s while 24 stays closed. When a ball is served the ROM resets the drop targets (17, 18) and
  trips the rollover target (20) about 1.4 s after the trough switch (34) rises, and fires Ball Release (28) about 2.6 s
  after it. A game started with the outhole empty kicked the one ball the host placed there and then showed "WARNING! Ball
  missing or stuck / Place 1 ball in outhole" until a second ball arrived (run gameplay). The Shooter Lane Kicker (9) is
  the shooter lane's (31) auto-launch.
- **Pyramid and Glidercraft.** The pyramid's top opens while TOP PYRAMID (16) is energized; TOP PYRAMID -SENSOR (102) reports
  the top open (the VPW table writes it half a second after 16 changes) and TOP PYRAMID -OPTO (91) sees a ball enter. The
  Glidercraft extends from the open pyramid and swings left and right through about 90 degrees: GLIDER MOTOR -FORWARD (24)
  moves it out and back, GLIDER MOTOR (L & R) (23) swings it. The ROM names three glider switches: GLIDER MOTOR STOP (21,
  closed when the glider is fully retracted in the VPW model), GLIDER RIGHT (MOTOR) (30, closed at the right end of the
  swing) and GLIDER LEFT (MOTOR) (20). Neither retained table ever closes 20 and both play, so a recreation that answers
  21 and 30 satisfies the ROM; what 20 senses on the real glider drive is not settled by any retained source. The ROM opens
  the pyramid before it moves the glider (its coil test energizes 16 about 0.2-0.3 s before either glider motor and holds it
  about 0.25 s after) and runs the pyramid and the swing together for about 7 s in attract mode.
- **Horus guardians.** Two Horus figures on pivots normally block two shots; LEFT and RIGHT PIVOT TARGET (14, 15) raise them
  while energized, lifting their targets (116, 117) into the air out of the shot. The ROM raises and lowers both together
  every couple of seconds during play.
- **Drop targets.** A left 3-bank (17, 27, 37; reset 17), a centre 2-bank (26, 36; reset 18) and a single right rollover
  drop target (35) with both a reset (19) and a trip (20) coil. The ROM reset both banks and tripped the rollover target
  when it served a ball. A completed bank held down for six seconds drew no reset, but only before the game's first ball
  had been served, so when the ROM resets a completed bank during play is not shown.
- **Kickers.** Three up-kickers at the top (80, an opto; 23; 33 with coils 10, 11, 12) and a lower-left kicker (25, coil 8)
  with a ball gate (13) whose position BALL GATE -SENSOR (101) reports. With 25 closed the ROM opened the gate about 0.5 s
  later, kicked about 0.5 s after that and closed the gate again; 23 held for 1.2 s drew two kicks of 11 (run gameplay).
- **Bullseye.** Switches 22 (inner) and 32 (outer) are two contacts of one target.

## Service menu

Test (-8) once shows TEST MODE (L FLIPPER TO STEP, R FLIPPER TO SELECT); once more shows the menu BOOKKEEPING, GAME
ADJUSTMENTS, SELF-TEST, UTILITIES with SELF-TEST highlighted. The left flipper steps, the right flipper selects, Test steps
from one self-test to the next (it is ignored while SELF-TEST 1 MEMORY TEST runs). The self-tests are 1 Memory, 2 Lamp
Check, 3 Lamp Matrix, 4 Relay & Solenoid (the credit button fires the shown driver), 5 Inoperative Switches (then a live
switch-matrix display), 6 Switch Edges, 7 Display, 8 Sound, 9 Front Door (coin chute counters) and 10 Aux Driver. A first
power-up from empty NVRAM shows REPLAY AT 00 and accepts no credit; GAME ADJUSTMENTS answers CREDIT BUTTON LOADS FACTORY
SETTINGS and then CREDIT BUTTON LOADS ENGLISH. The Tournament button in attract mode opens TOURNAMENT MODE (FREE PLAY,
GAME FEATURES, SPECIAL/REPLAY and EXTRA BALL settings) and asks to CLOSE DOOR TO BEGIN PLAY when 6 is open.

## Retained tables

- **VPW v2.0** (VPin Workshop, 2025, rebuilt from the 32Assassin/JLouLoulou release) is the binding and geometry authority.
  Its standup-target helper pulses `switch mod 100`, so a hit on a Horus target pulses 16 or 17 instead of 116 or 117; it
  holds the Tournament (5) and coin-door (6) switches closed from three seconds after start as a free-play customization;
  and it leaves the pop bumper, kicking rubber and kicking target coils unbound, kicking with table physics.
- **v1.3.0** (32Assassin and JLouLoulou, "made from real Stargate scan") pulses 116 and 117 correctly and models the trough
  as one four-ball stack reporting 34. It shares ancestry with VPW, so it corroborates rather than confirms.

## What would complete the record

The Stargate operations manual (IPDB lists it) would supply the switch, lamp and coil location drawings to check the
table placements against, the wiring of every matrix position and driver, the bulb types and counts, and what the
GLIDER LEFT (MOTOR) switch (20) senses and how it is wired. A harness run that drives the glider through a full swing with
the ROM in play, closing 20 and 30 at the ends, could show which level the ROM treats as the left end. A second table built independently from the machine could also validate the
placements.

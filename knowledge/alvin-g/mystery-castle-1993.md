## Identity and retained evidence

This is AG08, the physical two-ball **Mystery Castle** also titled **Mystery Castle of Doom** in its operations manual MAN-019. The verified [IPDB machine 1647](https://www.ipdb.org/machine.cgi?id=1647), manual copyright and pinned OPDB identity `G5vZZ-MQNNo` agree on Alvin G and 1993. The exact retained table is agentEighty6's v2.1.0, March 2020, SHA-256 `cf9a40e430a490428ca989a10fac4ac01aeac6cd8783e6da3d6c74f56f32cda5`. Its active ROM is `mystcasa` and its header credits arngrim's playtesting, gtxjoe's earlier table/reference photographs and djrobx's Alvin G emulator fixes. The table requires a sufficiently recent VPinMAME for reliable VUK/shooter firing; the pinned native build exceeds that historical minimum.

The [retained manual](https://www.ipdb.org/files/1647/1993_Mystery_Castle_Manual.pdf) is 75 scanned pages, SHA-256 `9fa91108ba60a181218c06420267a2f950109716c665385ac9b62daee329face`. All pages were rendered at native resolution and OCRed with Windows.Media.Ocr; the curator visually checked the cited matrices, parts and wiring pages. OCR is a search aid. Complete factual transcriptions and compact drawing crops are under `evidence/excerpts/alvin-g.mystery-castle.1993/`; neither the whole manual nor the table is redistributed.

Pinned PinMAME `97aa922bf8e4b6970126192ec1ac1fb0305a4f62` has exactly two drivers: root `mystcast` R02 (1993), and clone `mystcasa` R03 (firmware year `199?`). Both use `INITGAME2(... DMD, FLIP78, 3/*?*/, SNDBRD_ALVGS2, SNDBRD_ALVGDMD, 2)`, generation `GEN_ALVG_DMD2`, and the same 128×32 DMD layout. The final macro argument is `gameSpecific1=2`; `swCol=4`, `lampCol=4` and `custSol=0` are hard-coded earlier fields. R03 changes CPU/sound-program/DMD images and DMD ROM packaging, while preserving the physical controller and display layout. It is a firmware variant of this cabinet. Manual 1-3 explicitly supplies and installs **two balls**, agreeing with `bsTrough.Balls=2`; the driver's speculative `3/*?*/` simulator parameter is not a third physical ball.

## Controller-facing address contract

The definition enumerates every printed switch 1–96, lamp 1–96, direct latch -7/-6/-5, flipper-column position 113–120 and public solenoid slot 1–50. Blank matrix/coil rows are kept. Actual non-fitment comes from the machine's own DOC-003/DOC-004 wiring and DOC-002 load diagram, never from a missing callback or a quiet trace.

The `pinmame.alvin-g` profile derives `alvg_sw2m(n)=n+7` and `alvg_m2sw(c,r)=8*c+r-7`. Physical strobe HP4 forms each eight-switch block; HP5 is the eight return lines, despite the matrix drawing's row/column wording. The CPU reads `swMatrix[swCol]^0xff` and the game declares no inverted-switch mask. DOC-003 draws every fitted matrix contact normally open, so all fitted matrix switches use public 1 for closure. Do not invert the public state again.

PinMAME copies cabinet flipper buttons **114 right** and **116 left** into matrix switches **8** and **7** on every update. Drive 114/116, never the matrix copies. No EOS contact is in that matrix pair. Only those lower-button bits are used in column 15; the other six positions have no fitted independent control. The retained `alvinG.vbs` lower-button constants agree. Its misleading upper constants 115/117 are not this game's hardware and do not override the core conversion.

For direct service inputs use **-7 Enter / Begin Test** and **-6 Exit / Service Credits**. A fresh R03 service run with keyboard handling off confirmed -7 entering `MAIN MENU A ADJUSTMENTS` and -6 returning to the `EPC081R03` attract title. The active `CoinDoorSwitches_Read` implementation differs from its disabled manual-order block: -7 reaches CPU PB2 and -6 PB4. Volume/Test Plus and Minus are matrix 15/16. The location list's repeated TEST MINUS at 15 is a printing error settled by the matrix, wiring schematic and coin-door panel drawing.

Direct -5 is an **optional virtual diagnostic override**, not a fitted ticket switch. The driver ORs its latch with `swAvail1` (any asserted 17–24 flasher byte) at PB5, also copies `swAvail1` to PB1, and derives PB3 from solenoid 15 (`swAvail2`). Preserve these driver-specific sense conventions rather than inventing another physical ball switch.

There is no independent GI callback. GI and flasher power are ordinary solenoid-numbered relay outputs. Binary outputs are smoothed in `alvg_vblank` (20 video updates for coils, 30 for lamps), while PWM byte writes follow the CPU writes. The PWM type assigned to 17–24 models brightness; it does not prove bulb fitment.

## Ball path and moving assemblies

The outhole/trough is a two-ball stack with entry switch 17, monitored trough switch 18, transfer coil 13 and shooter-feed coil 30. Coil 13 moves a drained ball into the trough; coil 30 sends the next ball toward shooter-lane switch 21. Ball motion, rather than either coil directly actuating a switch, supplies the sensor transitions. The cabinet Shooter button is 14 and automatic shooter coil 9 is CLL-009 on CKA-002. Startup pulls the virtual shooter, catapult and secret-door plungers back.

The left-outlane **catapult/kickback**, coil 10 at switch 46, returns a ball through the lower-left lane when enabled by the ROM. The secret-door lane reports 55 and coil 1 fires its linear kicker; the plunger gameitem is not a motorized door or position sensor. No door-home contact is printed.

The tunnel reports 23 before a subway transfers the ball into the Megamode vertical eject at 24. Coil 6 ejects that cup. The retained script models the transfer by destroying the sw23 ball and creating one at sw24; authors should build a real subway path. Its angle 170 and strength 18 are recreation tuning. Generic AVK-001's assembly page lists CLL-004, while the game-specific coil table specifies CLL-007 at 6; the definition retains the machine-specific part number and discloses both readings.

Six CASTLE drop targets form two independent three-target banks: 33/34/35 (C-A-S) reset together from coil 11, and 36/37/38 (T-L-E) reset from 12. Each target latches down and has its own switch; there is no extra bank-home switch. The physical banks are ADT-006 and ADT-007. Begin raised and allow the ROM's reset outputs to restore them during play/startup. The table header reports R03 advancing the multiplier after four drops and R02 after five, instead of all six. That is an attributed, unverified firmware-behavior observation; it does not change the six-target physical inventory.

Switches 25/26 and coils 2/3 are the two pop bumpers; 27/28 and 4/5 are the two slingshots. Their solenoid callbacks in this table are commented out because VPX supplies native impact physics and switch pulses. The fitted physical coils remain used.

Rear gate coil 32 is ADV-001. `solDiverterx` moves a flipper gameitem as a diverter and returns it with a 600 ms timer. The catwalk entry/exit (29/30), tower entry/exit (31/32) and bridge (39) are ball-passage sensors, not gate-position marks. The physical rest position is spring return; no gate-home input exists. The timer gives a recreation clue, not a manufacturer dwell specification.

Check trough/subway/eject cups for jams, verify that all three members of each drop bank rise, ensure the gate spring returns freely, and check sling/bumper contacts before tuning software pulses. Exact coil pulse widths, plunger strength and mechanical travel remain table-owned tuning; no unsupported hardware timing is asserted.

## Three flippers and the upper-left activator

There are two lower flippers with CLL-015 coils and one upper-left flipper with **CLL-003**, not three identical coils. Manual PDF 44 / 5-5 explicitly distinguishes them. DOC-002 shows separate coil branches sharing the left cabinet-button return. The lower-left assembly has a normally-open **Upper Flipper Activator** contact (item 22, FLS-002); lower-flipper motion closes it and completes the upper-left branch. Each coil also has its own normally-closed local EOS contact (item 24). The coils are not in series, and the activator is not an EOS or matrix contact.

PinMAME fabricates 45/46 together for the right button and 47/48 together for the left while relay 27 is on. The definition exposes 45/47 as used virtual paired states and 46/48 as assembly callbacks. It does not claim the simultaneous bits are measured power/hold currents. The retained table consumes 46/48 and moves `LeftFlipper` and invisible `LeftFlipper1` together, approximating the mechanical activator sequence. A physical recreation can model the normally-open activator from lower-flipper motion without inventing an independent controller callback or upper-left button. Its exact travel threshold belongs to the table model.

## Lighting authority and known table approximations

The 96-cell lamp matrix fits lamps 1–61. Runtime uses `vpmMapLights AllLights`, which reads each member's `TimerInterval`; object names alone do not supply a binding. Lamps 47/48 illuminate the cabinet Start/Shooter buttons. **57–61 are five playfield billboard lamps**: DOC-004 spells out BILLBOARD BRIDGE/DUNGEON/TOWER/BALLROOM/CATWALK, and the location drawing places them at the corresponding playfield signs. The matrix abbreviates BILLBOARD as BBOARD and the location list as BB. Their retained-table light bindings are physical playfield candidates.

The lamp-location list identifies #555 wedge-base and #44 bayonet-base bulbs and socket parts, including genuinely blank socket cells at the bumper and cabinet-button lamps. The schematic labels the same features with alternate wording (e.g. Hunt Again/Shoot Again, ?/Mystery, BBoard/Billboard); these denote the same devices. Source-literal transcription keeps both the complete table regions and the load-bearing drawing facts.

The coil table, observed ROM service labels and VPX script call **16 Bottom** and **26 Top**, while DOC-002's load drawing labels **16 Top**, feeding **13 clear plus 13 red** playfield bulbs, and **26 Bottom**, feeding **12 bottom-playfield bulbs**. The shared F21 supply also feeds **2–4 unswitched coin-door bulbs** before the drawn relay-26 contact; those cabinet lamps are supply context and receive no invented controller binding. The conflicting claims about controlled playfield regions remain a formal unresolved conflict. The definition retains fitted, used GI relays 16/26 with generic playfield region labels and conflicted provenance. The drawing quantities are source candidates in notes; no per-address quantity or top/bottom region is validated. A public ROM trace cannot observe physical bulb wiring, and no grouping is inferred from proximity. An owner or technician must provide a wired socket survey or physical-machine service video linking selectors 16/26 to every bulb they illuminate. Normal backbox relay **29** controls two fused 12-bulb branches (24 bulbs total). Special backbox relay **31** supplies a dashed group labelled [4/6] BULBS, with its count digit uncertain, and another parallel branch drawn with two unlabelled bulb symbols. Both the degraded digit and the scope of the printed count are unresolved, so no total is promoted for 31. The return-footer voltage digit is also uncertain between 5 and 6 VAC; no typed voltage is recorded. Their BLU/BLU returns split across PP1-11 and PP2-10 and are shared with different normal-insert branches. Both outputs retain explicit backbox locations.

Flasher relay **15** supplies common power to 17–24 through PCA-007. DOC-002 draws quantities **2,2,2,1,1,2,1,4** for outputs 17–24: Dungeon, Bridge, Tower, Ballroom, Secret Door, Catwalk, Pit/Lock, Backbox. Playfield flashers use #47 bulbs; the four backbox flashers use #906. A dome or a pair of lightmap objects is not an extra emitter. The table supplies a bulb-mesh Light for only one member of several physical two-bulb branches; F23a/F23b have no rendered bulb mesh. Those omissions remain explicit.

# Pin-Bot (Williams, 1986)

Coverage: **partial - every controller address enumerated and named, wiring, polarity, mechanisms, variants and
recreation behaviour validated; spatial placement is the one open requirement (two visor G.I. outputs have no
socket position, the playfield G.I. sockets come only from the retained table, and one switch placement is not
validated).**

The definition is generated by `tools/curate_pinbot.py`; the spatial review is
`reports/spatial/williams/pinbot-1986.md`.

## Identity and variants

PIN-BOT is Williams model 549 (IPDB 1796, OPDB `G41Z8-MJKvP`), manufactured from October 6, 1986 on a System 11A
CPU board (D-11392-549) with an alphanumeric master display board and a Background Speech & Sound board. Its
playfield was reused for Jack*Bot (1995), a separate machine and record.

All ten pinned `pb_*` drivers run on this one physical machine and share `pbGameData` through `CORE_CLONEDEF`:

| Driver | Firmware |
| --- | --- |
| `pb_l5` | Williams L-5 production, the parent and the driver every runtime run used |
| `pb_l3`, `pb_l2`, `pb_l1` | Williams production revisions (U27 L-3, L-2, L-1, each with the U26 L-1) |
| `pb_p4` | Williams P-4 prototype U27 with the U26 L-1 |
| `pb_l5h` | 2012 community modification of L-5 (free play, Solar Value) |
| `pb_j1`, `pb_j2`, `pb_j3`, `pb_j5` | PEMBOT community game code (2020-2026) for the Pin-Bot hardware |

The U27 of every set in the local ROM corpus (`l5`, `l5h`, `l3`, `l2`, `l1`, `p4`, `j5`) holds the same lamp,
switch and coil name tables entry for entry (`evidence/excerpts/williams.pinbot.1986/rom-name-tables.md`), so no
variant addresses a device the others lack. `pb_j1`-`pb_j3` are not in the local corpus and were not read.

## Controller facts that matter to a recreation

- **Generation and display.** `GEN_S11X` with `s11_dispS11`: players 1 and 2 are seven-character sixteen-segment
  alphanumeric displays, players 3 and 4 seven-digit seven-segment displays with commas, and Ball In Play/Match and
  Credits are two-digit displays exposed by LibPinMAME as four single digits (indices 4-7). LibPinMAME's index 8
  (128 x 32) is PinMAME's own rendering of the alphanumerics, not hardware. The ROM's font draws `-` as a blank and
  `5`/`S` and `0`/`O` with the same segments.
- **A/C select relay.** Solenoid 14 is the Solenoid Select relay. Each switched driver (Q22-Q25, Q30-Q33) serves an
  "A" load while 14 is released and a "C" load while it is energized. PinMAME publishes the A loads at 1-8 and the C
  loads at 25-32 (`sxx.muxSol = 14`), so a consumer reads them directly. In the Coil Test and in play the ROM energizes
  14 before every C pulse and releases it for every A pulse. Pinned `s11.c`'s comment "Mux relay is solenoid #12" in
  the `pb_` output block is wrong for this machine.
- **Special solenoids.** `sxx.ssSw = {53,0,48,54,55,52}`: Lower Jet (17) follows switch 53, Left Jet (19) switch 48,
  Left Kicker (20) switch 54, Right Kicker (21) switch 55 and Upper Jet (22) switch 52 while the enable (23) is on.
  Special #2 (18) has no switch: it is the Left Visor G.I. lamp load, fired only by the CPU.
- **Flippers.** Two cabinet-wired flippers. `FLIP_SWNO(10,11)` copies the cabinet buttons at public 84 (left) and 82
  (right) into the Lane Change switches 10 and 11 on every update, so a consumer drives 82/84, never 10/11. PinMAME
  fabricates 45-48 from the same buttons. The flipper assemblies' End of Stroke switches have no matrix address.
- **Polarity.** System 11 reads the matrix uninverted and `pbGameData` has no inversion mask. In the ROM's Switch
  Levels test every address 1-60 shows its name while held at 1, so every fitted switch is normally open. The test
  never reports 61-64; it reports 57 and 58 (both unfitted) by their "NOT USED" names.
- **General illumination.** Playfield G.I. is the relay at 12 and insert-board (backglass) G.I. the relay at 11; the
  retained table treats both as on while the relay is released. In attract mode the ROM toggles them alternately.
  Pinned PinMAME's `pb_` block types 10 as the reverse-acting "Playfield GI" instead, a brightness-model label that
  disagrees with the manual's table and the ROM's own names; 10 and 18 are the visor's #1251 G.I. lamps.

## Mechanisms and behaviour

- **Visor.** One 11 rpm 24 VAC motor, run by the relay at 13, turns a cam that raises the visor (C-11159) and lowers
  the five teeth targets (28-32) on their carrier together. The cam's snap-action switches are Visor Closed (46) and
  Visor Open (47). The motor turns one way, so the relay alone decides motion, and a host model must close 46 or 47
  at the ends of travel while the motor runs. At power-up the ROM runs the motor from about 1.7 s; when 47 closes it
  stops the motor and kicks both eyes, right (8) then left (7). With both balls home it then runs the motor again and
  stops when 46 closes. With 46 or 47 held closed from power-up the motor kept running for the whole 30 s probe,
  consistent with the ROM acting on a limit switch closing while the motor runs. Lighting all five chest rows opens
  the visor for multiball; balls lock in the eyes (25, 26).
- **Chest panel.** A 5 x 5 grid of #555 lamps on the C-11309 Chest Lamp Matrix Board: columns Yellow, Blue, Amber,
  Green, Red (lamps 28-32, 36-40, 44-48, 52-56, 60-64), rows 1 (upper) to 5 (lower). The teeth targets light it
  vertically and the right 5-bank (33-37, top to bottom) horizontally.
- **Lifting ramp.** The B-11304 mechanism raises (5) and lowers (6) the end of the left ramp; switch 44 closes when it
  is down. At power-up the ROM pulses 5 once and retries it about every 1.3 s while 44 stays closed. At game start it
  pulses 6 together with 5 about every 1.7 s until 44 closes. The rules raise the ramp from the flashing drop target
  to give a shot at the Score Energy target (45).
- **Trough and serve.** Two balls. The outhole (16) kicker (1) feeds the trough (17 nearest the shooter lane, 18
  behind it), and the feeder (2) serves into the shooter lane (20). At power-up the ROM retries the outhole kick about
  every 2.2 s while 16 stays closed. In play it serves with 2 and repeats the serve about every 1.8 s until 20 closes.
  In the static gameplay run, where the visor never reached a limit switch, no ball was served; in the reactive run,
  with the visor closed, the ROM served about 2.5 s after the start, before the ramp was down.
- **Vortex.** The plunger shot enters the Vortex at the top right: 20K (22, upper), 100K (23, middle), 5K (24, lower,
  the exit), multiplied X1-X10 by successive plunges.
- **Single eject** (38, kicked by 3), **3-bank drop targets** (49-51, reset by 4; the ROM resets them at game start
  and retries about every 1.25 s while all three stay down, but not at power-up), **jet bumpers** (each hit also
  flashes the Energy flashers at 30), **slingshots**, **Advance Planet** (19) and **Score Energy** (45) targets and
  three **10-point rubbers** (56, 59, 60).

## Manual notes

- The Switches parts list misprints rows 33-37 (it gives 33 a visor-target part and describes it as a visor target).
  The Switch-Matrix Table, the ROM's switch table and the retained script agree that 33-37 are the right 5-bank,
  top to bottom; each device note keeps the printed row.
- The Solenoids/Flashers list prints relay part 5580-09555-00 for 11-14 and the Solenoid Table 5580-09555-01. This is
  a wiring-detail difference; the definition follows the table.
- The Lamp-Matrix Table's legend gives the #555 bulb as 24-8767, while the Chest Lamp Matrix Board list gives 24-8767
  as the socket and 24-8768 as the lamp. The definition records 24-8768 for the bulb.
- Lamp 59 and switches 21, 27, 41-43, 57, 58 and 61-64 are printed "Not Used" and the ROM names them NOT USED.

## What would complete the record

- A photograph or drawing of the visor's #1251 G.I. lamp sockets (solenoids 10 and 18) would give them a placement.
- A second independent recreation, or a measurement of a real playfield, placing the Enter Ramp switch (40) where the
  switch drawing's leader ends would validate its placement.
- A G.I. lamp layout from the machine (a photograph of an unrestored playfield or a G.I. wiring list) would let the 27
  playfield G.I. sockets the retained table models be validated; the manual prints no G.I. bulb count.

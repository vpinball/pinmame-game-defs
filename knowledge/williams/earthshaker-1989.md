# Williams Earthshaker (1989)

Coverage: **partial**. The definition is `machines/partial/williams/earthshaker-1989.json`, regenerated by `tools/curate_earthshaker.py`. The physical authority is the Williams Earthshaker! operations manual (Internet Archive item `williams-earthshaker-pinball-manual`). The runtime authority is the retained known-working VPW-Lite v006 table, which binds `esha_la3`. Pinned PinMAME owns the controller contract.

Two kinds of ROM evidence settle what the documents leave open:

- LibPinMAME runs of `esha_la3` and `esha_pa1`, summarized in `evidence/runtime/system-11/`.
- The ROMs' own switch and coil name tables, decoded by `tools/s11_rom_name_tables.py`.

Two things keep the record partial:

- **Spatial placement:**
  - The second bulb of flasher circuits 16, 26, and 27 has no drawn or modelled location.
  - Flasher 14's second bulb is drawn where the retained table models no flasher.
  - Building windows 17-25 are placed at their window meshes' centres, and the prototype optos 25/26 at the building's primitive origin; both are only observed.
- **Prototype building:** the sinking Institute building is undocumented beyond its parts.

## Identity and driver family

Earthshaker is Williams game 568 (IPDB 753). Pinned PinMAME's nine `esha_*` drivers share one `eshaGameData` and all run on this System 11B machine:

- **Production:** LA-3 (the parent), LA-1, and the German LG-1 and LG-2.
- **Family version:** PR-4. PinMAME comments it "Prototype ?? also known as F-1", but notes that the ROM itself "says PR 4, not F-1". Whether it is a prototype is unsettled; its name tables match LA-3.
- **Later software:** the 2016 LA-4C Competition MOD and the Metallica MOD (MA-3). MA-3 renames the Fault to FIGHT FIRE and the Quake motor to CROWD MOTOR in its service text.
- **Prototypes:** PA-1 and PA-4.

The catalog dates the parent 1988 from its firmware; the machine is 1989.

The prototypes are the one hardware difference. PinMAME comments that PA-4 "supports collapsable building", and the PA-1 ROM names solenoid 9 BUILDING MOTOR and switches 25/26 BLDNG HEIGHT 1/2. Production ROMs print UNUSED for all three. They are recorded as `compatible` rather than `identical` (see "The Institute building").

## Controller and public addresses

The machine uses `controllers/pinmame/system-11.json`. Its `INITGAME` line declares these settings:

- **Generation and display:** `GEN_S11B` with two 16-character alphanumeric rows (`s11_dispS11b2`).
- **Multiplexing:** solenoid 12 is the A/C mux relay.
- **Flippers:** `FLIP_SWNO(58,57)` with no CPU flipper drive.
- **Switch 2:** `S11_MUXSW2`.
- **Absent:** no special-solenoid switch map, no sound overlay board, and no custom solenoids.

The public addresses break down as follows:

- **Diagnostics:** -7 Advance, -6 Auto-Up/Manual-Down, -5 CPU diagnostic, -4 sound diagnostic.
- **Switches:** matrix 1-64, sequential column-major (`(column - 1) * 8 + row`).
  - Column 1 carries the cabinet inputs: plumb bob 1, credit 3, coins 4-6, slam 7, high-score reset 8.
  - Unused: 47-49, 51, and 59-64.
- **Country jumper:** DIP 0.
- **Switched solenoids:**
  - 1-8 are the A side, pulsed while relay 12 is released.
  - 25-32 are the same eight drivers routed to their C-side flashers while 12 is energized.
  - 8A is not used; 32 (08C) is.
- **Controlled solenoids:** 9-16.
  - 9 is the optional building motor relay.
  - 10, 11, and 15 are G.I. relays; 12 is the A/C relay.
  - 13 is the top popper; 14 and 16 are flashers.
- **Special solenoids:** 17-22, in Special #1-#6 order. These are three jet bumpers, two slingshots, and the quake shaker motor (22).
- **Virtual solenoids:** 23 is the ROM's game-on / special-solenoid enable, 45-48 are PinMAME's synthetic flipper states, and 24, 33-44, 49, and 50 are unused.
- **Lamps:** 1-64.

PinMAME inverts nothing on System 11: the switch read returns `core_getSwCol` directly, so public 1 is a closed matrix contact. Every fitted production switch rests open (`normally_closed: false`). The prototype building optos 25/26 carry no rest state, because no source or run establishes it.

## Switches

The switch-matrix table (printed page 68) and the switches list (printed 66) agree on almost every fitted address, and the ROM's switch table has one entry per public address from 1 to 58, in address order. They disagree in five places:

- **Switch 2:** the list prints "Not Used" and the matrix "C Side Power A/C Relay". No playfield switch is fitted. The position reads the A/C relay's C-side power contact, and PinMAME copies relay 12's state into it.
- **Coin chutes:** the list and the ROM put the right chute on 4 and the left on 6; the matrix reverses them.
- **Center coin chute (5):** "Not Used (USA)", fitted only on non-US three-chute doors (`optional`).
- **Ball Trough 2 (12):** the matrix prints "(Mid)" and the list "(left)". The trough holds three balls with 11 right and 13 left, so 12 is the middle one, as the matrix says.
- **Building Height 1/2 (25, 26):** the list prints opto parts on a "Bldg Positioner Bd"; the matrix is blank and production ROMs call them unused.

The 3-bank drop targets (27-29) are opto interrupters on the C-12559 board. The ROM treats 1 as a dropped target:

- **Targets held at 1:** after Game-Over entry the reset coil (3) fired seven times, and seven more after Start.
- **Targets held at 0:** the reset fired once at each point.

The flipper-lane-change inputs 57 (right) and 58 (left) are optotransistors on the Backbox Interconnect board (switches list, footnote **1); the manual does not show how they sense the buttons. `FLIP_SWNO(58,57)` without `FLIP_SOL` makes PinMAME's `core_updateSw` rewrite 57/58 from the flipper button bits on every update, so 1 means pressed and a host write to 57 or 58 is overwritten. A consumer presses the flippers through public 82 (right) and 84 (left). The retained script writes `Controller.Switch(57/58)` from its flipper keys; those writes are overwritten on the next update and have no effect, so that is how the table behaves, not a path a recreation should copy. The same keys reach the VPinMAME `S11.VBS` library, whose `vpmKeyDown`/`vpmKeyUp` set `swLRFlip = 82` and `swLLFlip = 84` (excerpt `vpm-script-library-flippers`). The definition enumerates PinMAME's whole flipper column, 81-88: 82 and 84 are used, and 81, 83 and 85-88 are never read, because `FLIP_SWNO(58,57)` sets no `FLIP_EOS` or upper `FLIP_SW` bit and the System 11 ROM reads only matrix columns 1-8. The script's `NoUpperLeftFlipper`/`NoUpperRightFlipper` calls also stop `S11.VBS` from writing 81/83.

Jet bumpers are assigned by the manual's switch drawing, which matches the ROM's names and the lamp and coil drawings:

| Switch | Jet bumper | Coil | Lamp |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 54 | Top (uppermost) | 21 | 41 |
| 53 | Right | 19 | 43 |
| 52 | Left (nearest the left wall) | 17 | 42 |

The retained script's `Bumper1_Hit` (the top bumper) pulses 52 and `Bumper3_Hit` (the left one) pulses 54. That is a defect of the table, not a question about the machine.

## Flippers

The machine has three flippers:

- **Lower right:** C-11626-R-3, FL-11630 coil.
- **Lower left:** C-11626-L-3, FL-11630 coil.
- **Upper left:** C-11626-L-6, FL-11722 coil.

Each cabinet button fires its coils directly: the right button (Orn-Vio, 1P19-1) fires the right flipper, and the left button (Orn-Gry, 1P19-2) fires both left flippers. Each assembly has an end-of-stroke switch (03-7811) that opens the power winding and is not in the matrix. Public 45-48 are PinMAME fabrications from the buttons, which a consumer drives at 82 (right) and 84 (left).

## Ball path

- **Trough.** A drained ball rests on the outhole (10). The Outhole Kicker (1) kicks it into a three-ball trough: 13 left, 12 middle, 11 right. The Ball Release / Shooter Lane Feeder (2) feeds the ball at 11 into the shooter lane (50).
- **Skill shot and on-ramp.** From the plunger the ball climbs the on-ramp past 33 (50K), 34 (25K), and 35 (100K) to the jet bumpers. A strong shot bypasses them (36) and drops onto the center-ramp exit (rules, printed 8).
- **Center ramp.** A ramp shot crosses Center Ramp Entry (44), Middle (45), and End (46) and returns down the left side.
- **Right ramp and top popper.** Right Ramp Entry (43) leads up the right side to the Top Ball Popper (37). Its coil (13) lifts the ball onto a wire ramp across the top to the California/Nevada fault. In the retained table the fault switches that ball between two exit ramps: left while closed, right while open. The right ramp is one of the two lock routes.
- **Drop hole.** A ball in the drop hole falls under the playfield across Drop Hole 1 (38) and Drop Hole 2 (39) into the Bottom Ball Popper (40). The popper coil (6) lifts it to the right return lane.
- **Eject hole.** The eject hole (20, zone 5) kicks with coil 5 and is the Quick Multi-Ball lock.
- **Fault loop.** The loop under the California/Nevada fault is zone 6 (31 right, 32 left).
- **Targets and captive ball.** Standups 19, 21, 22, and 30 are zones 1-4. The captive ball (23) is zone 9. The right return lanes are 14 (zone 7) and 15, and the left return lane is 18 (zone 8).

## The California/Nevada fault

The California Fault coil (4, AE-23-800) drives the Zone Opener Assembly (C-12429) under the state maps:

- **Mechanism:** a threaded plunger turns a flat cam on the zone-opener shaft, so each pulse steps the fault open or closed. The State Assembly (C-12433) slide carries the shifting California map.
- **Feedback:** a roller micro-switch beside the cam (42, 5647-12073-06) reports "Fault Open".
- **Retained script:** it toggles open and closed on each pulse, slides both map primitives, and sets 42 at the end of the opening stroke.

## The quake shaker

Solenoid 22 is the Shaker Assembly (B-12388): an 11 RPM motor (14-7951) turning an eccentric weight, mounted under the playfield. The location drawing marks it with a dashed callout.

The Solenoids/Flashers list prints 22 "Not Used", but three sources name the motor:

- the Solenoid Table ("Quake Motor");
- the coil tests run on `esha_la3` and `esha_pa1` (QUAKE MOTOR), a name the coil tables of LA-1, LG-1, LG-2, and PR-4 share; MA-3 prints CROWD MOTOR;
- the ROM's QUAKE INTENSITY adjustment text.

## The Institute building

Production machines carry a static building (Building Assembly B-12728). It holds nine #555 window lamps on the Building Lamp Board (C-12427) and has no motor, slide, or opto. The windows are lamps 17-25, numbered on the decal as below (seen from the front):

| Row | Windows | Lamps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Top | 7 8 9 | 17 18 19 |
| Middle | 4 5 6 | 20 21 22 |
| Bottom | 1 2 3 | 23 24 25 |

The prototypes sank the building into the playfield with a motor. The production manual still carries its parts: the Solenoids/Flashers list prints "Building Motor Relay" (5580-12145-01) and, as a separate item, "Building Motor" (14-7941-1) at 9, and the switches list prints Building Height 1/2 optos on a "Bldg Positioner Bd" at 25/26. Solenoid 9 therefore drives a relay that switches the motor. The location drawing's callout 9 leads down into the building, but no page shows the relay's board or the motor's supply. The Solenoid Table prints 9 "Not Used" and the matrix leaves 25/26 blank.

The two ROMs run here, `esha_la3` and `esha_pa1`, still drive the relay; the other sets were not run:

- **Game-Over entry:** both energize 9. With 25/26 held static in any of the four combinations, the ROM releases it after about 5.9 s.
- **Modelled building:** in nine `esha_la3` runs the host moved a building and rewrote 25/26 while 9 was on. The ROM ignored the first change of 25/26, whatever it was, then released 9 within about 0.1 s of the next fall of 25 or rise of 26. It never stopped on a rise of 25 or a fall of 26.
- **No time threshold:** two runs rule one out. A fall of 25 stopped the motor 0.73 s after it started when a rise of 25 had come first. A fall of 25 at 2.50 s did not stop it when it was the first change.
- **Timeout:** in that second run 9 stayed on for 10.08 s, longer than the static 5.9 s, with changes 2.5 and 5.0 s apart. The timeout may run from the last change; the runs do not settle that.

Which physical heights those edges mark is not documented; the manual has no page for the positioner board. That is why the record lists `variant_differences`. A production recreation needs only the static building and can leave 9, 25, and 26 alone. A recreation of the moving building needs the positioner geometry.

The retained table models a moving building with its own four-state guess (`InstituteTimer`). The table's comment says the production ROMs still contain the building code, which the harness confirms.

## Lamps and flashers

The lamp-matrix table (printed 67) and the lamps list (printed 69) name every lamp. Lamps 58-64 are the jackpot-value lamps in the speaker panel under the score displays. The road-sign lamps 49 and 57 are placed at the bulb-cover meshes the script fades, not at the table's larger glow lights.

Two lamp entries disagree between the sources:

- **Zone 8:** the matrix prints the circled zone-8 marker in the Left Outlane cell (16). The list, switch 18, and the rules put zone 8 on the left return lane (15), so the marker is a printing slip.
- **Lamp 53:** the list's "(Eject Hole)" is wrong. The matrix names the Center Standup (zone 4), which is switch 30; the eject hole is zone 5 (lamp 52).

Flashers:

- **Switched C side (25-32):**

  | 25 | Captive ball | #89 |
  | 26 | Center ramp 1 and building | two |
  | 27 | Center ramp 2 and spinner | two |
  | 28 | Center ramp 3 | one |
  | 29 | Center ramp 4 | one |
  | 30 | Right ramp 1 | one |
  | 31 | Right ramp 2 | one |
  | 32 | Right ramp 3 | two |

- **Controlled:**
  - 14 (Jackpot/Sun): two #906. The drawing's first callout-14 leader ends on the center arrow insert where the table's `f114` light sits. The second ends on lamp 45's own insert (the lamp drawing's lamp-45 leader reaches the same insert), where the table models no flasher. The table's second bound light, `f114a`, sits on an unlabelled round insert that neither drawing gives a callout, so only the first bulb is placed.
  - 16 (On-ramp and jet bumper): two bulbs.

For 16, 26, and 27 the drawing and the table show one location each, and the second bulb's location is unknown. For 14 it is drawn but not modelled. The two lists disagree on some bulb counts (07C and 08C) and on the bottom popper coil (06A AE-24-900 against AE-23-800). The Solenoid Table and the popper assembly (D-11335-2, AE-23-800) are followed.

## General illumination

Three solenoid-driven relays switch G.I.:

- **10:** upper playfield.
- **15:** lower playfield.
- **11:** the backbox insert board.

G.I. is lit while its relay is released. Pinned `s11.c` types the three as reverse-acting #44 G.I., and the retained script turns its GIU (10) and GI (15) light collections off while the callbacks are on.

The relay boards' own parts page (printed 47) puts 10 and 15 on C-11902-1 (relay 5580-12145-00) and 11 on C-11998-1 (relay 5580-09555-01), and the record follows it. Two other sources disagree:

- The Solenoid Table's note 4 prints the board letters reversed, and gives 5580-12145-01 for 10 and 15.
- The Solenoids/Flashers list's footnote puts all three relays on C-11998-1 and lists 5580-12145-01 for all three.

The manual prints no G.I. bulb count, so placements come from the retained table's collections: 13 upper and 18 lower bulbs.

## Service

A fresh ROM boots to FACTORY SETTING and waits there; coins and Start are ignored. To reach Game-Over mode:

1. Set Manual-Down (-6 = 0).
2. Press Advance twice (Game ID, then Ad 70).
3. Set Auto-Up (-6 = 1).
4. Press Advance.

The same recipe also leaves FACTORY SETTING on `eatpm_l4` (Elvira) and `whirl_l3` (Whirlwind).

The Auto Burn-in adjustment (Ad 67) runs Music, Display, Sound, All Lamps, and then the coil test, which names every solenoid step. The switch tests are not part of burn-in and were not reached. The ROM's switch table carries the names the switch tests print.

## Spatial convention

Coordinates are x/964 and y/2162 from the retained table's own bounds. Building windows 17-25 are placed at the centres of their window meshes: each retained window primitive's local bounding box is scaled by 100, rotated by its 22-degree `ObjRotZ` (VPX's `x' = x cos - y sin`, `y' = x sin + y cos`), and moved to the primitives' shared position (440.5, 438.0). The three windows of a column share one point and differ only in height. The lamp-board sockets behind them are not surveyed, so these placements are observed only. The Fault Open switch and coil are anchored on the California map primitive. The prototype building-height optos (25, 26) are anchored at the same building origin and are also observed only. The building motor relay (9) has no playfield placement.

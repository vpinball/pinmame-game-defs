# Williams The Machine: Bride of Pinbot (1991)

Coverage: **author-ready**. The definition is `machines/author-ready/williams/the-machine-bride-of-pinbot-1991.json`, regenerated by `tools/curate_bride_of_pinbot.py`. The physical authority is the Williams operations manual 16-50002-101 (March 1991); the runtime authority is the retained known-working VPW 1.0.3 table, which binds `bop_l7`; pinned PinMAME owns the controller contract. LibPinMAME runs of `bop_l7` (`tools/bop_head_mech_experiment.py`, summarized in `evidence/runtime/wpc-alpha/bride-of-pinbot-head-helmet-and-service-names.json`) settle the questions the documents leave open.

## Identity and driver family

The machine was sold as The Machine: Bride of Pinbot; the manual cover reads only THE MACHINE (IPDB 1502). PinMAME's fourteen `bop_*` drivers share one `bopGameData` and are all this physical machine: Williams L-2 through L-7, the LED ghost-fix D builds, and L-8/D-8, which PinMAME describes as a billionaire multiplayer patch of L-7. The catalog dates `bop_l7` 1992 because that is the L-7 firmware year; the machine itself is 1991.

One driver difference reaches a recreation. `bop_handleMech` recognises the name `bop_l7` only: when a host sets `HandleMechanics` to -1 or -2 it writes face 1 into the ROM's stored head position (`wpc_ram[0x1fc9]`). The other thirteen drivers never receive that reset (see the head section).

## Controller and public addresses

The machine is a WPC alphanumeric game (`GEN_WPCALPHA_2`, two sixteen-character alphanumeric displays, `wpc_dispAlpha`) and uses `controllers/pinmame/wpc-alpha.json`:

- **Coin door:** switches 1-8 are the dedicated coin-door inputs (D1-D8).
- **Switch matrix:** 11-88 in WPC column/row notation.
- **Generic flipper inputs:** 111-118, of which only 112 (right button) and 114 (left button) are live.
- **Solenoids:** 1-28 are driver outputs.
  - 29/30 are J111 state channels.
  - 31 is the real Game-On relay.
  - 32 is constant zero.
  - 33-44 are unused.
  - 45-48 are synthetic flipper states.
  - 49 is PinMAME's simulator shooter channel.
- **Lamps:** 11-88 are the 64-lamp matrix. 91-98 and 101-108 are the sixteen helmet chase lights.
- **General illumination:** five strings, 0-4.

`bopGameData` declares `lampCol = 2` for the helmet. It is the only WPC-Alpha driver that does, and the profile's lamp group now admits those two auxiliary columns.

PinMAME inverts nothing on this game: the inverted-switch mask is all zeros. That matches the physical construction, because the only opto the manual names (23, Ticket Opto) is not fitted. Every fitted playfield switch is a closed-when-active contact. Switch 24 is a permanently closed link (A-8630), and 22 is closed while the coin door is shut.

## Switches

The switch matrix (printed 2-40) and switch-locations list (2-41) agree on every fitted address. The ROM's own switch-edge test names match them too. 42, 48, 61, 62, 66, 68, and 78 are printed Not Used. 81-88 carry no description at all and produce no response in the switch test.

Jet-bumper switches: the manual, its drawing, pinned `bop.c`, and the ROM (`U.R. JET BUMPER` for 53, `U.L. JET BUMPER` for 54) agree on this assignment:

| Switch | Jet bumper | Coil |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 53 | Upper right | 11 |
| 54 | Upper left | 9 |
| 55 | Lower | 13 |

The retained script pulses a permutation of those three switches, as does the older VP10 Rev 2.0 table retained beside it. It is a harmless defect in the table, not a disagreement about the machine.

The flipper buttons appear in the matrix as 11 (right) and 12 (left), copied there from the generic cabinet inputs because the game declares `FLIP_SWNO(12,11)` and no CPU flipper drive.

## Flippers

Two FL-11630 flippers are wired straight to the cabinet buttons (Blu-Yel J109-7 right, Gry-Yel J109-5 left) through the Game-On relay. There are no upper flippers. Public 45-48 are PinMAME fabrications from the buttons, used only to animate the bats.

## Ball path

The playfield ball path runs through these stages:

1. **Trough.** A drained ball lands in the outhole (switch 38). Outhole (1) kicks it into a three-ball trough: Left 27, Center 26, Right 25. Ball Release (2) feeds the ball at 25 to the shooter lane feeder (52).
2. **Shuttle skill shot.** The plunged ball climbs the right-hand skill-shot lane between rubber cylinders and falls back past five rollovers, stacked top to bottom as 35 (25K), 34 (200K), 33 (100K), 32 (75K), 31 (50K). The Skill Shot Kicker (5, AE-24-900 on a B-11395-1 kicker) then fires it into play from the bottom of the lane.
3. **Shuttle (left) ramp.** Every ramp shot crosses Left Ramp Enter (76). At the top, the Controlled Gate (4, A-14406) sends the ball two ways:
   - Energized, the gate routes it to the mini-playfield entry (73).
   - Otherwise the ball continues through Enter Head (47) into the head.
4. **Mini-playfield (upper right, A-14358).** It holds three jet bumpers and the jets sling kicker (switch 56, coil 14). Its lamps are 86-88 on the rear bracket. It has two exits:
   - Mini Exit Left (74) returns the ball to the playfield.
   - Mini Exit Right (75) returns the ball toward the shooter. The rules call that "leave Pinbot playfield on right side" and it raises the skill-shot multiplier.
5. **Wireform lock.** Balls leaving the head run down the wireform past Wireform Top (71) to a post that holds them at Wireform Bottom (72). The Wire Ball Holder (6) retracts the post to release them toward the left inlane. The playfield parts list carries the post as the A-14046 Disappear Post Assembly, whose AE-26-1200 coil matches output 6.
6. **Small wheel.** The far-left shot drops to an under-playfield kicker (switch 46). The Under Playfield Kicker (3) fires the ball back up at the far left.
7. **Other shots:**
   - Left Loop (43).
   - Right Loop Top/Bottom (44/45).
   - The Heart (right) ramp, entered at 77 and made at 41.
   - Spinner 51.
   - Standups 28, 36, and 37 (B-11854-4 red targets).
   - Slingshots 57/58 (coils 10/12).

## The rotating head

The head (A-14121) is a box carrying four face plates, turned by a +12 V DC motor (A-14119). The motor is fed by the A-13892-2 Motor Regulator Board, which drops +20 V to +12 V.

- **Drive and direction.** Head Motor (28) switches the motor's low side. The Motor Relay (27, A-14423-1) is a double-pole change-over relay that swaps the two motor leads. The ROM keeps it released for forward (clockwise) moves and energizes it for reverse. The manual's head tests call these directions clockwise and counter-clockwise.
- **Face 1, the mouth (Face Plate A-14130).** One opening. Head Mouth (65) closes while a ball rests there, and the Head Mouth Kicker (8) kicks it out.
- **Face 2, the eyes (Face Plate A-14131).** Two openings behind a passive Y lane-select diverter. Head Left Eye (63) and Head Right Eye (64) have their own kickers, 15 and 16. Each kicker is an AE-30-2000 coil on its own bracket with its own mini microswitch (assemblies A-14125, A-14124, A-14123).
- **Faces 3 and 4.** No openings; ball guides return the ball.
- **Service tests.** The ROM's normal solenoid test skips 8, 15, 16 and the motor. A separate head-coil test fires 8 only with face 1 up, and 15 then 16 at face 2.

Face Position (67, mini microswitch 5647-12693-06) is the head's only feedback. The switch-locations drawing prints it above and behind the head box; no retained table object models it, so its placement is anchored at the head assembly's centre. The manual says only that it must be open over an indentation and closed off one. Pinned PinMAME's simulation and the retained VPW script model it differently. The runs therefore drove 67 from three synthetic patterns while integrating the head angle from outputs 27/28. In every case `bop_l7` behaved the same way:

- **Faces 1-3.** Moving to face 1, 2 or 3, the ROM stops the motor shortly after 67 closes.
- **Face 4.** Moving to face 4, it stops shortly after 67 opens.
- **Homing.** The head tests home by running to the next opening edge of 67, which the ROM treats as face 4, and then stepping one face. Under one synthetic pattern, four start angles homed onto three different physical positions, so these patterns give the ROM no absolute home mark. The real cam must give it one: the manual's head-test introduction (printed 1-32/1-33) says the head homes to face 1 when a head test starts, re-times every face position (calibrates) if it cannot home, and shows ERROR UNABLE TO CALIBRATE HEAD if that fails. No run showed that error, but the synthetic patterns' identical switch-closed windows do not reproduce whatever the real cam provides.
- **Stored face.** The ROM keeps the current face in NVRAM. That rests on pinned `bop.c` (its header comment and its `wpc_ram[0x1fc9]` face reset) and on the retained script's advice to delete the `.nv` file when the head desynchronizes; every harness run started from the same empty NVRAM, so the runs themselves do not vary the stored face.

A recreation must therefore do two things:

- **Produce the edges.** Generate those edges as the modelled head arrives at each face. The known-working VPW model closes 67 only while faces 1-3 dwell. That puts the face-4 opening edge where the head leaves face 3, so the table's standard path leaves the head just past the face it left (face 3 moving forward, face 1 in reverse) while the ROM believes face 4; only its P-ROC path keeps turning to the next face and snaps (`delayHeadStop`, script lines 1115-1170). Faces 3 and 4 are both blank, so play is unaffected, but a recreation that must show face 4 correctly needs that coast-to-next-face behaviour.
- **Keep the stored face in step.** Keep the ROM's stored face aligned with the modelled head. The VPW table does this by starting its head on face 1 and setting `HandleMechanics = -1`, which for `bop_l7` writes face 1 into the stored position.

The physical cam that produces the edges on a real machine is not documented. It is implementation detail below the ROM contract.

## Lamps and flashers

The lamp matrix (2-38) and lamp-locations list (2-39) disagree at three addresses, and the ROM's single-lamp test, which shows each name beside its lamp number, sides with the locations list every time. At 25 the matrix prints `Circle 500K` where the list and ROM say `Circle 50K`. At 61 and 63 the matrix swaps `Right Ramp 100K` and `Right Ramp Million`; the ROM shows RIGHT RAMP MILL at 61 and RIGHT RAMP 100K at 63.

- **Bulbs.** Shoot Again (18), Space Shuttle (37), Launch Pad (38), and Wire Ball Lock (64) are #44 bulbs; every other lamp is #555.
- **Backbox inserts.** Lamps 71-78 (the Jackpot ladder) and 81-85 (the backglass Hip, Middle Leg, Knee, Foot, and Shoulder) are printed "(on insert)" and live in the backbox.
- **Mini-playfield lamps.** 86, 87, and 88 are three bulbs on the bracket at the rear of the mini-playfield, left to right.

Flashers 17-24 are:

| Output | Flasher | Location |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 17 | Billion | Centre insert |
| 18 | Left Ramp | |
| 19 | Jackpot | Far left |
| 20 | Skill Shot | Skill-shot lane |
| 21 | Left Helmet | Red mini dome on the helmet |
| 22 | Right Helmet | Red mini dome on the helmet |
| 23 | Jets Enter | Dome below the helmet, right |
| 24 | Left Loop | Dome below the helmet, left |

- **Backbox bulbs.** 18, 19 and 21-24 each also light a #906 bulb on the backbox insert panel. The table prints bulb types, not counts, so one insert bulb each is assumed.
- **Dome bulb type.** The manual disagrees with itself on the dome bulbs. Both copies of the solenoid table print #555, while the solenoid-locations page prints #89 and the helmet parts list calls it a Single Flashlamp Assy. Pinned PinMAME models all eight flashers as #89.

## Helmet chase lights

Sixteen #555 bulbs sit on two 8-lamp boards in the helmet (A-14508). They are switched by the A-14007 Chase Light board:

- **Drive.** The ROM shifts a 16-bit pattern through data (25) and clock (26) into two 74LS164 registers driving sixteen 2N6427 transistors.
- **Power.** The bulbs' B+ comes from G.I. string 02. The manual's helmet test says so, confirming the guess in pinned PinMAME's FIXME comment.
- **Public addresses.** PinMAME publishes the register as lamps 91-98 and 101-108.

The manual's single-lamp helmet test starts on the lower-left bulb and steps clockwise with Up. On `bop_l7` it starts on 108 and steps 107 … 101, 98 … 91. Clockwise from the lower-left socket, the bulbs are therefore 108, 107, 106, 105, 104, 103, 102, 101, 98, 97, 96, 95, 94, 93, 92, 91.

The retained table numbers its helmet objects the opposite way round, which only reverses the visible chase direction.

## Service

- **Service tests.** The WPC menu's head tests (motor/switch, head coils, head cycle, and head manual) and the helmet-light test are described on printed pages 1-32 to 1-36. The `bop_l7` menu numbers the head motor/switch, head coil, head cycle, and helmet-light tests T.14, T.15, T.16, and T.17, two places later than the printed T.12-T.15; the ROM lists extra tests ahead of them, including ORDERED LAMP at T.13 (the menu walk did not capture T.12). The head manual test follows later in the ROM menu.
- **Country jumpers.** Country is set with jumpers W14-W18:
  - American: all jumpers In.
  - French: W17 Out.
  - German: W18 Out.

# F-14 Tomcat (Williams, 1987)

This is the physical Williams model 554, IPDB 804, released in 1987 on System 11A. It has four flippers, one jet bumper, two slingshots, twenty standup targets, a spinner, three sensed eject/lock lanes, a vertical launch popper, two launch-ramp diverter gates, two kickbacks and three rotating beacons above the backbox. The record remains **partial** because complete physical socket placement and prototype hardware compatibility are not established. The two remaining coverage requirements are `spatial_placement` and `variant_differences`.

The factory manual, its full-size schematics and service bulletins control physical construction, wiring and population. The retained VPW Mod 1.0 script controls the documented runtime contract. Pinned PinMAME `46e1a01a0f73dc5ce163d98db1ae435883ecc450` controls public addresses and driver/display topology. The IPDB page was independently checked as machine 804; a table header alone was not used to identify it. Source-linked factual transcriptions live under `evidence/excerpts/williams.f-14-tomcat.1987/`.

## Physical revisions and ROMs

PinMAME includes `f14_l1` and prototype revisions `f14_p3`, `f14_p4` and `f14_p5`. All share `init_f14`, the same System 11A routing and display/sound topology. Production L-1 is the ROM selected by the retained VPW release. A common initialization function does not prove that every prototype had the production playfield population: prototype labels, different dome colors, exchanged ROMs and field updates cannot establish that association. Prototype `physical_compatibility` therefore stays `unknown`, with a concrete explanation on each driver.

The six-page preliminary manual (16-549-101 P, 18 March 1987) prints four FL11630 coils, before SS41 changes the upper pair. Its solenoid table still leaves special 19 spare; it does not identify a unique P-3/P-4/P-5 hardware population. The complete preliminary coil table is retained as its own excerpt.

The early material's additional upper slingshot is not fitted to the documented production game. The production solenoid table leaves special 19 spare and the switch list supplies no additional upper-sling scoring input. Do not invent a third production sling from an early illustration.

Service Bulletin SS41, dated 21 April 1987, changes the upper flippers to green-label FL11722 coils and revises the four loop-plastic flashers. Its BEFORE diagram names RED1, BLUE, CLEAR and RED2. RED1 and CLEAR share the orange/white-violet circuit; BLUE has RED and YEL annotations on its left-hand connection and WHT on its right-hand connection; RED2 uses yellow/white-black. The update removes RED1 and RED2 bulb/socket/bracket assemblies, reconnects CLEAR and moves the white-black lead on the nearby resistor board. Apply all of the bulletin, including the resistor-board change; a bulb-count decrement alone is wrong.

The as-printed manual and the retained table lineage represent the earlier population: public 31 drives RED1 plus CLEAR, public 15 drives RED2 plus two backbox lamps, and public 9 drives BLUE plus two backbox lamps. The completed update leaves BLUE and CLEAR on 9, the backbox branch on 15, and no playfield load on 31. A later owner's prototype-style restoration is not an independent factory chart: it can redistribute the white and red domes differently. The definition preserves pre/post update population in the affected device notes and does not silently assign one arrangement to P-3/P-4/P-5.

## Controller contract

`GEN_S11A` aliases `GEN_S11X` numerically (`0x100`); that alias does not turn this physical board into System 11B. The correct controller profile is `pinmame.system-11`. Matrix inputs are sequential column-major 1–64, not decimal column/row notation. Diagnostics are -7 Advance, -6 Auto-Up/Manual-Down, -5 CPU Diagnostic and -4 Sound Diagnostic. DIP 0 is the country jumper.

The game declares `FLIP_SWNO(63,15)`. Consumers drive cabinet button **82 right** and **84 left**. PinMAME copies them to matrix 15 and 63 on every update and overwrites direct writes to those matrix addresses. The manual's physical EOS wording does not describe what a consumer receives there. The real power-winding EOS contacts and the normally open lane-change contacts are separate hardware. No EOS-name conflict is manufactured.

The entire emulator flipper column 81–88 is enumerated. Only 82 and 84 reach this game; its eight-bit matrix strobe cannot read the other column positions. `S11.VBS` supplies the button constants. The library's upper callback constants 34/36 and staged upper switch constants do not create independently addressable F-14 upper coils. The earlier table's direct 15/63 key writes are overwritten; the VPW table also has local fast-flip and staged visuals. These limitations of consumed artifacts are device notes, not disagreements about the physical machine.

Output 14 selects the A/C relay. With it released, public 1–8 are the A-side loads; with it energized, their C-side partners are 25–32. Printed 08C is therefore public 32. The script's comment questioning 32 versus 8 is explained by that transport. Output 24 is reserved and does not select the relay. Outputs 9–16 are controlled circuits; 17–22 are special circuits; 23 is game-on/special-solenoid enable. The game's switch-driven special slots are 57→17, 58→18 and 28→20, gated by 23. The PIA special-line permutation is separately preserved in the source excerpt.

Public 45/46 and 47/48 are paired synthetic right/left flipper states. They do not measure independent winding currents, EOS timing or four CPU-controlled flipper coils. The 33–36 legacy upper range has no ordinary F-14 binary output path. System 11 extension addresses 37–44 are registered, but this game enables neither printer nor sound-overlay writers. The remaining simulator/reserved/custom namespace is enumerated with its source-backed disposition; the machine defines no custom solenoids.

## General illumination and controlled lamps

There is no independent GI 0 callback on this game. Public solenoid 11 controls the GI relay on the backbox power supply. The full schematic's D-8345 K1 contacts are normally closed: energizing the relay opens the GI supply. PinMAME models this as an inverse AC bulb output. Ordinary binary output is raw coil state; physical/modulated mode can instead expose integrated bulb brightness, with a further legacy compatibility reversal. VPW selects physical-output mode and applies brightness in `SetGI`. Reversing that brightness again as though it were raw coil state would be wrong.

The factory lamp matrix covers all 64 controlled addresses. The lamp drawing and boxed-2 legend identify two bulbs each at 24 (both drain lanes) and 39 (both flipper return lanes), giving **66 controlled bulbs**. The six 57–62 lamps are on three playfield launch-ramp signs: blue landing above red lock, ordered top 60/57, middle 61/58, bottom 62/59. They are not backbox lamps. The new VPW table maps `AllLamps` by each member's `TimerInterval`, so neither a light's name nor the collection's total length alone identifies a lamp.

The GI collections are rendering arrangements. `GIbulbs` includes the six matrix-bulb meshes for ambient shading, and `GILighting` includes the virtual center-target light. These do not become extra physical GI sockets. The power diagram uses ellipses and does not enumerate a complete GI population. No complete bulb count is invented from the remaining helper count.

## Ball handling and causal behavior

**Outhole, trough and shooter.** Four balls rest on trough switches 11 (right/front feed), 12 (right-center), 13 (left-center) and 14 (left). The retained script establishes all four occupied at startup. A drain closes outhole 10; output 1 transfers that ball into the gravity trough. Output 2 feeds the front ball into the manual shooter lane, sensed by 16. Ball movement releases and advances the contacts. A coil does not electrically actuate a trough switch: its ball does. An occupied contact that remains after the attempted release is a jam/failure symptom. Virtual delays, kick speeds and ball-conservation helpers remain table tuning.

**Eject/lock lanes.** Right eject 21 is driven by coil 7, left-center 22 by coil 10, and right-center 23 by coil 5. Each sensor records a resting ball. The spring-return eject mechanism releases it toward the playfield when the ROM chooses. These are three separate lock/eject positions, not one multi-position actuator. The manual's right-eject part number differs between its wiring and parts pages; both readings are retained without inventing a physical identity conflict.

**Popper and launch ramp.** A ball in the top-right cup closes 24. Coil 3 lifts it into the overhead rear channel, joining the shooter route downstream of switch 20. Switch 20 detects the right-side shooter ramp entry, so it is not a popper-lift sensor. The plunger returns by spring, and the cup switch clears as the ball leaves. This is a vertical upkicker, with no motor, home or limit switch.

**Two diverters and three branches.** The overhead ramp reaches the left side, then travels toward increasing normalized y. Energized upper gate 21 is encountered first and selects the upper wire ramp, sensor 32, ending at right-center eject 23; gate 22 does not matter in that case. With 21 released and 22 energized, the middle branch, sensor 31, ends at left-center eject 22. With both released, the lower branch, sensor 30, ends at right eject 21. The truth table comes from exact script-bound collision walls and connected ramps, reconciled with the factory layout, and is retained in the derived route excerpt. Branch sensors sense balls rather than gate positions. No gate home switch is invented.

The diverter binding bulletin provides the useful physical home and failure details: adequate plunger insertion into the coil at rest, gates flush with the plastic trough ports, the corrected stop, plastic washer 03-8042 and E-ring 20-8712-18. Insufficient insertion, friction or a gate protruding at the wrong port can prevent clean selection. The parts page prints AE-26-1500-02 where the solenoid table prints AL-26-1500-02; this is a part-number detail, not a different mechanism.

**Kickbacks.** Upper-left Line-of-Death/Yagov input 55 and output 12 return an arriving ball rapidly toward the lower playfield. Left-outlane Rescue input 61 and output 13 return a ball upfield when the rules enable Rescue; otherwise it drains. Their inactive plungers are spring returned. The lane trigger and ball kicker are distinct script objects; no direct electrical sensor relationship is inferred from their proximity.

**Slings and jet.** Scoring switches 57 and 58 belong to the two lower slings, and 28 to the one jet. Their special-solenoid switch slots fire 17, 18 and 20 while game-on enables them. The ROM can also command the relevant PIA lines. Mechanical scoring contacts and assembly trigger contacts should not be confused with a second matrix device.

**Flippers and beacons.** Lower flippers use FL11630 coils, upper flippers FL11722 after SS41. All four are cabinet wired. The beacon assembly C-11604 uses one 14-7946 motor, drive belt 20-9543 and three rotating reflector shafts with #1683/28V bulbs. Output 16 runs the assembly. No home/index sensor appears in the machine's matrix or assembly list; stopping leaves the reached angle, rather than a ROM-commanded home. VPW's aircraft wings, exhaust flames and VR furniture are virtual additions.

## Displays and diagnostics

`s11_dispS11a` exposes four seven-digit glasses: two 16-segment alphanumeric player displays and two numeric displays with comma segments, at controller segment starts 1, 9, 21 and 29. The factory display-board parts table (PDF 39, printed 34) and the table's 28-digit layout agree. These are cabinet/backbox displays with controlled not-applicable playfield spatial records; no extra credit/ball glass is invented.

The complete service tables preserve unused switch positions 17, 18, 19, 27, 29, 39, 40 and 64, and spare coil positions 4A, 8A and 19. An operator test can pulse an unfitted driver; observed activity alone would not fit a coil. Input 9 Playfield Tilt is physically fitted even though the retained table omits it. A fresh-state L-1 ROM Switch Levels probe names PLAYFLD TILT on both level 1 assertions of switch 9 and clears on both releases, with a same-run Plumb Bob control. Combined with the uninverted row read, this proves normally-open construction; host input readback was not used. The compact observation and raw-trace hashes are retained in `evidence/excerpts/williams.f-14-tomcat.1987/switch9-derived-summary.md`.

They are retained and hashed as supplementary evidence, and their coordinates are never mixed into this transform. The new table's extra bulb meshes establish what that lineage modelled; they are not independent physical measurement.

Contact/event anchors and below-playfield coil assemblies are disclosed projections. They stay `observed`, with the exact named gameitem, raw point and file hash in the geometry excerpt. The off-playfield helper flipper pivots used to animate the diverters are not clamped onto the playfield. Ramp-sign placements use individual modeled filaments. No insert overlay, lightmap, reflection, glow helper or primitive origin is presented as a measured physical socket.

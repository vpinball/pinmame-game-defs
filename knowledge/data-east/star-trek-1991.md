# Star Trek (Data East 1991)

Coverage is **partial**. The unit is Data East model 500-5514-01, produced beginning in October 1991. PinMAME's 1992 catalog labels describe the firmware grouping. The physical identity is IPDB 2356 and OPDB G42qQ-MLn3y. [IPDB machine page](https://www.ipdb.org/machine.cgi?id=2356)

## Sources and controller

The 21-page retained operations-manual selection contains the switch parts and location map, lamp matrix and layout, PPB and special-coil diagrams, and major assemblies. The complete IPDB scan adds the F/X adjustment procedure, cabinet and playfield wiring, and board schematics. The portable transcriptions and drawing crops are linked through the definition. [Operations-manual selection](https://www.arcade-museum.com/manuals-pinball/S/Star_Trek_25th_Anniversary_OPS.pdf) · [Full factory manual through Wayback](https://web.archive.org/web/20220705145241id_/https://www.ipdb.org/files/2356/Data_East_1991_Star_Trek_English_Manual_with_schematics.pdf)

All eight drivers use the CPU Rev 3 Data East DMD16 path, one 128 × 16 DMD at controller index 0, and the same sound set. The controller has 64 sequential matrix switches and 64 sequential lamps, negative coin-door STEP and FORWARD/REVERSE inputs, a separate flipper column, and 53 configured solenoid slots. There is no game-specific switch inversion mask or extra matrix column. The factory switch schematics draw the fitted contacts normally open. PinMAME copies left button 84 to matrix 15 and right button 82 to 16. Drive 84 and 82. The physical contacts are shown in the cabinet wiring. PinMAME uses 23 to enable its synthetic flipper states, exposes 45–48, and the VPX uses 46 and 48 through the VPM flipper callbacks. [Pinned PinMAME game declarations](https://github.com/vpinball/pinmame/blob/46e1a01a0f73dc5ce163d98db1ae435883ecc450/src/wpc/degames.c#L544-L633)

The factory CPU Jumper Table specifies J8 installed. It grounds PIA 11B PA7 pin 9 against pull-up RA19-9. PinMAME maps DIP bank 0 bit 0 to that pin. The fresh-state default of public 0 matches the factory jumper position and public 1 models a removed jumper. All seven CPU versions set the entire port to output during boot and periodic reinitialization, masking external PA7. Both 2.01 switch-test runs produced the same diagnostic frames at host values 0 and 1. The alternate jumper position has no documented operator function. The exact 32assassin 0.9 VPX from the local Tables Archive declares the production 2.01 driver. Its embedded script was read along with the playfield objects. The pinned script corpus also has a 3.00 script and a later 2.01 table script. The 0.9 geometry is our retained placement reference. ## Ball handling and mechanisms

- **Trough and launch.** A drain closes 10. Coil 1 moves the ball into the three-ball gravity trough (11 left, 12 middle, 13 right). Coil 2 serves the shooter lane. The ball in that lane closes 14 and the cabinet button is 30. In a clean production-ROM probe, Start with all three trough switches closed pulsed 2 twice. Pressing 30 while 14 was closed pulsed 3. Closing 10 after launching pulsed 1 and elicited a retry when the probe left 10 closed. The host supplied all ball moves. A recreation must move a ball and subsequently update its switches. There is no direct electrical write from the coil to a trough contact.
- **Subway, VUK and up/down ramp.** The right VUK holds the arriving ball at 40 and fires coil 14. The under-field routes connect the left and center holes with the VUK. Factory diagrams identify the upper-left hole's top contact as 28 and its bottom contact as 52. The 2.01 diagnostic reads each closure independently and prints UPPER LEFT HOLE with its own switch number and wire pair. The VPX queues 52 and then immediately queues 28 from its entrance handler. Its 1.2-second timer waits after the 28 pulse before adding the ball to the simulated VUK. The physical switch order and exact passage timing need an instrumented ball pass. Coils 5 and 6 raise and lower the transporter ramp and the upper limit is 39. The raised ramp exposes the shot into the chute. For a ramp that fails to return down, the factory checks the Ramp Up switch and the latch coil bobbin screw. [Switch test evidence](../../evidence/excerpts/data-east.star-trek.1991/upper-left-switch-test.md) · [Service Bulletin 32](https://www.pinwiki.com/wiki/images/f/fa/Sb32.pdf)
- **Upper gate and swinging target.** Output 22 lifts the gate, which the script models through s22. Output 16 switches motor power through a relay. The swinging-target motor drives a cam and stud attached to the target's linkage. Stern's later CSI manual lists the same motor part, 515-5534-00, at 22½ RPM, about 2.67 seconds per shaft revolution. A target hit makes 25, while 29 detects a ball at the central hole. The VPX animates nine collision positions at a nominal 4.8 seconds per full sweep and a separate quarter-second hit deflection. The physical travel, parking angle and swing time remain unmeasured. [Stern CSI motor table](../../evidence/excerpts/data-east.star-trek.1991/motor-specifications.md)
- **Backbox F/X.** Output 13 controls the Q26 relay, which switches 28 VAC to motor 515-5532-00-14 in the Data East schematic. The cam drives a striped grid across interleaved artwork for the crew and the empty room. The grid travels approximately 0.040 inch under spring tension. The top limit 31 marks the crew visible and the bottom limit 32 marks the crew gone about half a revolution later. The factory adjustment closes the top contact in the top 15 degrees and sets the bottom contact at the point where the crew disappears. Only one limit can be closed at once. The Star Trek Transporter F/X row in Stern's later CSI manual lists 500-5421-00 at 24 VAC and 3½ RPM. The two documents give no cross-reference, so the speed of 515-5532-00-14 remains unverified. Check guide clearance and grid friction if the effect binds. Check switch actuation points and fixed-art alignment if the image is faint or diagonal black lines appear. The VPX assigns switch 31 to step 0 and 32 to step 9 as a visual approximation. The backbox mechanism is independent of the ball ramp and VUK. [Service Bulletin 33](https://www.pinwiki.com/wiki/images/e/ea/Sb33.pdf) · [Stern CSI motor table](../../evidence/excerpts/data-east.star-trek.1991/motor-specifications.md)
- **Target banks, flippers and bumpers.** Each four-target bank has four contacts and one reset coil. The left switches count from bottom to top, and the right switches count from top to bottom. Both flippers have one physical 23-1100 coil apiece. The three turbo bumpers and two slings have dedicated switched coils. The pin-level driver permutation and game wiring put them at 17–21, with gate lift at 22. The two contacts on each sling share a matrix address. Their positions project the bore centers in the VPX playfield texture, using an installed right sling and the bare reproduction board to identify the matching four bores. The blades extend above the measured playfield plane. [Sling aperture evidence](../../evidence/excerpts/data-east.star-trek.1991/sling-apertures.md)

## Lamps, illumination and drive branches

- PPB relay 10 selects the A or B side of the eight shared drives. The A coils are public 1–8 and the B flash circuits are public 25–32. Separate flash outputs are 9 and 12. Sticking normally closed relay contacts can fire the knocker or shooter-lane kicker in place of the flashers. CPU 2.00 changes relay control to reduce arcing. Follow the factory device count even where the table combines multiple bulbs into a render effect. The backbox insert panel and rear panel above the playfield are different locations. [Service Bulletin 35](https://www.pinwiki.com/wiki/images/9/9a/Sb35.pdf)
- GI is solenoid 11. PinMAME's DMD16 model types it as the reversed AC relay/bulb output. The script extinguishes the playfield GI collection when 11 is asserted. The manual shows independent wire pairs for the two playfield feeds. The VPX pairs each broad glow helper with a physical-looking mesh bulb. Only shown bulb meshes were placed. The source provides no complete bulb inventory or feed membership.
- PIA2 port B is inverted by the DMD16 driver and exposed at 37–44. Public 37, 38 and 39 carry chase phases 1, 2 and 3. The ROM start probe saw the same transitions at custom 51, 52 and 53. The factory chaser board brings out A/B legs for each phase on CN2. The ROM animation and service paths drive three bits across the seven CPU variants. Setting port B's direction register while its output latch is zero can briefly assert 40–44 through the emulator's inversion. The chaser board has no corresponding inputs. The twelve VPX segment lights per phase represent a rendering effect. Physical socket count and placement remain open. [Chase firmware and wiring evidence](../../evidence/excerpts/data-east.star-trek.1991/chase-rom.md)
- Lamp 50 has two bulbs in the left and right bumpers. The VPX also illuminates the center bumper as a proxy. The factory matrix, wiring and all four display ROM diagnostics identify lamp 36 as Backbox #5. The playfield also has a 36 callout on the drawing and a visual proxy in the script. Whether a second lamp was physically wired to the circuit remains open. In the rear panel, the two outer RAMP lenses (44, 64) are green and the two inner MILLION lenses (45, 63) are red. Their service-test labels and original-machine photographs agree. The matrix prints the colors in reverse. [Rear lamp evidence](../../evidence/excerpts/data-east.star-trek.1991/rear-lamp-diagnostics.md)

## Firmware differences

PinMAME pairs CPU 1.10 and 1.20 with display 1.06 and CPU 1.17, 2.00 and 2.01 with display 1.09. Service Bulletin 31 recommends 1.17 and 1.09 together to address launch, tech alerts and display synchronization. Service Bulletin 35 describes the 2.00 update for display recovery, ball search and the shared coil relay. The factory also records an insulation change to late-production chase light assemblies and subsequent replacement stock. [Service Bulletin 31](https://www.pinwiki.com/wiki/images/b/b4/Sb31.pdf) · [Service Bulletin 35](https://www.pinwiki.com/wiki/images/9/9a/Sb35.pdf) · [Service Bulletin 34](https://www.pinwiki.com/wiki/images/0/09/Sb34.pdf)

The catalog entry labelled Alpha Display uses the same CPU and sound ROMs as 1.10 with the earlier A0.27 display ROM. A clean boot produced an A0.27 startup banner and graphical attract animation through the emulated 520-5042-00 dot-matrix board documented for the factory machine. The retained evidence establishes the display firmware difference and shared display interface. The provenance of the A0.27 dump and which machines shipped with it remain undocumented. [Display firmware comparison](../../evidence/excerpts/data-east.star-trek.1991/display-firmware-comparison.md)

Unofficial 3.00 replaces the CPU and display ROMs on the production machine. It changes scanner awards, ball save, skill shot and multiball among other rules. Version 3.01 retains the 3.00 display ROM and changes the CPU program. It adds simultaneous-flipper launch, changes the top-gate behavior for the kickback feature, and scores multiball jackpots on the top-left hole switches before the ball arrives at the VUK. The physical wiring stays applicable. [Firmware author's release notes](https://www.pinballcode.com/trek300)

# The Who's Tommy Pinball Wizard (Data East, 1994)

Coverage: **partial - manual- and ROM-verified I/O for the full 8x8 switch and lamp matrices with connector, wire-colour and drive-transistor wiring, every public solenoid address with the ROM's own coil-test name, switch polarity for all 55 used matrix switches, the Black service button and both flipper buttons, the blinder's PinMAME addresses, fourteen source-reconciled mechanisms, and 141 normalized playfield placements from the one retained recreation; held below author-ready by four open requirements: the W7 jumper's meaning, the purpose of CN3 data lines 37-43, the Green FORWARD/REVERSE button's contact polarity, and placements that have only the one recreation behind them (the general illumination is not placed at all)**

## Identity

Data East The Who's Tommy Pinball Wizard, manufactured from 8 January 1994 (IPDB #2579, model 500-5528-01, 4,700 units confirmed), on Data East/Sega "Version 3" CPU hardware with a 128 x 32 dot-matrix display (`GEN_DEDMD32`, `de_128x32DMD`) and the DE2S sound board. PinMAME roots the family at `tomy_400` with 7 drivers, every one sharing `init_tomy` and therefore one `tommyGameData`, so all of them present identical playfield hardware: 1.02, 1.02 Belgian, 2.01 Dutch, 3.00 Dutch, 3.01 German, 4.00 and the 2016 community MOD 5.00. They differ in the CPU and display ROM images; the five sound ROMs are identical across the family. The MOD runs on the same cabinet and stays a driver variant of the physical machine.

IPDB lists three flippers, three pop bumpers and six-ball multiball, "flipper blinders [that] extend from under metal apron to cover flipper area from player's view", unlimited buy-in balls, and two toys, Captain Walker's airplane and a raising/lowering mirror. It also records ten pre-production prototypes with six pop bumpers, one with its upper left flipper mounted higher; this definition describes the production machine.

## What the evidence is, and what it is not

The manual is the IPDB-hosted scan of Data East Pinball, Inc.'s operations manual: 117 image-only pages at 300 dpi, read from native-resolution renders (printed page = PDF page minus four in the diagnostics section). The committed excerpts transcribe the switch and lamp matrices with their wire, connector and transistor headers, the complete switch part-number and lamp location tables, the coil and flash-lamp table, the Flipper Solenoids table, the coil chart schematic's per-drive text, the servo interface board's connector, the major assemblies, the bulb table, the cabinet parts that matter, the motor circuits of the playfield wiring diagram, the instruction card and the Mirror, Arch Motor and Pinball Servo Controller text. IPDB also hosts a preliminary manual (retained, not used for any claim) and the instruction card. No factory addendum is retained.

The retained known-working table is the VPW Mod 1.2.1 of ninuzzu's recreation. **It runs `tomy_500`, the unofficial 5.00 MOD**, with `tomy_400` kept as a commented alternative, so its callbacks are evidence about the MOD's use of the factory map. They agree with the factory manual drive for drive, which is why the table is admitted as runtime evidence.

PinMAME is the pinned 97aa922 checkout. The ROM evidence is ten fresh-state LibPinMAME runs of US 4.00 (`tomy_400`), driven only by direct public switch writes and reached by display-title checkpoints; the raw runs and every 128 x 32 frame stay under the working root with a canonical manifest, and the frames the definition rests on are read and recorded beside their pixel digests in `evidence/runtime/data-east/the-who-s-tommy-pinball-wizard-tomy_400-service-tests.json`. The ROM's diagnostics run: DIAGNOSTICS, service call, SERVICE CREDITS, PLAYFIELD STATUS, EASY TROUGH CLEAR, BURN IN TIME, SPEAKER TEST, a DMD pattern, the MIRROR test, ARCH TEST, SWITCH TEST, ACTIVE SWITCH TEST, ALL/ROW/COLUMN LAMPS ON, LAMP TEST, CYCLING FLASHERS, CYCLING COILS, COIL TEST and DISPLAY VERSION (`TOMMY A4.00`).

## The address model

Data East runs on the shared Williams System 11 core (`s11.c`). Both printed matrices are **column-major, address = (column - 1) x 8 + row**, which is the number printed in each cell. Column 1 of the switch matrix is the cabinet column (plumb tilt, 4th coin, credit button, three coin switches, slam tilt, extra-ball button).

- **Switches.** Nine positions are not used: 44-46, 52-54 and 59-61. The two Enter Trough switches, Mirror Trough 41 and Skill Trough 42, are under the playfield. Mirror Up 28 and Mirror Down 31 are the mirror motor's limit switches and Mirror Target 32 is the mirror itself.
- **Lamps.** All 64 positions are populated. Lamps 1, 10, 19, 28 and 37 are the backbox insert's TOMMY letters, two bulbs each; 41-45 are the same letters on the playfield. Lamps 23 and 64 light the Extra Ball and Credit (Start) buttons. 46 (Outlanes X2) and 55 (Return Lanes X2) are two bulbs each. 39, 40, 60 and 61 are on the ramps and 56 is on the airplane. The chart prints the cell number 41 in lamp 42's cell, a misprint the ROM's own test confirms (`T(O)MMY #42`).
- **There is no GI channel.** General illumination is **public solenoid 11**, the G.I. relay on the power-supply board, and `s11.c` types it as a reversed 6.3 VAC #44 output for this machine, so a consumer shows the GI lit while solenoid 11 is off.
- **Public solenoid 10 is the Left/Right relay.** It re-routes drives 1-8 from the coils (left set, public 1-8) to flash lamps (right set); pinned PinMAME publishes the right set at **25-32** (`muxSol = 10`). The ROM's Coil Test turns 10 on before every R entry and has no entry of its own for 10.
- **Unfitted drivers.** 7L (public 7), 9, 16 and 22 carry no load: the coil table prints them Not Used, the cabinet parts list says the shaker motor is "Not Used in this Game" (drive 9's PPB driver and the Shaker Motor Board are still fitted; the board feeds the airplane motors), and the ROM's Coil Test names all four NOT USED. It still pulses 7 and 9 as it sweeps the drivers, and fires nothing for 16 and 22.
- **Direct drives.** 12 is the top diverter, which the ROM holds on rather than pulsing; 13 runs the two airplane motors; 14 is the mirror motor relay (a Relay Board 520-5010-00 switching 28 VAC to the motor; the Mirror test text says Q23, the coil table and drawings Q25 - a transistor-designator disagreement, not a device one); 15 is the 'Tommy' flash lamp group, one bulb at the mirror and three in the insert.
- **Solenoids 17-21 are switched solenoids:** the three pop bumpers and two slingshots. The printed coil numbers and PinMAME's public addresses are different identities in principle (Data East's PIA permutation is not sequential), and the ROM's Coil Test settles the pairing: public 17, 18 and 19 are `COIL: LEFT POP BUMPER`, `CENTER POP BUMPER` and `RIGHT POP BUMPER`, 20 and 21 the `LEFT SLINGSHOT` and `RIGHT SLINGSHOT`.
- **Solenoid 23 is game-on** (`S11_GAMEONSOL`), 24 is empty, 33-36 are inert on this generation, 45-48 are PinMAME's synthetic lower-flipper power and hold bits, 49 is a simulator slot and 50 is reserved.
- **37-44 and 51: the blinder.** `tommyGameData` sets `S11_PRINTERLINE`, so `s11.c` publishes PIA2 port B (the CN3 printer data lines) at 37-44, and its comment names bit 7, CN3 pin 1, "Blinder on Tommy". `tommyGameData` also declares one custom solenoid whose handler returns `core_getSol(44) > 0`, and the pinned core arithmetic puts the first custom solenoid at **51** (the driver's comment says 33, which it is not). **Public 44 and 51 both carry the blinder's commanded position**; only these two ever moved in the retained runs. Lines 37-43 never moved, not even when Start was pressed on Adjustment 56 PRINTER INTERFACE, so their purpose stays open.
- **Three flippers, two buttons.** The left button works the lower and the upper left flipper (a double left flipper switch); the right button works the lower right flipper. PinMAME declares `FLIP6364 = FLIP_SWNO(63,64)` with no `FLIP_SOL`: `core_updateSw` rewrites matrix switches 63 and 64 on every update from PinMAME's own flipper column, so **a consumer drives 84 (left button) and 82 (right button), never 63 or 64**, and the same bits synthesise 47/48 and 45/46 while the switched-solenoid enable is on. The ROM calls 63 and 64 `LEFT END OF STROKE` and `RIGHT END OF STROKE`, but no end-of-stroke state is modelled. The manual's Flipper Solenoids table prints the third row `Left Flipper Upr.` with wires copied from a right-flipper row; nothing in PinMAME represents the upper flipper separately.
- **Service buttons** sit in switch column 0: -7 is the Black STEP button, -6 the Green FORWARD/REVERSE button. Public 1 is the depressed STEP button and it rests open; the FORWARD/REVERSE button's contact polarity is not established.

## What the ROM's own service tests say

- **Switches.** With every public switch at 0 the Active Switch Test shows NONE; holding any public address 1-64 shows its name, the manual's column and row wires (the ROM spells ORN as ORG) and its number, so no switch is closed at rest and the 55 used matrix switches are `normally_closed: false`. The ROM calls the pop bumpers POP BUMPER where the manual says Turbo Bumper. 82 shows `RIGHT END OF STROKE #64`, 84 `LEFT END OF STROKE #63`, and 81, 83 and 85-88 show NONE.
- **Lamps.** The single-lamp test names all 64 lamps with the manual's wires, one per short Start press (a longer press can step twice, so a lamp lit only between two snapshots is missed rather than skipped); the Row and Column tests light each row and column. The ROM agrees with the chart that lamp 22 is the right ramp's stand-up (`RT RAMP S-U`) where the location table prints Left Ramp Stand-Up Top.
- **Coils.** The Coil Test selects an entry with the flipper buttons and fires it with Start, naming each drive with its wires. The flash entries spell out the bulb split: 1R `ARCH LT/RT X4`, 2R `UPPER RIGHT X4`, 3R `INS X2 SCOOP X2`, 4R `INS X2 EJECT X2`, 5R `POP BUMPERS X4`, 6R `BACK PANEL X4`, 7R `INS X2 C-BALL X2`, 8R `UPPER LEFT X4` and 15 `INS X3 MIRROR X1`. Some differ from the manual's table names (4R Upper Right, 5R Turbo Hot Dog, 7R Lower Right Hot Dog, 8R Hot Dogs); the bulb counts agree.
- **Kickers.** In the Mirror and Arch tests, closing 16, 19, 23 or 47 fires 3 (auto launch), 4 (VUK), 5 (left scoop) or 6 (eject).

## Mechanisms and recreation guidance

- **Trough.** Six balls rest on 9-14; the lockout (1) feeds one to the release position 15 and the ball eject (2) kicks it into the shooter lane (16). After Start the ROM pulses the lockout about every 1.2 s until 15 closes, then pulses the eject until 15 opens.
- **Launch.** A manual shooter and an auto launch (3, 22-600 on +50 V) share the shooter lane; the ROM fired 3 when 16 closed in the service tests but left the served ball on 16 in a game.
- **Mirror.** A motor and cam raise and lower the mirror target (32) between two limit switches, Mirror Up 28 and Mirror Down 31, which close just before each limit and are never both closed. Lowering it opens the Mirror Trough (41) to the VUK (19, kicked by 4). The motor runs only while 14 is on and the ROM stops it on a limit switch; with neither limit closed the ROM ran it from power-up until Start. The retained table instead finishes a whole stroke once 14 turns on at a limit; a recreation should move the mirror only while 14 is on. Spell T-O-M-M-Y on the mirror, then enter it for multiball.
- **Blinder.** A radio-control servo on the Pinball Servo Controller board swings two blades out from under the arch over the lower flippers. It has two commanded positions (each set by a pot); the servo positions itself by its own feedback, and nothing reports its position to the CPU. Drive it from 44 or 51: on is out over the flippers, off is folded under the arch. It cycles once at power-up.
- **Airplane.** Two motors on 13 turn the airplane's propellers; lamp 56 is on the airplane.
- **Top diverter** (12) is held on while active. **Pop bumpers** 17-19 with skirts 49-51; **slingshots** 20-21 with 17-18. **Stand-ups:** left and right 3-banks (25-27, 33-35), ramp stand-ups 20-22, middle stand-up 38, silver ball target 24. **Captive ball** target 43. **Spinners** 39 and 40.

Mechanism speeds, travel and servo angles are table-owned tuning, not machine data.

## Spatial placement

141 placements come from the one retained recreation, chosen by what its script binds; all stay `observed`. The mirror's limit switches are projected onto the mirror; the propellers, blinder blades and flasher domes use the world-space centre of the table's meshes; the back-panel domes of 6R are on the rear wall. The GI is not placed: the table's GI grouping is not the machine's wiring and no source states which bulbs are GI. The spatial report lists every omitted device.

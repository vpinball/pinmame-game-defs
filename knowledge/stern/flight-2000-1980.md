# Flight 2000 (Stern, 1980)

**Coverage is partial.** The switch matrix, MPU options, driver differences, walker behavior, coil decoder and six display fields are documented. The spatial report records measured and projected positions and lists the remaining lamp and mechanism evidence gaps.

## Machine and controller

Stern Electronics model 128 is a four-player widebody with MPU-200, an LDA-100 feature lamp driver, a solenoid driver/regulator, C-605 sound module and VSU-100 voice synthesis. Its August 1980 identity is IPDB 887 and OPDB `G48kY-MDxRV`. PinMAME's `dispst7` exposes four seven-digit player fields and two two-digit credit and ball/match fields. The blank video/DMD callback seen in the harness has no corresponding display in the factory backbox inventory. [IPDB machine 887](https://www.ipdb.org/machine.cgi?id=887)

Drive matrix switches 1–40 with active-high closures. The pinned BY35 matrix read passes the closed-contact level through; its Stern-generation byte or register handling does not invert the public state of these contacts. The two cabinet buttons use public 82 and 84. The physical normally closed EOS contacts are separately wired at the flipper assemblies. While the game-on relay is enabled, PinMAME asserts both outputs in each virtual power/hold pair (45/46 right, 47/48 left) together. A recreation must model physical winding current and EOS timing separately if it needs them. The voice board shares PIA and sound-board control paths. The printed sound test identifiers 20–29 are separate from the public solenoid range. [Pinned Flight 2000 driver and layout](https://github.com/vpinball/pinmame/blob/46e1a01a0f73dc5ce163d98db1ae435883ecc450/src/wpc/stgames.c)

The manual's solenoid test prints the transistor `Q` number. Public 9 drives Q13, the ball launcher, public 10 drives Q14, the outhole, and public 19 drives Q15, the flipper-enable relay. In the occupied-trough service run the settled player-one digits matched the factory Q1–Q19 order. The switch test displayed a held trough contact through the later 39 and 40 stimuli. A separate gameplay trace paired a closure of 39 with a walker pulse. [Service trace summary](../../evidence/runtime/stern/flight-2000-service.json) · [Factory coil chart](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/solenoid-identification.md) · [Gameplay observations](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/walker-recreation.md)

DIP 23 is bank 3 bit 6. In `flight2k` and `flightfp` the CPU saves the bank at `$0028` and checks the whole four-bank value for all-off before entering the automatic boot test. Turning on DIP 23 alone bypassed that test in both builds. The factory option list and the ROM review give DIP 23 no individual gameplay assignment in those builds. The Idleman variants use it to select the center-Special award. [6800 dataflow and controlled comparison](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/dip23-stock.md)

## Recreation

- The left five-target bank has a common reset and five individual trip coils. Use switch addresses 28–32 for the leaves, public 7 for the reset and 13, 14, 12, 11, 8 for individual trips numbered 1–5. The ROM spots lit countdown targets and tracks the ordered 5-to-1 hits. The simpler right three-target bank uses switches 25–27 and reset 15. All three down advances multiplier and spinner value. [Factory operation and parts](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/factory-operation.md)
- The B circuit has three physical contacts on public 17. The left outer return rollover and the two rear stand-ups score the same shared switch. The exact VPX right-hand stand-up object is named `sw17a1`; the script calls its second stand-up handler `sw17c`. Preserve the factory wiring in a recreation even if a VPX object needs its event binding corrected. The top four rollovers spot L-A-S-T. Lower return lanes spot B, O and both F letters. [Factory switch chart](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/switch-identification.md)
- The B-758 walker uses one coil at public 9 to retract a common bar and move the pivoted fingers. The return spring restores the resting arrangement. A new ball depresses 39 as it enters, then settles past the wireform against the first finger so the contact can reopen. Two balls can park between fingers without keeping 39 closed. A qualified third arrival initiates release through the left kickout, right kickout and rear arch. Use switches 15 and 16 and outputs 20 and 17 at those kickouts. The dedicated top rollover on 40 releases the next rocket after BLASTOFF and countdown qualification per the factory rules. [Factory operation](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/factory-operation.md) · [Walker construction and stock I/O](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/walker-recreation.md)
- Keep the entry finger fully upright after a stroke. A bowed bar on one restoration trapped the finger halfway and let the next ball overshoot and kick back down the entrance. A verified contact must clear the parked ball for the next arrival to register. In two stock-ROM arrival probes, 9 rose about a quarter second after 39 and stayed on about 1.2 s while 39 was held. The exact bord VPX substitutes three kickers and releases all three stop walls together. Use the contact transitions and mechanical ball path when recreating the linkage. [Restoration and timing notes](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/walker-recreation.md) · [VPX contract](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/vpx-contract.md)
- The trough contacts 33, 34 and 35 report occupancy under the apron. The stock gameplay probe saw no recovery during its occupied boot wait. With 35 open, a separate 65-second wait without coin or Start observed repeated right kickout, left kickout and walker commands. In play, 10 serves one ball into the shooter lane. Route the next ball into position and update the actual contact states as it moves. Surviving games show the kicker above and below the playfield wood. Both mountings serve at the same outlet. The two documented serials establish examples of each mounting. [Boot comparison](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/walker-recreation.md) · [Early serve photo](https://www.ipdb.org/showpic.pl?id=887&picno=49153) · [Later serve photo](https://www.ipdb.org/showpic.pl?id=887&picno=49151)
- Feature lamps are on four decoder banks of 15 channels. Match the published address to the SCR before reading the load chart. Shoot Again at public 11 has both a playfield socket and a backbox branch, listed at J2-21. The board drawing appears to print J2-2 on both Q3 and Q31. Public 47 drives Q31 during burn-in and lamp test. The combined named-function crosswalk supports no fitted lamp on Q31 despite the remaining pin uncertainties. The specimen photos corroborate the general connector population. [Lamp crosswalk](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/lamp-followup.md)
- The playfield GI is a transformer-fed 6 VAC circuit. Three rear socket positions between L, A, S and T come from registered holes in an original playfield and corresponding lit fixtures on its rebuild. The full stock GI count, remaining socket positions and per-branch membership are still open. [Rear GI measurements](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/rear-gi.md)

## Driver variants

- `flight2k` is the production set with stock U9 speech. `flightfp` replaces U5 and U6 for always-free play and retains stock U1, U2 and U9. The all-off boot test is common to both sets. [6800 comparison](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/dip23-stock.md) · [Pinned driver declarations](https://github.com/vpinball/pinmame/blob/46e1a01a0f73dc5ce163d98db1ae435883ecc450/src/wpc/stgames.c#L922-L962)
- `flight2m` is Idleman's 3335 build with stock U9 speech, DIP 19 free play, DIP 18 countdown reset and DIP 23 center-Special extra ball. It adds a LAST skill shot, Quick Shot, a 19,000 bonus ladder, spinner counts and revised multiball scoring. The first coin-door self-test press goes to lamps. The author confirmed that 3335 cycles through closed switches with a zero separator. The documented replay-knocker configuration requires moving the former coin-lockout lead at A3J2-8 (public 18) to the knocker. The walker remains on A3J5-12 (public 9). The author documents DIP 14 routing selection from subversion 39 onward. [Release documentation](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/idleman-variants.md)
- `flight2mb` is 3347M, with revised speech by Idleman and Pure Penalty and new tilt warning, high-score replay, maximum-credit and multiball difficulty settings 19–22. DIP 17 now enables attract speech. Compatibility requires revised speech and harness wiring that matches DIP 14. ON means walker at A3J5-12 (public 9), with the former coin-lockout lead at A3J2-8 (public 18) moved to the knocker if fitted. OFF expects the swapped harness, with walker on 18 and knocker on 9. A mismatch can damage the driver board. PinMAME loads two 2716 speech images at U9 and U10. The author also documents a single 2532 in U9 with U10 empty for physical hardware. [Release documentation](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/idleman-variants.md) · [VSU-100 ROM positions](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/vsu-rom-sockets.md)

The two modified variants are documented from the authors' release notes and pinned PinMAME registrations. Retained runtime traces cover `flight2k` and `flightfp`. [Variant evidence](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/idleman-variants.md)

PinMAME also makes `gamatron` a clone of `flight2k`, but its ROM drives the separate 1985 Pinstar conversion. The conversion has its own playfield, lacks Flight 2000's speech board, and keeps its own [machine record](../../machines/partial/pinstar/gamatron-1985.json). [Pinstar Gamatron, IPDB 984](https://www.ipdb.org/machine.cgi?id=984)

Status: validated and promoted to `machines/author-ready/bally/kiss-1979.json`.

The matching source is the retained known-working `Kiss (Bally 1979) v2.0.0.vpx` at SHA-256
`c46c74d640d95672acc02a70e5c2675493f9ce522f53d2f77f919f6beec32b5e`. A fresh `vpxtool git:v0.33.3`
extraction produced 2605 files totalling 313060497 bytes, manifest SHA-256
`50488710d4777ca101649652ea380241d3dd890be2735ff976281764e1e54ba7`. `vpxtool` established exact playfield bounds `left=0 top=0 right=952.941
bottom=1976.471`, and every canonical coordinate is x/952.941 and y/1976.471 rounded to at most six
fractional places. 99 placements are recorded.

## Evidence decisions

- The Bally game #1152-E manual is the physical inventory, wiring and connector-function authority;
  the ROM's own solenoid self test captured by the pinned LibPinMAME harness owns the
  printed-to-public solenoid mapping; pinned PinMAME owns controller topology; and the retained
  table supplies geometry only.
- The retained table runs `kisse`, a seven-digit conversion that PinMAME models with `lampCol = 0`.
  It therefore has no auxiliary lamp bindings at all, which is why the twelve A9 circuits are
  identified from the board schematic and the lamp chart rather than from the table.
- The lamp chain is derived rather than read off one page. `public = 16*k + lampadr + 1` follows from
  `by35_lampStrobe`; decoder output to SCR is AS-2518-23 board wiring and therefore game-independent;
  and Kiss's own schematic and plug-connector sheet supply the connector endpoint and the function
  printed against it.
- An SCR does not reach exactly one connector pin. The board fans several out to more than one
  branch and each game's harness plugs a different one, so endpoints are read from Kiss's own
  schematic. An earlier pass reused another machine's single-endpoint list, lost eight lamps, and
  had to be corrected.
- The split is 46 playfield-only, 7 that light both a backglass socket and a playfield insert, 18
  back box-only including the twelve auxiliary circuits, 1 unused and 4 unpopulated, totalling 76 =
  60 main board plus 16 auxiliary. That is 53 addresses feeding playfield lamps, and the retained
  table models exactly 53 lamp objects - the independent check that the derivation is right.
- A5J2 is marked TO BACKBOX, but Table B on the connector sheet routes seven of its pins onward
  through the panel-to-back-cab plug to playfield inserts. Those seven are placed at the insert and
  declare two bulbs against one coordinate; the backglass socket has no coordinate of its own.
- The twelve auxiliary circuits are the K-I-S-S backglass letter animation and are likewise
  `cabinet_or_service`. Their board wiring is primary; the function printed against each A9J2 pin is
  from the Bally Kiss lamp chart, a secondary source disclosed in every affected device note.
- `Q3` is marked N/U on all four MC14555B halves, so latched address 3 - public 68, 84, 100 and 116 -
  reaches no bulb. The retained runtime trace corroborates it: those four latch on early and never
  change again while the twelve fitted circuits switch throughout a game.
- Only A5J2-16 is marked N/U, so public 14 alone drives nothing. Public 43's SCR Q40 also reaches
  A5J3-22, "Same Player Shoots Again", on the block marked TO PLAYFIELD, so it is a real lamp.
- The displays are backbox hardware and take controlled `not_applicable` records with both PinMAME
  core and manual provenance.

## Explicit projections

- Solenoid 14, Drop Target Reset: the reset coil is mounted beneath the four-target bank rather than on the playfield surface, so it is projected onto the mean of the four drop target positions. This is a projection onto the mechanism the coil drives, not a centroid of unrelated devices, and it does not land on top of another placed device.
- Solenoid 7, Outhole Kicker: taken from the retained BallRelease kicker, which is the outhole the coil fires from. Switch 8 shares that location because on a 1979 single-ball outhole the switch and the kicker are the same place.
- Switches 35 and 36, the slingshots: taken from the drag-point centroid of the RightSlingshot and LeftSlingshot wall objects, because a slingshot switch is the wall itself rather than a point object.

## Unresolved

None. Every enumerated address is either placed, or carries a controlled not-applicable record whose
reason the evidence supports.

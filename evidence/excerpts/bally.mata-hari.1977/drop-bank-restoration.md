# Mata Hari production drop-bank construction

J. Charlton's first-person December 8, 2014 [Mata Hari Rejuvenation Project](https://pinballprojects.wordpress.com/2014/12/08/drop-target-assemblies/) follows a Bally solid-state machine through removal, teardown, rebuilding and installation. The part geometry and coil wiring match the right and left four-target assemblies AS-2795-44 and AS-2795-45 in FO 579 Figure II. The restorer replaced the previously fitted tab-top faces with reproduction tombstones and reused the Bally assemblies.

| Source frame | Direct observation |
| --- | --- |
| `img_93671.jpg`, before work | One side-mounted Bally coil, four springs and four independent target arms on one transverse pivot. |
| `img_93931.jpg`, rebuilt assembly | One side-mounted NO-26-1900 reset coil and one plunger act on the shared transverse reset arm. |
| `img_94091.jpg`, both installed | Mirrored housings carry one coil at each outer end, with four separate leaf contacts on each bank. |

In its up position each target is pulled forward against its catch. A ball pushes the face back far enough to unhook it and the spring pulls it down. The target stays down and its contact stays closed until the coil lifts the reset arm. A single reset raises all four positions. The original NO-26-1900 markings are visible in the photographs. The one-per-bank construction agrees with FO 579's quantity of two for the game. W-1192-1c prints two symbols per reset branch. That drawing determines each branch's electrical connection. Vic Camp's production underside image also shows one reset coil on each bank. The [fixture register](fixture-photo-register.md) records coarse actuator projections from that separate photograph and the Bally catalog.

The article and original image bytes are retained in `manuals/by-machine/bally.mata-hari.1977/charlton-2014/` and the session evidence folder's `charlton/` directory. Exact source URLs and digests are in the definition and the retained manifest.

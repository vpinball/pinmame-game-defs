The retained HTML has SHA-256 `7bd62ee30b8c60a38ba6587e868fa02c5883a5495be61298d382f51105e46f92`. The following original 600 × 450 JPEGs were visually inspected; the originals and HTML are retained under `review-artifacts/data-east-star-trek-1991/followup/restoration/`. The owner is the photograph author; rights are not asserted and the images are not redistributed here. Retrieved 2026-11-20. The server's expired TLS certificate required `curl.exe -k` to acquire the originals.

The factory manual's PDF page 29 names switches 11, 12 and 13 left, center and right respectively. Its diagram marks the three labels in the trough region without individual switch symbols or leader endpoints. The retained VPX's `bsTrough.InitSw 10,13,12,11,0,0,0,0` is a simulated ball-stack ordering; its invisible `BallRelease` and `Drain` kickers are ball-routing objects, not measured trough-contact positions. The extraction contains no named `BallSwitch` object. Neither the photo alone nor this script identifies the physical contact centre under each ball.

The factory manual's PDF page 41 bottom drawing is the trough **eject** or ball-feed assembly: it draws the eject cam, spring, link, coil and bracket, but does not draw the three trough position switches or the complete ball rail. It therefore cannot place those contacts relative to the eject cam. PDF page 37 shows a trough outline in the whole-playfield parts drawing, but no individual trough switches. Neither drawing supplies the missing individual contact centres.

## Photo-to-table fit and its limits

The occupied 600 × 450 original was compared with the retained VPX extraction's `images/Playfield.png`, SHA-256 `225e94db4553e56a847fccf33a3c7826b37ba74358bde1b9c1c27b1ac1d175d9`, 1992 × 4295 pixels. Its `gamedata.json` selects this playfield image and has bounds left 0, right 952, top 0, bottom 2162; texture pixels are divided by 1992 and 4295 to report the table's normalized playfield x/y. Coordinates in the photographs are image pixels from the upper left, not VPX units.

A reproducible exploratory projective fit uses SIFT art features from the portrait, bonus-insert and surrounding artwork in the occupied photograph and texture rows 2800–4199. OpenCV 4.12.0 SIFT (`nfeatures=8000`, `contrastThreshold=.014`) and a .72 nearest-neighbor ratio produced 85 candidate matches; a 10-texture-pixel RANSAC fit kept 73, and deduplication within two photograph pixels retained 65. Their exact pixel pairs are in retained `review-artifacts/data-east-star-trek-1991/followup/photo-fit-analysis/art-controls.json`, SHA-256 `04262f8991721735f6e3b96be691a74b3125afed39d43bc3814e4066a5425388`. `photo-fit-analysis/measure.py`, SHA-256 `7c86392ff9807710d9ae551db1e7b038c9719d542561a720f2f425a1a20e166b`, recomputes the controls, fits and checks. The accepted control coordinates span photo x 143.6–411.0, y 2.9–191.2 and texture x 322.6–1384.5, y 2961.9–3806.5. These are automatically proposed artwork matches, not individually observed hardware locations.

Refitting all 65 pairs gives mean/max normalized control residual .00079/.00279; leave-one-out median/max .00061/.00294. The apparent centers of the three occupied balls, approximately (437,222), (464,217), (492,213) in photo pixels, project to about (.782,.898), (.825,.887), (.871,.878), respectively. Every ball is outside the controls' convex hull: by approximately 53, 74 and 99 photo pixels. Individual-control leave-one-out moves those predictions by at most .0012, .0014 and .0016 normalized, but this tests removal within the same cluster and does not measure extrapolation error. The balls are three-dimensional, partially obscured by their trough, and do not reveal the height of the sensor contact plane; projecting their image centers onto that plane introduces additional unknown parallax. The manual's left/center/right names make their order a useful lead, not proof of the hidden blade positions.

Four independently read corners of the physical red apron artwork were checked in order left tip, lower left, lower right, right tip: photo (154,143), (201,206), (281,228), (374,203), versus VPX texture (360,3848), (500,4068), (1265,4076), (1405,3822). The art-feature homography predicts texture (400,3829), (695,4034), (1020,4035), (1320,3846); their normalized residuals are .021, .098, .123 and .043. A four-corner fit alone projects the three ball x positions to approximately .900, .897 and .902, collapsing their apparent separation. The VPX texture visibly enlarges and reshapes the red panel compared with the physical photographs, so those corners are not valid common-plane controls. The tight feature residuals therefore cannot establish the physical-to-VPX mapping in the untextured trough area.

As a physical cross-check, the independent bare-board image `followup/de-startrek00.jpg` (1102 × 1800, SHA-256 `8bf6e5a3c0559b11ecb630a303f4e89d054b38bae06c91444a8154222f10bc39`) was registered to the occupied photograph by their matching artwork; the warped opening overlays the visible trough and its balls. This checks the physical aperture and the two photos' identity, but the VPX texture has no corresponding visible aperture. It does not independently validate a target-space coordinate. No photo-derived normalized switch positions are asserted. A detailed photograph of the contact actuators and identifiable wiring, paired with independent target-space controls around the trough, would resolve the physical contact locations and their relation to the table.

The factory assembly drawing (manual PDF page 41) confirms two switches per slingshot but is an isolated assembly view, not a measured playfield placement.

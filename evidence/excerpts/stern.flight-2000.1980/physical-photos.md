# Flight 2000 photographic checks

IPDB machine 887 identifies Stern Electronics Flight 2000, model 128, August 1980. The playable stock machine is a widebody with two flippers, two pop bumpers, four slings, a three-bank, a five-target memory bank, a walker and two staged kickouts. The IPDB photos, retained outside Git, show different parts of the hardware:

- Kevin Wayte, image 15827. The stripped upper playfield shows the two bumper mounts, three-bank, metal lock channels, rear rollover bank and shared-B stand-up locations.
- Kevin Wayte, images 15841 and 15842. Oblique views show the captive walker channel with successive ball positions, the first and second kickout channels and the elevated guides over the lock. They confirm visible apertures and assembly regions. They do not expose the count or wiring of every concealed socket and switch.
- Mike Long, image 49153. Serial 598 has a blue serve solenoid on the player-side surface below the apron, acting directly on the launch guide.
- Image 49151. The serial 6324 top view has no exposed above-board serve solenoid in the equivalent area. IPDB records the associated below-playfield embodiment.
- Mike Long, images 49154–49156. The underside shows conventional bulb holders, the left bank structure and the dual-winding actuators below the flipper pivots. Oblique angles and harness crossings preclude a complete orthographic GI or conductor trace.

The dates of the factory serve change and any serial boundary remain undocumented. Both embodiments are represented by one stock physical game record. The shared coordinate is the VPX serve effect at the exit to the shooter lane.

# Skateball — observed replacement-board lane orientation

Source: retained `photo-026.jpg`, SHA-256 `8703e7d2fee918fb5838ccc7fd36a0b1a9563d019c0c4e984bce390049032a40`, Pinside image `IMG_4432 (resized).jpeg` from the Bally Skateball playfield-swap thread. Original acquisition time and direct image URL are retained in `review-artifacts/skateball-1980/photos/download-manifest.json`. The primary curator visually inspected the complete retained photograph.

The boxed, unassembled physical replacement playfield clearly labels the three rear rollover inserts **1 at left, 2 in the middle and 3 at right**, viewed from the player's end. This is an observed orientation on a replacement reproduction. It does **not independently authenticate the original 1980 artwork or wiring**. The old-board photograph `photo-022.jpg` supports broad geometry but its lane numerals are not reliably legible.

The factory manual's complete switch table assigns public 13 to lane #3, 14 to lane #2 and 15 to lane #1; it does not assign those numbers to sides. The redrawn artwork `images/pf.png` in the retained JPSalas table agrees with the replacement board's numerical orientation. The same table's controller binding and render-light layout instead pair public switch 13 with lamp 24 at the left lane, and switch 15 with lamp 56 at the right. This pairing is internally consistent in the table and differs from its artwork and the observed replacement board.

The manual's Figure V (PDF 22, printed 18) unambiguously draws the three rollover circles **left-to-right 15, 14, 13**. Combined with the PDF 21 names, that corroborates the relative order **#1, #2, #3**. Its upper bank and lane geometry differs from the retained table and old-board photograph, so the drawing is not fitted or used to validate absolute placement coordinates or as-built artwork. The statement that no sides are assigned applies to the self-test table, not to this relative order on Figure V.

Independent reads of the exact extraction: `sw13_Hit` writes switch 13 and `sw15_Hit` writes switch 15; `NFadeLm 24, l24` drives `Light.l24` (center x 481.5, y 123.583885) on the left, and `NFadeLm 56, l56` drives `Light.l56` (center x 685.6491, y 168.37616) on the right. The factory lamp wiring names 24 lane #3 and 56 lane #1. These lamp centers describe render objects, not socket locations.

The definition retains **observed** crosswise switch anchors: public 13 at right `Trigger.sw15`, public 15 at left `Trigger.sw13`, using the replacement-board orientation. Their semantic identities remain simply Number 3 lane and Number 1 lane, with no validated side assertion. The corresponding lamp-layout mismatch is disclosed, but lamps 24 and 56 remain unplaced. No new coordinate, original-era orientation claim or promotion follows from this photograph.

# Williams Johnny Mnemonic (1995) — Service Bulletins and Manual Addenda

Source: the four text PDFs that IPDB machine 3683 lists beside the operations manual, retrieved from
the Internet Archive's Wayback Machine captures of `https://www.ipdb.org/files/3683/`. Each has a
text layer (`Acrobat Web Capture 4.05`); the quoted text was read from that layer with Poppler
`pdftotext`. Only the statements the definition relies on are quoted.

## Service Bulletin SB 85, November 9, 1995, "Manual Amendment" (3 pages)

> Page 1-43 LED List
> LED 2 and LED 3 on the Power Driver board are not used.

> The Data Glove Ball Catcher is an electromagnet. When the ball is shot up to the magnet assembly,
> the electromagnet is turned on to full power. [...] Once the ball is in the magnet, the ball closes
> the "Ball in Hand" switch located inside the magnet. When this switch is closed, the magnet is duty
> cycled in order to prevent overheating of the coil and to prevent magnetizing the ball.

> 1. Verify that the magnet is positioned approximately over the ball popper mechanism [...]

> 2. Check the ball popper assembly located beneath the playfield. [...] This assembly is designed
> to consistently shoot the ball straight up to the hand.

> The switch must actuate due to the weight of the pinball only.

> The Data Glove consists of three major component assemblies. They are:
>
> - The "X" Direction Drive
> - The "Y" Direction Drive
> - The Hand Magnet
>
> The "X" and "Y" drives are similar. The main components of
> each drive are a DC gear motor, a drive screw with a floating nut, a coupler, a home switch, and a
> bi-directional optical encoder.

> The magnet assembly consists of an electromagnet fastened to a sheet metal can. The can has a core
> pin staked in the center and a leaf switch to detect when a ball is in the magnet.

## Service Bulletin SB 87, March 19, 1996, "Hand magnet causing fuse to blow intermittently." (2 pages)

> This procedure will move the hand electromagnet power supply circuit from fuse F104 to fuse F103,
> which is not used by any other circuit in the game.

> 3. Locate the 6-pin connector containing two red/brown 18 ga. wires in the same pin, two red/white
> wires in the same pin, one blue/yellow wire in its own pin, and one purple/blue wire in its own pin.
> This connector comes off the back panel assembly and is located at the back of the playfield.

## Manual Addendum 1, October 13, 1995, "Updating game with production modifications" (2 pages)

> GAME: Johnny Mnemonic (first 100 games manufactured)

> Several modifications were made to Johnny Mnemonic before regular production started, but after the
> first 100 (sample) games were manufactured.

> Game code version 1.0 or 1.1 must have Sound Code version 1.0.

The addendum also describes insulating the back-panel flash lamp terminals, whose shorting blows
fuse 111, and installing wire clip 12-7331 to keep the magnet in its housing.

## Manual Addendum 2, December 1995, "Fuses 104 and/or 105 blowing" (1 page)

> CAUSE: Wires to data glove not correctly dressed.

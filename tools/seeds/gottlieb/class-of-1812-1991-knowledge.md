# Class of 1812 (Gottlieb 1991)

Coverage: **partial**. Every controller address is enumerated and named from the ROM's own service tests, polarity is
settled for every switch, and the mechanisms are inventoried. Three things keep it partial, so `coverage.missing` is
`["output_semantics", "mechanism_behavior", "spatial_placement"]`: lamps 24 and 87, which the ROM names NOT USED,
publish state in play that no source explains, and no source shows the knocker (30) fitted; the ball lock was lit in
the scripted game but never made, so the gate's path and the lock's hold and release are unknown; and the playfield
positions come from one retained table and stay observed, with no factory drawing or second, independent table to
check them. The definition, its seed and the spatial report are generated by `tools/curate_class_of_1812.py`, which
also copies this note from its pinned seed `tools/seeds/gottlieb/class-of-1812-1991-knowledge.md`.

## Identity

- Premier Technology (trade name Gottlieb), August 1991, model 730, Gottlieb System 3, 1,668 units (IPDB 528, excerpt
  `evidence/excerpts/gottlieb.class-of-1812.1991/ipdb-page.md`). OPDB `G5DYX-M0oVW`. IPDB lists the features as
  Multiball (2) and the toys as a beating heart and chattering teeth.
- One physical PinMAME driver, `clas1812`. Its power-up screen reads `CLASS OF 1812 / 73O 88FB` (game 730, checksum
  88FB; the font draws 0 like O). Its clone `beav_butt` (Beavis and Butt-Head) is a 2023 VPX-only reskin that
  `scope.py` excludes.
- Pinned `src/wpc/gts3games.c` lines 217-226 declare it `INITGAME(clas1812, ALPHA, FLIP67, 3/*?*/, SNDBRD_GTS3, 4)`: two
  20-character 16-segment alphanumeric displays, flipper buttons copied into matrix switches 6 (left) and 7 (right), and
  `hw.lampCol = 4`, so there is no auxiliary driver board and lamps 120-127 do not exist on this machine. The ball count
  3 carries PinMAME's own `/*?*/`; the machine holds two balls.
- It is an early System 3 game: `MACHINE_INIT(gts3)` sets alpha generation 1, which reads slam and tilt on VIA U4 PB3
  and PB4 and puts the test button on CA1.

## Evidence

- **No manual.** IPDB lists a 3 MB English manual as "Availability limited by copyright" without a link, and the live
  file is a 0-byte placeholder. No other attributable copy was found, so no switch, lamp or coil chart, schematic or
  location drawing is retained.
- **Instruction cards.** IPDB's reproduction cards by Inkochnito (cards 28290 English and 28291 French, retrieved through
  the Wayback Machine; `instruction-cards.md`) give the rules and say that the two buttons right of the coin door select
  initials and the credit button enters them.
- **IPDB photos 5, 11, 12, 13, 19 and 24** (`ipdb-photo-<n>.md`): 11 shows which bulb on the sign at the right ramp
  carries which rule, 12 and 13 where the teeth and the heart stand, 19 the Bat-O-Meter sign, and 5 and 24 two coin
  doors with two slots and one.
- **The ROM's service tests** stand in for the manual, as on Gladiators. Runs of the pinned LibPinMAME on `clas1812`
  (`evidence/runtime/gts3/class-of-1812-clas1812-service-tests.json`, built by `tools/class_of_1812_runtime_evidence.py`
  from runs retained under `review-artifacts/gottlieb.class-of-1812.1991/runtime/`) step through:
  - the test button's menu: 1 bookkeeping and adjustments prompt (`PUSH L, WHITE BUTTON / FOR GAME ADJUSTMENTS`),
    2 memory test, 3 lamp check, 4 **Lamp Matrix Test**, 5 **Relay+Solenoid Test**, 6 **Switch Matrix Test**, 7 display
    test, 8 sound test, 9 **Front Door Test**;
  - the Lamp Matrix Test, which blinks one public lamp per step and prints its name and `=nn`;
  - the Relay+Solenoid Test, which numbers drivers from 0, one below the public address, steps on the right white
    button (5) and fires the displayed driver on the credit button (3);
  - the Switch Matrix Test, `ALL SWITCHES OPEN` with every address at 0 and the ROM's name with `=nn` while one address
    is held at 1;
  - the Front Door Test, which counts coins per chute (`L- C- R-`); plus a factory-settings load, one scripted game and
    two trough probes, `tilt-trough-closed` and `tilt-trough-open`, which play the same tilted game with the trough
    switch 44 held closed or opened after the release.
- **The known-working script** is tom tower & ninuzzu's `Class of 1812  - NZ&TT 1.0.vbs` as patched by Thalamus in
  2018, in the pinned `sverrewl/vpxtable_scripts` corpus (bindings in `nztt-script-bindings.md`). The same corpus holds
  UnclePaulie's 3.0.21 update of the same table, which corroborates only.
- **The retained table** is the original NZ&TT 1.0 `.vpx` (2017), retained under the working root's
  `vpx-sources/gottlieb/class-of-1812-1991/nztt-1.0/` with its vpxtool extraction, manifest and OBJ export. Its embedded
  script is the pinned script before Thalamus's patch: every binding is the same, the line numbers are shifted.
  `vpx-table-objects.md` lists each placed object, its coordinate, its light flags and the lines that bind it, and the
  lights the script draws that are not placements.

## Controller addressing

- Switches and lamps use Gottlieb's decimal numbers: tens digit is the strobe, units digit the return or row (0-7). The
  service tests print 100-117 as A0-A7 and B0-B7.
- Public 1 is the closed contact the CPU sees (`xvia_0_a_r` complements the column and no inverted-switch mask is set).
  The Switch Matrix Test reads `ALL SWITCHES OPEN` at 0 and names the held switch at 1, so every used matrix switch is
  recorded `normally_closed: false`.
- The cabinet port fills public 0-5: the left, right and center coin chutes (0, 1, 2), the credit button (3) and the
  left and right white buttons (4, 5) on the cabinet front right of the coin door, which `GTS3_COMPORTS` calls Left and
  Right Advance. In the self-tests the left white button enters the adjustments and the right one steps.
- The Front Door Test counts all three chutes, but doors differ: IPDB photo 5 shows a door with two coin slots and
  photo 24 a French door with one (10, 5 and 1 Fr). No source says which inputs a given door wires.
- The flipper buttons are 141 (right) and 143 (left) in PinMAME's flipper column. `core_updateSw` copies them into 7 and
  6 on every update, so a host must drive 141/143, never 6/7. With the Q relay (32) energized it also fabricates the
  synthetic flipper outputs 45/46 (right) and 47/48 (left). `Class1812.vbs` comments the two the other way round
  (`swLRFlip = 141 '06`, `swLLFlip = 143 '07`); the ROM's own names settle it.
- Matrix switches 56-117 are NOT USED in the Switch Matrix Test.
- Solenoids are public 1-32, the Relay+Solenoid Test's driver number plus one. No driver is NOT USED. The 3.0.21 script
  notes the same offset ("manual starts at zero"). 26, 31 and 32 are the A (lightbox), T (playfield G.I.) and Q (game
  on) relays. The Relay+Solenoid Test is the game's own driver table: it names each flasher with its bulb type and
  count, which is what records the backbox flashers 13-21 as fitted. It names the knocker (30) with nothing more and the
  script only plays a sound from it, so the knocker's fitment is recorded unknown.
- The Lamp Matrix Test names lamps 1, 24 and 87 NOT USED and every other lamp 0-117. Lamp 1 rose only inside the
  self-test runs, so it is recorded unused. Lamps 24 and 87 are driven in play all the same: 24 rose 25 times in the
  scripted game (18 of them while the bottom upkicker's switch was held), 7 times in `tilt-trough-closed` and 3 in
  `tilt-trough-open`; 87 rose 43 times in the scripted game (19 of them with the upkicker's switch held), 8 times in
  `tilt-trough-closed` and 3 in `tilt-trough-open`. No source says whether a bulb is fitted at
  either or what the state means, so both are recorded with unknown availability and not placed.
- PinMAME's `clas1812` block in `gts3.c` (lines 550-553) types solenoids 13-25 as #89 'Playfield & Backbox flashers' and
  lamps 10-14 (the 'Battometer') as strobed LEDs. The ROM's flasher names carry 67, the #67 bulb, and the bulb count in
  brackets.

## Mechanisms and recreation notes

**Ball path.**

- The machine holds two balls (IPDB Multiball (2); the script creates two). A drained ball lands on OUTHOLE (54); the
  OUTHOLE coil (29) moves it into the trough, and BALL RELEASE (28) feeds the lead ball into the shooter lane, where
  SHOOTER LANE (43) sees it waiting at the plunger.
- TROUGH (44) is closed only while both balls are home. The two trough probes run the same sequence and tilt the game
  on the second tilt-switch pulse; the open one adds two writes of 44, each followed by a 0.5 s settle:
  - Held closed throughout, the ROM raised A and T (26, 31) and moved the display straight on to ball 2; within the next
    step it reset both banks, energized Q (32) and released a ball (28) with the tilted ball still out and the outhole
    open. The fifth and sixth pulses did the same for ball 3, and when the ball finally reached 54 the ROM fired 29 and
    28 twice each, alternately.
  - Opened once the ball was released, the ROM showed `TILT`, released nothing while 44 stayed open, fired 29 four
    times (about every 0.65 s) while 54 was held for 3 s, and served ball 2 (8 and Q, then 9 and 28) only once 44
    closed again.
  - So the ROM reads 44 closed as "both balls in the trough", which places the switch at the trough's second position,
    behind the ball waiting at the release.
- At Start the ROM reset both drop banks (8, 9), energized Q (32) and energized 28 for about 0.5 s. When 54 rose in the
  scripted game the ROM released Q and showed `YOU DUG UP 1 STIFF`; once the host had opened 54 and closed 44 again, it
  fired 29, reset both banks, energized Q and 28, and ball 2 began.
- The script models the trough as two kickers, `Slot1` at the release and `Slot2` behind it, and the outhole as the
  `Drain` kicker. It writes 44 from `Slot1`, which is a table defect (below). The definition places 44 at `Slot2`.
- The script models no ball lock: balls rest only in the trough, the outhole and the two kickers, and both ramps end in
  wire-ramp sounds. The scripted game lit the lock but never made it or started a multiball (see Kickers and gate).

**Drop banks.**

- Left bank L DROP TARGET 1-4 (16, 26, 36, 46), reset by LEFT DROP T BANK (8); right bank R DROP TARGET 1-4 (17, 27, 37,
  47), reset by RIGHT DROP T BANK (9).
- Completing the left bank fired 8 once and started a `COUNTDOWN` value on the display; with L DT LIGHT LOCK (65)
  lit, it also turned 65 off, and R BB LOCK (47) flashed twice at once and again from about 12 s later. With the four
  right targets held down, the ROM fired 9 seven times in about 2.7 s: it retries a reset while the targets stay down.
- The cards: completing the left drop targets when lit lights the right ramp for the multiball lock (lamps 65 L DT LIGHT
  LOCK and 47 R BB LOCK); the right bank carries R DT MILLIONS (54) and R DT DOOR PRIZE (55).

**Kickers and gate.**

- BOTTOM UPKICKER (52 → coil 6) is a scoop in the middle of the playfield; TOP KICKER (53 → coil 7) is at the upper left,
  with TOP KICKER RO (55) on the lane above it.
- Held at 1 for 8 s, 52 drew six pulses of 6 (about every 0.7 s) and 53 eight pulses of 7 (about every 0.9 s): the ROM
  kicks again while its switch stays closed.
- GATE (10) is a diverter flap the script turns about Primitive `DiverterP` above the top kicker, swapping two walls.
- **Unresolved: the gate and the lock.** The cards read "COMPLETE LEFT DROP TARGETS WHEN LIT TO LIGHT RIGHT RAMP FOR
  LOCK" and "INSTANT MULTIBALL FROM DOOR PRIZE". In the scripted game, completing the left bank while 65 (L DT LIGHT
  LOCK) was lit turned 65 off; 47 (R BB LOCK) flashed twice at once, stayed dark until about 12 s later and then
  flashed through one pulse of the right ramp switch (50), which fired no gate and left 47 flashing. The host never
  closed the top kicker (53) afterwards. 10 rose only in the coil test, and the script models no lock device. So the ROM's full lock sequence, which path the gate opens, whether the
  top kicker holds the locked ball, and how the lock releases for multiball are not known. A gameplay run that lights the lock and then writes the right ramp (50)
  and the top kicker (53) would show the ROM's sequence on 10, 7 and the ball release 28, but there the host supplies
  the ball path; the gate's physical route and where the ball is held need the manual, a factory drawing or an
  observation of a real machine.

**Heart and teeth.**

- The teeth stand on a bracket at the back panel, left of the heart (IPDB photos 12 and 13), under the backbox.
- HEART (11) beats the heart and HEART 67 (2) (25) lights it; TEETH (12) works the jaw and lamp 2 (TEETH) lights it.
- The ROM chattered the teeth in two bursts of pulses in the scripted game: sixteen from about 2.5 s to 8.2 s after
  Start, the first held about 1.1 s and the others 0.06-0.34 s, and thirteen of 0.06-0.07 s each over about 1.7 s,
  beginning within 0.1 s of the first of two spinner pulses 0.8 s apart.
- The table puts both toys' meshes just behind the playfield's rear edge; the definition clamps them to y = 0.

**Pop bumpers and kicking rubbers.**

- Three pop bumpers (10/11/12 → coils 1/2/3) and two kicking rubbers, Gottlieb's slingshots (13/14 → 4/5), are fired by
  the CPU from their switches. A consumer drives only the switch.

**Bat-O-Meter.**

- Lamps 10-14 (5,000, 10,000, 20,000, EX BALL, SPECIAL). The cards advance it by completing the white targets and
  collect the flashing value at the spinner (15, 5,000 a pulse in the run).
- IPDB photo 19 shows it as a separate blue-bordered sign at the upper left, in front of the teeth, with five rows
  reading, top to bottom, Special, Extra Ball, 20000, 10000 and 5000, each with two dark round marks near its left end;
  photo 12 shows the same sign from the side with a wiring loom running to it. PinMAME types the five as strobed LEDs;
  the table draws them as bulbs in a vertical stack at the left rear (`bulb9`..`bulb5`).

**C.O.F.F.I.N. and ramps.**

- The return rollovers (23, 24) light a ramp to add a letter; lamps 40-45 are the letters C, O, F, F, I, N, which the
  ROM names by letter alone. Ramp switches are RIGHT RAMP (50) and LEFT RAMP (51); the script files them under
  "Optos", which no manual confirms.
- Two signs carry two bulbs each: the ROM's L BB ADD LETTER (36) and L BB MILLION (37) on the left one, R BB ADD LETTER
  (46) and R BB LOCK (47) on the right one. IPDB photo 11, captioned "Right Ramp", shows the right sign: a red bulb on
  the left over "Add Letter To COFFIN When Lit" and a blue one on the right over "LOCK BALL When Lit". CRYPT MILLION
  (67) is another bulb the table draws at the rear.
- Completing the top rollovers (25, 35, 45) advances the multiplier (lamps 20-23 2X-5X); in the run the third set off
  `YOU ADVANCED MULTIPLIER`.

**Relays and cabinet.**

- A (26) switches the backbox lightbox, T (31) the playfield G.I. (off while energized), and Q (32) enables the flippers.
  The pinned script switches its playfield G.I. from both A and T; the 3.0.21 script drives the backglass G.I. from A.
- KNOCKER (30) would be in the cabinet, but no retained source shows it fitted: the ROM names it and the script plays a
  sound from it. COIN MET/TIX COIL (27) is an optional accessory.

**Flashers.**

- 13-21 are LIGHTNING (three bulbs) and eight named for characters with two bulbs each (WILLY WOOF, LA GHOST, VAMP,
  MUMSY, ZOM B, ZOM B KILT, CLEAVER, PIECEMEAL). Both scripts treat them as backbox flashers; no manual confirms it, so
  they are not placed or classified.
- 22-24 (RAMP 1-3) light the playfield domes. The script draws 22 under the yellow bottom and the white dome and 23
  under the yellow top and the red dome. It draws 24 under the blue dome and on a light under the ramp, which no dome
  covers and which shows no bulb mesh: a table effect, so whether a second bulb is fitted under the ramp is unknown, and
  only the blue dome is placed. The 3.0.21 script's comments name the same
  pairs ("White dome and yellow dome bottom", "Red dome and yellow dome top", "Blue dome and under ramp").

**Placements.**

- Every placement comes from the retained table: the object the script binds, the light under each flasher dome, the
  bulb primitive the script draws a sign or crypt lamp on, and the members of the GI collection that show a bulb mesh.
- The table draws the other matrix lamps as image-mode overlays of their inserts (`1812 PFhole`) with no bulb under
  them, so those lamps are projected onto each overlay's centre, the insert they light, not a measured socket.
- The playfield G.I. collection has 23 lights; 19 show a bulb mesh and are placed, and `light18`, `light21`, `light22`
  and `light23` show none and are not.

## Table defects (not machine facts)

- The trough's switch 44 is written from `Slot1`, the release (lines 226-236), so the table keeps 44 closed while one
  ball waits there and the other is in play. The ROM reads that as both balls home, which is how a tilt puts a second
  ball in play. The 3.0.21 update writes 44 from `Slot2` (lines 789-790), and its changelog says the original's `Slot1`
  and `Slot2` subroutines were reversed and caused its trough, tilt and multiball faults (lines 4602-4611).
- `vpmNudge.TiltSwitch = 151` (line 112) names no System 3 address. The machine's tilt is -7.
- Thalamus's patch writes the white buttons 4 and 5 from the flipper keys (lines 160-161, 167-168), a convenience for
  entering initials. On the machine the flipper buttons do not close 4 or 5, and in the table every flipper press also
  presses a white button, including in the self-tests.
- `GIRelay` switches the playfield G.I. from the A relay (26), which PinMAME types as the backbox lightbox relay.
- The comment on `AddLamp 47, bulb4` (line 544) reads 'Right Ramp Million (Right)'; the ROM names lamp 47 R BB LOCK and
  photo 11 shows "LOCK BALL When Lit" under that bulb.

## What closes the record

- **output_semantics.** Lamps 24 and 87 are NOT USED in the Lamp Matrix Test yet driven in play, and the knocker (30) is
  named but not shown fitted. The service manual's lamp chart and coil table, or the machine's wiring, would say whether
  a bulb is fitted at either lamp and a knocker at 30.
- **mechanism_behavior.** The ball lock was lit in the scripted game but never made, so the gate's path (10) and the
  lock's hold and release are unknown. A gameplay run through the lock would show the ROM's sequence; the gate's physical route and the hold
  point need the manual, a factory drawing or an observation of a real machine.
- **spatial_placement.** The placements are observed only: they all come from one table. The service manual's switch,
  lamp and coil location drawings, or a second table built independently from the machine, would validate them. The
  same drawings would also settle the backbox flashers 13-21, the G.I. bulb count and whether 24 has a second bulb
  under the ramp.

## Ball storage, serve and manual shooter

Two balls rest in the gravity trough at11/12; outhole10 is clear. The retained stack initializes exactly two balls and occupancies11/12. A drain enters outhole10; coil1's spring-return lever moves that ball into storage. Coil2's feeder releases the lead ball to shooter lane22. Gravity then changes trough occupancy. Do not wire coil1 directly to either trough switch as a causal relationship: a ball changes those contacts. A blocked feeder, ball bridging, stuck microswitch or an empty model causes ROM search/retry behavior, not a fictitious successful serve. Initialize actual occupied switches before starting the controller.

The manual plunger is mechanical, with no launch coil address. It feeds the Spinout ramp/bowl R12262. Each passage of the counter closes/pulses44; collecting at the Kickbig closes43. Coil7 pivots its crank/plunger and kicks the collected ball into play, then spring returns it. Recreate circulation and gravity collection rather than assigning a fixed switch44 rate. VPX heading330deg/force35, angle variation2/force variation3 are useful physics tuning clues, not milliseconds or measured hardware velocity. The additional SpinoutKicker1 transfer object is a scripting helper. Bowl wall wear is documented by SS54; protectorA12471 prevents damage. A ball caught above the kicker or stuck counter can alter awards or search behavior without adding a mechanism.

## Catapult, saucers and passive ball paths

## Drop banks

Middle passenger bank: targets27/28/29 left/middle/right; reset4. Right PIN-BOT bank:30/31/32 top/middle/bottom; reset6. Both C11223-1 assemblies have individually latched spring-loaded targets and a common coil-driven reset plate. C11318-1 optical electronics distinguish each target; down public1, raised0. Clear all three only when the modeled reset actually raises them. Raised targets are the mechanical default, but ROM startup/reset pulses must operate the modeled state rather than be guessed into inputs. A latched down target, misaligned opto, failed LED supply or sluggish reset produces incomplete-bank states. Do not infer normally-closed polarity from optical beam rest.

## Jets, slings and flippers

Left/right/lower jet switches17/19/21 trigger their corresponding special outputs17/19/21. B9414 bumper skirts close leaf contacts; B9415 coil links pull rings to strike the ball, and springs restore them. Two slings18/20 have paired actuating contacts and B12283 spring-return kicker arms. The retained table animates slings without reporting switch18/20, a script deficiency to correct in a recreation using the factory matrix. Their fitted physical contacts remain in the definition. No switch-driven simulator special-coil shortcut is installed in Taxi; consume ROM outputs through the proven Williams permutation.

Lower flippers C11626-L-3/R-3 use FL11630 dual windings and local EOS. Game-on23 enables their cabinet power circuit. A held button sustains the hold winding after EOS opens at end travel; release lets the spring restore. Recreate the local EOS power cutoff and movement without publishing an invented separate sensor. Synthetic callbacks46/48 are the right/left compatibility outputs used by S11.VBS;45/47 are separate lower states. A stuck EOS risks an energized high-power winding; it must not become an unrelated matrix contact.

Bell13 is an electromechanical striker; knocker14 is in the backbox. They have no feedback switches. They are independent effects, not ball-routing devices.

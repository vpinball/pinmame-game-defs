## Ball storage, serve and manual shooter

The factory [Playfield Parts table](../../evidence/excerpts/williams.taxi.1988/playfield-parts.md) (PDF 65, printed TAXI 62) identifies item 52 as `01-3569-1 Ball Trough`.

Two balls rest in the gravity trough at 11/12; outhole 10 is clear. The retained stack initializes exactly two balls and occupancies 11/12. A drain enters outhole 10; coil 1's spring-return lever moves that ball into storage. Coil 2's feeder releases the lead ball to shooter lane 22. Gravity then changes trough occupancy. Do not wire coil 1 directly to either trough switch as a causal relationship: a ball changes those contacts. A blocked feeder, ball bridging, stuck microswitch or an empty model causes ROM search/retry behavior, not a fictitious successful serve. Initialize actual occupied switches before starting the controller.

The manual plunger is mechanical, with no launch coil address. It feeds the Spinout ramp/bowl R12262. Each passage of the counter closes/pulses 44; collecting at the Kickbig closes 43. Coil 7 pivots its crank/plunger and kicks the collected ball into play, then spring returns it. Recreate circulation and gravity collection rather than assigning a fixed switch 44 rate. VPX heading 330 deg/force 35, angle variation 2/force variation 3 are useful physics tuning clues, not milliseconds or measured hardware velocity. The additional SpinoutKicker1 transfer object is a scripting helper. Bowl wall wear is documented by SS54; protector A12471 prevents damage. A ball caught above the kicker or stuck counter can alter awards or search behavior without adding a mechanism.

## Catapult, saucers and passive ball paths

Catapult C11641-1 uses AL23-800, moving arm 03-8089, plunger A12293 and spring 10-135. Switch 35 closes with a seated ball; coil 3 pivots the arm and throws it onto an elevated wireform return. Return spring restores the arm; no home switch exists. The VPX saucer's force 140/KickZ 1 is a model clue. Dracula inbound/outbound kickers and a 75 deg primitive animation represent ball transfer; they are not extra physical outputs. Failure modes include misaligned arm/stop, missed capture, weak launch and wireform obstruction. Do not make the ROM's launch pulse immediately clear 35 unless the modeled ball actually leaves.

Upper-left JOYRIDE hole 13 is held while occupied. Coil 5 raises the eject cam B9361-R-1, launching back into play, then spring returns the cam. VPX 170 deg/force 20. ## Jets, slings and flippers

Left/right/lower jet switches 17/19/21 trigger their corresponding special outputs 17/19/21. Two slings 18/20 have paired actuating contacts and B12283 spring-return kicker arms. The retained table animates slings without reporting switch 18/20, a script deficiency to correct in a recreation using the factory matrix. Their fitted physical contacts remain in the definition. No switch-driven simulator special-coil shortcut is installed in Taxi; consume ROM outputs through the proven Williams permutation.

Lower flippers C11626-L-3/R-3 use FL11630 dual windings and local EOS. Game-on 23 enables their cabinet power circuit. A held button sustains the hold winding after EOS opens at end travel; release lets the spring restore. Recreate the local EOS power cutoff and movement without publishing an invented separate sensor. Synthetic callbacks 46/48 are the right/left compatibility outputs used by S11.VBS; 45/47 are separate lower states. A stuck EOS risks an energized high-power winding; it must not become an unrelated matrix contact.

Bell 13 is an electromechanical striker; knocker 14 is in the backbox. They have no feedback switches. They are independent effects, not ball-routing devices.

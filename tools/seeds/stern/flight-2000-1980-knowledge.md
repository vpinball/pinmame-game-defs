# Flight 2000 (Stern, 1980)

**Coverage is partial.** The production switch matrix, coil decoder, six display fields, stock ROM family and most controlled lamp effects are mapped. The spatial report gives switch positions, mechanism projections and candidate insert apertures. The trough and launcher geometry, lamp sockets, ambiguous wiring and modified-ROM behavior remain open.

## Machine and controller

Stern Electronics model 128 is a four-player widebody with MPU-200, an LDA-100 feature lamp driver, a solenoid driver/regulator, C-605 sound module and VSU-100 voice synthesis. Its August 1980 identity is IPDB 887 and OPDB `G48kY-MDxRV`. PinMAME's `dispst7` exposes four seven-digit player fields and two two-digit credit and ball/match fields. The blank video/DMD callback seen in the harness has no corresponding display in the factory backbox inventory. [IPDB machine 887](https://www.ipdb.org/machine.cgi?id=887)

Drive matrix switches 1–40 with active-high closures. The pinned BY35 matrix read passes the closed-contact level through; its Stern-generation byte or register handling does not invert the public state of these contacts. The two cabinet buttons use public 82 and 84. The physical normally closed EOS contacts are separately wired at the flipper assemblies. While the game-on relay is enabled, PinMAME asserts both outputs in each virtual power/hold pair (45/46 right, 47/48 left) together. A recreation must model physical winding current and EOS timing separately if it needs them. The voice board shares PIA and sound-board control paths. The printed sound test identifiers 20–29 are separate from the public solenoid range. [Pinned Flight 2000 driver and layout](https://github.com/vpinball/pinmame/blob/46e1a01a0f73dc5ce163d98db1ae435883ecc450/src/wpc/stgames.c)

The manual's solenoid test prints the transistor `Q` number. For example public 9 drives Q13, the ball launcher, public 10 drives Q14, the outhole, and public 19 drives Q15, the flipper-enable relay. The stock-ROM harness with all three trough contacts initially closed stepped through burn-in, lamps, displays, the 19 routed driver positions and switch test. In the coil test, the settled player-one digits a quarter second after each pulse matched the factory Q1–Q19 order. The final switch samples still displayed the closed trough contact. They establish no independent switch-test reading for 39 or 40. See the [retained trace summary](../../evidence/runtime/stern/flight-2000-service.json) and [factory coil chart](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/solenoid-identification.md).

## Recreation

- The left five-target bank has a common reset and five individual trip coils. Use switch addresses 28–32 for the leaves, public 7 for the reset and 13, 14, 12, 11, 8 for individual trips numbered 1–5. The ROM spots lit countdown targets and tracks the ordered 5-to-1 hits. The simpler right three-target bank uses switches 25–27 and reset 15. All three down advances multiplier and spinner value. [Factory operation and parts](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/factory-operation.md)
- The B circuit has three physical contacts on public 17. The left outer return rollover and the two rear stand-ups score the same shared switch. The exact VPX right-hand stand-up object is named `sw17a1`; the script calls its second stand-up handler `sw17c`. Preserve the factory wiring in a recreation even if a VPX object needs its event binding corrected. The top four rollovers spot L-A-S-T. Lower return lanes spot B, O and both F letters. [Factory switch chart](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/switch-identification.md)
- The upper-left walker stores successive balls, turns on Apollo stages after qualifying shots, and releases them through the left then right kickout paths toward the rear arch. The ball launcher contact is 39, Release Next Rocket is 40, and stage contacts are 15 and 16. Their respective public stage outputs are 20 and 17. The stock rules qualify a three-ball release with two balls staged, BLASTOFF and an ordered countdown. Build the mechanical ball guide and establish its default latch positions using real hardware. The bord VPX uses three virtual kickers and releases their stop walls together from public 9. That implementation shows the controller contract and deliberately abstracts the walker linkage and release timing. [VPX contract](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/vpx-contract.md) · [IPDB close-up](https://www.ipdb.org/showpic.pl?id=887&picno=15842)
- The trough contacts 33, 34 and 35 are separate physical reports under the apron. The ROM serves via public 10. The factory shipped examples with the kicking solenoid on top of the playfield wood beneath the apron and examples with it underneath. Both feed the same shooter lane. A VPX effect anchor represents the outlet. Lifetime serial boundaries remain unverified. [Early serve photo](https://www.ipdb.org/showpic.pl?id=887&picno=49153) · [Later serve photo](https://www.ipdb.org/showpic.pl?id=887&picno=49151)
- Feature lamps are on four decoder banks, 15 channels each. Pair factory loads to published addresses through the SCR, not the connector number. Shoot Again at public 11 has a playfield socket and a backbox branch. The stage, T insert, Match and associated pin discrepancies stay visible in their device records. A direct harness continuity check or a corrected factory lamp list would settle the ambiguous loads. The playfield GI is a transformer-fed 6 VAC circuit. The VPX glow pass does not establish the original socket count. [Lamp wiring](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/lamp-wiring.md) · [Decoder routing](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/decoder-routing.md)

## Driver variants

`flight2k` is the production set, `flightfp` changes U5 and U6 for free play, `flight2m` is the 3335 modified-rules CPU set, and `flight2mb` is the 3347 rules and speech revision with changed CPU ROMs and two VSU ROMs. PinMAME declares the same input ports, segment layout and hardware module for all four. The factory manual lists one populated voice ROM at U9. The VSU-100 schematic provides both U9 and U10 for the two-ROM installation. The retained runtime trace used only the production set. The exact rules and speech differences of the modified sets remain open. [VSU-100 ROM positions](../../evidence/excerpts/stern.flight-2000.1980/vsu-rom-sockets.md)

PinMAME also makes `gamatron` a clone of `flight2k`, but its ROM drives the separate 1985 Pinstar conversion. The conversion has its own playfield, lacks Flight 2000's speech board, and keeps its own [machine record](../../machines/partial/pinstar/gamatron-1985.json). [Pinstar Gamatron, IPDB 984](https://www.ipdb.org/machine.cgi?id=984)

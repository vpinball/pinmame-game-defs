# X-Men Pro (Stern, 2012)

Coverage: **partial — output 20 fitment/function and normalized spatial placement gaps.**

The original Pro service book and fresh Pro ROM tests settle the assigned lamp functions and the identified Pro flasher circuits. Output 20's fitment and function remain unknown: its own chart prints a possible bulb specification but no function, and no retained positive source settles whether a load is fitted. Coverage is missing output_semantics as well as spatial_placement. Independent Pro drawing checks reconcile 54 ordinary lamps and 10 switch assemblies. There are 51 accepted measurements: 38 plan reads, twelve observed rear-panel projections and one observed raised Q27 housing projection. The partial GI census records 35 supported bodies/projections without asserting a whole-machine count. Two GI identities, Q27 height/parallax, Q31 bracket geometry, missing individual trough-actuator geometry, rear perspective and remaining observed projections prevent promotion. The three former trough points were wiring hardware and are withdrawn. The spatial report preserves rejected history.

## Controller and lamps

The SAM matrix exposes switches 1-64, dedicated cabinet ranges 65-72, 81-88 and -7 through 0, and DIP ordinals 1-8. Main outputs are 1-32; 33 is PinMAME's virtual game-on state. The physical backbox DMD is 128 x 32, not a playfield display. The Pro has no LE auxiliary outputs 51-58.

The Pro's own matrix and complete ROM lamp walk agree on 61 assigned lamp functions, including optional tournament lamp 2; 60 functions are standard. The manufacturer's native cabinet photograph shows the fitted green Start button and a black plug below it. Lamp 2 and tournament-start switch 15 are optional, while Start lamp 1 remains standard. Firmware naming does not prove that an optional button is installed. Quantity 1 on lamp 2 is per fitted optional button, not 61 physical lamps on every Pro. Stable IDs and public bindings remain unchanged. Other function cells are blank in the chart and read NOT USED in the ROM. Lamp 63 is a different, unused address; an owner report about a missing Wolverine head lamp at 63 cannot be mapped to 59. The old lamp 59 conflict is settled by the Pro chart and diagnostic. Its own physical drawing supplies a calibrated scoop-top assembly projection; the `f59b` reflection is never the coordinate source.

Lamps 11 and 38 are the static left/right ramp-entrance inserts with Nightcrawler artwork, not moving toys. Lamps 28/29 are CENTER TARGETS (LEFT/RIGHT) in the chart and LIGHT LOCK (LEFT/RIGHT) in the ROM. Lamps 48/49 are the right target-bank top/bottom inserts, not two additional Magneto mechanisms. Diagnostic names distinguish the red Wolverine insert at 30 from Wolverine at 31, and Magneto red/white/green at 33/34/35. Retain the working callback addresses even when the artwork's wording differs.

The matrix prints #555 clear bulbs except #44 clear at 54/55/56/57 and LED pop boards at 60/61/62. The owner's subsequent conversion sheet lists different bases at 50/56/70 and says a blue LED was already at 59. These are retained source readings, not factory color assignments or proof of extra sockets. The physical insert count is one per populated lamp; `lN`/`lNa` are render layers. ## Mechanisms and initial state

Install four balls and initialize trough occupancy 18-21 closed, with the stacking/jam beam 22 clear. The common 500-6318-24-ND cut-away distinguishes **three microswitches 18-20** from the vertically stacked exit optos **21 below 22**. The old Pro microswitch classification of 21 was wrong. The working script uses `bsTrough.InitSw 0, 21, 20, 19, 18, 0, 0, 0` and pulses 22 during output-1 eject. The 21/22 beam stack projects in plan onto its own `BallRelease` exit assembly. The three former microswitch projections are withdrawn: pixels (355, 149)/(314, 169) show cable-tie heads, and (270, 190) shows a white harness clip/connector. Rejected pixels and former coordinates remain history, while 18/19/20 have no accepted individual placements. The cutaway establishes order but its drawing break cannot supply a length ruler. No replacement points or equal spacing are invented.

Output 2 auto-launches from shooter switch 23; retain a manual plunger as well. Power Scoop switch 4 stays active while holding a ball and output 3 ejects it; the working model uses a 168-degree, speed-12 kick with vertical component 0.4 and force variation 2. These are table tuning, not measured physical specifications. The VUK captures at `sw55a` on switch 55; output 5 launches onto the left ramp through helper `sw55`. The helper's remote ejection position is not the physical socket.

Output 4 controls the Magneto playfield magnet. The orbit diverter on 26 routes the shot into the four-level vertical Magneto lock. Occupancy is 53 at the bottom, then 38, 39 and 40 at the top; linked up-post and latch outputs are 6 and 7. The working model raises both posts and returns them after a 500 ms latch delay. Model real occupancy and failed release states so the ROM can perform its own retries and ball search; a projected lock anchor is not a sensor census.

Wolverine is a passive wobbling bash toy sensed by 36, with a separate output-32 magnet. The model disables center-grab so the magnet deflects rather than rigidly captures the ball. Pops pair 9/30 left, 10/31 right and 11/32 bottom; slings pair 13/26 and 14/27. The passive Cyclops spinner pulses 47. Standup targets are 1/2 Hellfire, 7/8 Brotherhood, 41/42 Light Lock, plus Wolverine 36; none is a resettable drop-target bank. Build both molded ramps, the inner loop and both outer orbits.

The Pro omits the LE's moving Iceman Ice Slide, latched Nightcrawler pop-ups, spinning Magneto disc, subtractive red/blue/white GI channels and auxiliary board. Similar artwork does not establish those mechanisms on a Pro.

# Sega Baywatch (1995)

Baywatch uses Sega model 33, Data East CPU revision 3b, DE2 sound and the 192 × 64 backbox DMD. It is a five-ball machine with four flippers, three turbo bumpers, two slingshots, a three-target drop bank, twelve stand-up targets, a spinner, three distinct capture/eject devices, two launchers and a left-ramp trapdoor. The verified identity is IPDB 2848 and OPDB Grxvy-M4oZj. The exact evidence is linked by source IDs and complete excerpts in the [definition](../../machines/partial/sega/baywatch-1995.json).

All eight pinned PinMAME variants remain on this physical record. The language releases share the Baywatch initializer and I/O; the English release has a different sound-ROM population. The 2017 and 2025 unofficial firmware revisions remain firmware variants of the 1995 machine. No driver uses Whitestar, extra lamp columns, custom switch columns or custom solenoids. The older sample-game manual has different launch/flasher construction; its complete location legend is retained for comparison, while the production assemblies govern this record.

## Controller contract

Read the public state as PinMAME delivers it. The 8 × 8 matrix uses sequential 1–64 addresses; the driver mask is zero and the row read is not complemented. The production manual explicitly describes the trough VUK opto as an open matrix contact with the beam clear and a closed contact when blocked. Its public state is 1 when occupied, as in the table's separate ball stack. An opto's detector voltage does not imply an extra host inversion.

Drive cabinet flipper buttons 84 (left) and 82 (right). PinMAME overwrites matrix 63/64 with those button states every update. The table's own writes to 63/64 work only because it also calls de2.vbs, which writes the effective column. Outputs 45/46 and 47/48 are paired synthetic states; they do not report independent physical windings. The lower flippers have board-local EOS contacts; PinMAME exposes no physical EOS host input. Both upper bats also accept CPU output 13 through isolation diodes, independently of their cabinet-button path. The upper SSFB drawing explicitly shows the N.O. cabinet switches.

The PPB's A/B relay at 10 multiplexes the eight coil drives 1–8 with flasher circuits 25–32. Special-solenoid output order is derived through the Data East PIA permutation and checked in the ROM's cycling test: left/bottom/right bumpers 17/18/19, left/right slings 20/21, Laser Kick 22. Enable 23 is virtual game-on state. Reserved gaps and absent custom slots are enumerated separately from the eight CN3 printer-line outputs, whose availability remains unknown after the tried printout.

## Ball handling and mechanisms

The trough has five mechanical switches, 10 at the left through 14 at the right, and one separate up-kicker opto at 15. Output 1 operates the 25-1240 lock-ball cam; output 2 lifts the released ball into shooter lane 16, where output 3 performs the separate launch onto the elevated wire ramp. The retained table represents these as two ball stacks. Initialize five balls in the mechanical trough and leave 15 clear until the cam transfers a ball. Ball-transfer timing and the table's 250 ms timer are recreation tuning, not factory measurements.

Output 3 fires the lower-right automatic launcher at shooter-lane switch 16, feeding the shooter wire ramp toward the three rear SPF lanes. Output 4 drives a separate mid-right Upper Ball Launch / Kick Big with rollover 47. They are separate mechanisms and routes. The automatic-launch assembly and upper-launch assembly settle the coil schematic's swapped/mistyped labels and parts; the complete printed disagreements remain in the excerpt.

The rear-left upper VUK is switch 44/output 5. The under-arch front-left Super VUK is switch 46/output 6 and receives the under-playfield return detected at 55. The left mid-playfield Shark Super Scoop is switch 56/output 7. Keep all three ball stacks distinct. Completing drop targets 22/23/24 lights the tower lock. Output 12 opens the left metal ramp's trapdoor, diverting a ball into the under-playfield return; otherwise the ramp continues to exit 26. Tower indicators 33/34/35 are three #555 sockets with teal covers; the table exposes no three individual tower-ball sensors.

Upper gate 9 and lower gate 14 are separate mini-coils; the upper gate lies near the rear lanes under the left ramp, the lower near the upper launch and Shark Fin route under the butyrate. They have ball-route sensors, not measured gate-limit switches. The drop bank resets through output 16. Its physical switches remain closed while targets are down and clear when the bank rises.

Shark Fin rollover 54 is ahead of the upper-right bat. When Shark Flips is lit the ROM times output 13 to send the ball toward the left Super Scoop; the table moves both upper bats on this pulse, matching the physical dual enable wiring. The manual's Shark Fin test and Adjustment 50 tune the automatic shot. This switch is not EOS. Laser Kick 22 returns a ball up the left outlane when switch 57 closes and the feature is lit; the two stand-up targets 9/53 relight it. Bumper and sling switches pulse their own addresses; the ROM drives the PIA outputs.

## Lighting and positions

There are 64 matrix addresses. Address 25 has two outlane Special bulbs; the remaining matrix addresses each denote one bulb. Lamps 7/8/64 are cabinet buttons. The Shark Super Scoop, Tourist Season and Ironman signs are physical elevated bulbs; names describing their shots in the manual do not turn them into floor inserts. The exact table drives the sign and tower BulboF primitives. Their positions use exported world-space mesh bounds, checked in the table coordinate convention, not primitive origins or flasher sprites.

Each of the eight flasher circuits has four physical sockets, including its backbox bulbs. Circuit 25's two non-backbox lamps are on the rear panel; one schematic text instead names a drop-target bulb, which disagrees with the location drawing, the production table and the retained physical photograph. No drop-target sprite is promoted from that wording. The summary chart lists #89 for every circuit, but the detailed wiring drawing specifies mixed #89/#906 populations on 1R/3R/4R/5R/6R/8R. That drawing retains older location wording too, so the six circuits carry `conflict.flasher-bulb-composition` and omit an exclusive bulb part number. The two remaining circuits agree on four #89 bulbs. The parts list's unsplit quantities, production-change caveat and inherited other-game footnote cannot settle the six circuit populations. PinMAME's uniform #89 brightness model is emulator metadata.

G.I. is one solenoid-controlled relay at 11. The table inverts this state into local lamp 200, so 200 is neither a real matrix address nor a separate hardware GI channel. Only one bulb object from each GIBulb render family is admitted, plus the two G.I. bumper bulbs. The three drop-target glow helpers, broad lightmaps, reflections and all backglass sprites are excluded. The exact physical GI socket quantity and full branch allocation remain unresolved.

Coordinates use x=0 left, x=1 right, y=0 rear and y=1 apron, divided by the retained table's exact 952 × 2162 bounds. One recreation lineage supplies these observed positions; the LW/nFozzy scripts derive from the same Kiwi recreation. Manual drawings and physical IPDB/Pinside photos corroborate selected identities, sides and bulb categories; they do not validate all placements. The measured lamp drawing discrepancy below remains unreconciled. The reset coil is explicitly projected onto the middle drop target; the individual trough sensors and lock-cam coil have no admitted object. Flasher 29 has no honest socket objects, and flasher 30 is missing one of its two playfield bulb objects. See the [spatial report](../../reports/spatial/sega/baywatch-1995.json) for exact objects, coordinates, hashes, omissions and rejected helpers.

The factory lamp drawing (PDF 41 / printed 37) and the exact table remain unreconciled at lamp 12 and tower lamps 33/34/35. Independent readings of the actual socket bodies, using the drawing's outer frame without fitting floor controls, give approximately (.722796,.419259) for 12 and (.127482,.226669), (.170373,.223523), (.211676,.220377) for the tower group. The corresponding observed BulboF world-mesh positions remain (.800330,.322505), (.120437,.108845), (.148651,.107626), (.177002,.106530). Six floor controls differ by at most .005188 in either normalized axis; this cannot establish the projection of elevated lamps. Drawing reading uncertainty is about +/-0.009 x and +/-0.004 y, with additional unquantified projection and symbol-to-emitter uncertainty. No alternate physical emitter bound to these addresses was found near the drawing locations: nearby bulbs are GI and the f objects are render sprites. Retain the observed positions with device warnings, without declaring the table physically wrong or treating the difference as a machine-design conflict. The [comparison excerpt](../../evidence/excerpts/sega.baywatch.1995/lamp-drawing-comparison.md) and spatial report retain frame corners, pixels, controls, math, source roles, exact object checks and diagram crops.

## Service and remaining requirements

The Portals ROM uses black Begin Test/Enter and green Service Credits/Select as momentary buttons. The cabinet wiring drawing (PDF 86 / printed 82) explicitly shows both STEP/FOR-REV contacts normally open. Bulletin 74, April 28, 1995, removes the green button's latching C-shaped wire form on early games so the menu stops scrolling. The earlier manual's FORWARD/REVERSE navigation text predates that behavior. The modification changes service-button mechanics, with no documented playfield change; it does not change their electrical contact construction.

Fresh-state runs with the verified USA 4.00 ROM exercised the switch matrix, all 64 single-lamp slots, the automatic coil cycle and printer interface. The ROM's printed number is paired with the public output trace; no press-count inference substitutes for the displayed state. A successful gameplay successor completed same-run checkpoints for trough serve, lower launch, all three capture/eject devices, Laser Kick, all bumpers and slings, and drop reset, then observed both cabinet flipper pairs. Shorter failed attempts remain external and their unexecuted actions are excluded. Adjustment 50 published output 13, but its delayed timing did not isolate rollover 54 as the cause; the physical wiring, manual and working script establish that route. Output 15 also pulses during gameplay despite the manual and cycling test naming it NOT USED, so its purpose and availability remain unknown. See [runtime evidence](../../evidence/runtime/data-east/baywatch-usa-400-service-tests.json).

This record remains partial for four precise dimensions: input semantics (country jumper W7 values are not assigned); output semantics (driver 15, CN3 37–44 usage and the full physical GI inventory remain unsettled); spatial placement (one lineage, unreconciled lamp 12/33/34/35 drawing/table positions, missing trough geometry, incomplete flasher sockets and GI survey); and unresolved conflicts (six flasher circuits' bulb composition). No untried available harness run is named as a resolution path. No ROM, manual, VPX, NVRAM or photograph is redistributed in Git.

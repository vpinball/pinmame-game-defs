# Tales from the Crypt (Data East, 1993)

Coverage: **partial - manual- and ROM-verified I/O for the full 8x8 switch and lamp matrices with connector, wire-colour and drive-transistor wiring, every public solenoid address, all sixteen direct and relay-paired coil drivers, the six auxiliary solenoids and three flippers, switch polarity for all 58 used matrix switches, the Black service button and both flipper buttons, fifteen source-reconciled mechanisms with a ROM-observed ball serve, and {placed} normalized playfield placements from the one retained recreation, {validated} of them validated against the factory location drawings; held below author-ready by three open requirements: the general illumination and the placements the drawings do not confirm have only the one recreation behind them, no retained source states what the W7 jumper selects, and the contact polarity of the Green FORWARD/REVERSE button is not established**

## Identity

Data East Tales from the Crypt, released 4 November 1993 (IPDB #2493, model 500-5518-01, 4,500 units made to 6 January 1994). It is a three-flipper machine on Data East/Sega "Version 3" CPU hardware (the manual's CPU jumper table lists `Tales From the Crypt Ver3`, with jumpers J1b, J3, J5, J5b, J6b, J7b and J8 installed and J1a, J2, J4, J5a, J6a and J7a removed), with a 128 x 32 dot-matrix display (`GEN_DEDMD32`, `de_128x32DMD`) and the DE2S sound board. PinMAME roots the family at `tftc_303` with {driver_count} drivers, every one sharing `init_tftc` and therefore one `tftcGameData`, so all of them present identical playfield hardware: 1.04 Spanish, 2.00, 3.00, 3.02 Dutch, 3.03, and the 2015 community MOD 4.00. They differ in the CPU and display ROM images and nothing else; the three sound ROMs are identical across the family. The MOD runs on the same cabinet and stays a driver variant of the physical machine.

The title is licensed from the EC Comics series. IPDB lists three flippers, three pop bumpers, two slingshots, three spinning targets, two vertical up-kickers, two ramps, two scoops, a 3-bank drop target, six eyeball targets, a captive ball, 3- and 6-ball multiball, an autoplunger shaped like a door handle with the head of the Crypt Keeper, a shaker motor and a rising and lowering gravestone. IPDB also records that the video mode the manual describes ("the player will interface with the Display to help the Crypt Keeper defeat foes") did not appear on machines leaving the factory, and that the designer wrote the Door Prize select video sequence.

## What the evidence is, and what it is not

The manual is the GameEx-hosted scan of Data East Pinball, Inc.'s 1993 operations manual: 122 pages, image-only, so every table in the committed excerpts was read from native-resolution renders of the pages (printed page = PDF page minus four). No factory addendum is retained, so none of the printed figures has been checked against a correction. The excerpts commit the switch and lamp matrices with their wire, connector and transistor headers, the complete switch part-number table, the lamp location table, the coil and flash-lamp tables, the Special Coil Wiring Diagram's per-drive text, the bulb table, the playfield major-assembly list and the Laser Kick and Gravestone test text; crops of the drawings are committed beside the tables.

The retained known-working table is the VPW 1.01 recreation. **It runs `tftc_400`, the unofficial 4.00 MOD**, so its callbacks are evidence about the MOD's use of the factory map. They agree with the factory manual drive for drive (1-9, 11, 16 and 22 bind the manual's coils, 25-32 its right-hand flash lamps, and the tombstone mechanism binds 15), which is the only reason the table is admitted as runtime evidence for the factory ROMs. A second local table, Bigus MOD 1.3, comes from the same freneticamnesic and 32assassin lineage and says in its own header that it is not verified; it is not independent evidence.

PinMAME is the pinned 8371478 checkout. The ROM evidence is six fresh-state LibPinMAME runs of US 3.03 (below): five service tests, each reached by display-title checkpoints, and one game start in which the harness host moves the balls through the trough; all are driven only by direct public switch writes; the raw runs and every 128x32 frame stay under the working root with a canonical manifest, and the 179 frames that the definition rests on are read and recorded beside their pixel digests in `evidence/runtime/data-east/tales-from-the-crypt-tftc_303-service-tests.json`.

## The address model

Data East runs on the shared Williams System 11 core (`s11.c`); there is no `de.c`. Neither printed matrix uses the WPC column-times-ten notation: **both are column-major, address = (column - 1) x 8 + row**, which is exactly the number printed in each cell. Column 1 of the switch matrix is the cabinet column (plumb tilt, 4th coin, credit button, three coin switches, slam tilt, buy-in button).

- **All 64 lamp positions are populated;** the printed chart has no Not Used cell. Six switch positions are not used: 34, 35 and 58-61.
- **There is no GI channel.** General illumination is **public solenoid 11**, the General Illumination Relay K-1 on the power-supply board, and `s11.c` types it as a reversed 6.3 VAC #44 output for this machine, so a consumer shows the GI lit while solenoid 11 is off. The retained table's `SetGI` agrees.
- **Public solenoid 10 is the Left/Right coil relay** on the PPB board. It re-routes drives 1-8 from the coils (left set) to flash lamps (right set); pinned PinMAME publishes the right set at **25-32** (`muxSol = 10`). The retained table binds all eight right addresses to its Sol1R-Sol8R flasher callbacks and never binds 10.
- **Drives 12, 13 and 14 are not fitted:** the Special Coil Wiring Diagram prints `NO COIL AT THIS LOCATION` on each. **Drive 15 is the tombstone motor relay** (the retained table's `cvpmMech` binds Sol1 = 15) and **drive 16 is the cabinet shaker motor** through the Q4 power transistor.
- **Solenoids 17-22 are the six switched solenoids:** the manual's CPU Controlled Auxiliary Solenoids table lists Left Turbo Bumper 17, Center Turbo Bumper 18, Right Turbo Bumper 19, Left Slingshot 20, Right Slingshot 21 and Laser Kickback 22 with drive transistors Q11, Q9, Q8, Q10, Q12 and Q13. Pinned PinMAME publishes them at 17 + `ssSolNo[1]` = {3,4,5,1,0,2} by PIA line, which is not sequential, and `INITGAMES11` leaves the switch-driven table empty, so they appear only when the ROM drives a PIA special line itself. The printed coil number and the public address are different identities, and the ROM's own Cycling Coils test settles the pairing: it fires public 17, 18, 19, 20, 21 and 22 in that order under the names LEFT TURBO, BOTTOM TURBO, RIGHT TURBO, LEFT SLING, RIGHT SLING and LASER KICK 50V, so on this machine the printed coil numbers are the public addresses.
- **Solenoid 23 is game-on** (`S11_GAMEONSOL`), 24 is empty, 33-36 are inert on this generation, 45-48 are PinMAME's synthetic lower-flipper power and hold bits, 49 is a simulator slot and 50 is reserved.
- **Solenoids 37-44 are the printer data lines.** `tftcGameData` sets `S11_PRINTERLINE`, so `s11.c` publishes PIA2 port B there; its comment puts bits 0-2 on CN3 pins 9, 8 and 7 and bit 7 on pin 1, and elides bits 3-6. The manual's Adj. 61 PRINTER INTERFACE lets the operator print an audit sheet with the Start button, and pressing Start on that adjustment asserts public 40, 42, 43 and 44 (bits 3, 5, 6 and 7) together; nothing from 37 to 44 moves in any other retained test. The addresses carry the printer's data port, so they are `used` virtual outputs with no playfield device.
- **Three flippers, two buttons.** The manual's Flipper Solenoids table lists a Left Flipper (090-5032-00), a Right Flipper Lower (090-5032-00) and a Right Flipper Upper (090-5044-00, coil 25-1800); the upper right flipper shares the lower right's `ORN-VIO CPU CN19-1` flipper-ground line and the retained table fires both from one right-hand solenoid. PinMAME declares `FLIP6364 = FLIP_SWNO(63,64)` with no `FLIP_SOL`: the flippers are cabinet-wired and `core_updateSw` rewrites matrix switches 63 and 64 on every update from PinMAME's own flipper column. The chart calls those two addresses Left and Right End of Stroke (part 180-5124-00) but no end-of-stroke state is modelled, so the addresses carry button state; **a consumer drives 84 (left button) and 82 (right button), never 63 or 64**, and the same bits synthesise the flipper outputs 47/48 and 45/46 while the switched-solenoid enable is on. The other six positions of the flipper column cannot be read by the ROM and are recorded unused.
- **Service buttons** sit in switch column 0: -7 is the Black Advance (STEP) button and -6 the Green Up/Down (FORWARD/REVERSE) button. Public 1 is the depressed STEP button, and `s11.c` hands the PIA its complement, the level of a contact closing to ground, so the momentary button rests open. The FORWARD/REVERSE button is an alternate-action push-button whose two maintained positions the manual calls FORWARD (up) and REVERSE (down); public 1 is REVERSE, but `s11.c` passes that level to the PIA uncomplemented and no retained page draws the coin-door circuit, so which position closes its contact is not established and its polarity is left undeclared.
- **Flipper buttons.** The Flipper Solenoids table routes each flipper's ground from the CPU (`ORN-GRY CPU CN19-2` left, `ORN-VIO CPU CN19-1` right) through the cabinet flipper switch to the Flipper PCB, so the coil is energised while the switch is closed and the released button rests open. The parts list names a single Flipper Switch (Left) 180-5048-01 and a Flipper Switch, Double (Right) 180-5122-00 for the two right flippers.
- **Relay-paired wiring.** On drives 1-8 the coil and its flash lamps share only the CPU-to-PPB wire (`GRY-...` on CN-11) and the transistor. The coil leaves the PPB board on J2 (`VIO-...`, or `WHT-ORG` and `WHT/BLU` into the +50 V boosters on drives 3 and 6) and returns to `+VL`; the flash lamps take `+32 VR` on J6-4,5 (`ORG`) and return on J9 (`BLK-...`).

## What the ROM's own service tests say

The US 3.03 ROM prints the machine's own names and wire colours in its service tests, which settles every public number against the manual.

- **Switches.** With every public switch at 0 the Active Switch Test shows NONE; holding any public address 1-64 at 1 shows its name, its column and row wires and its `#nn`. The wires are exactly the manual's chart headers, so the column-major numbering is confirmed for all 64 addresses, and 34, 35 and 58-61 are named NOT USED. No switch is closed at rest and every one closes at public 1, so the 58 used matrix switches are `normally_closed: false` (the S11 read path hands the CPU `core_getSwCol` uninverted, so public 1 is the closed contact). The ROM's names mostly match the manual's; the ones that differ are the tombstone limit switches (33 and 36 are `UP/DWN BAR - UP` and `- DOWN`, 37 is `TOMB STONE`), 38 (`CRYPT VUK (LEFT)`), 52 (`RIGHT SUPER VUK`), 8 (`BUYIN BUTTON`), and 63 and 64, which the ROM calls `LEFT FLIPPER` and `RIGHT FLIPPER` where the manual's chart says End of Stroke. Holding public 82 shows `RIGHT FLIPPER #64` and 84 `LEFT FLIPPER #63`; 81, 83 and 85-88 show NONE.
- **Lamps.** The discrete Lamp Test lights one lamp at a time and names it, lamp 64 first and then 63 down to 1. The lit public lamp always equals the displayed number. The ROM names lamps by position where the manual names them by feature: lamps 1-12 are `WHEEL - #1` to `#12`, the ring of twelve inserts around the Crypt Jam hub; 53-56 are the four right-ramp feature lamps (`RIGHT RAMP - TOP/RT`, `- BOT/RT`, `- BOT/LT`, `- TOP/LT`, which the manual calls Living Dead, Grave-digger, Chainsaw Mode and Play the Organ); 37-40 the four left-ramp lamps (Rats, Goblins, Ghosts, Bats); 41-43 and 49-51 the upper and lower rows of the drop-target inserts (`DROPS - UPPER LEFT` and `DROPS - LOWER LEFT`, which the manual calls Left Drop Trgt. and Mystery Door 1); 31-36 the left and right spinner inserts; 15, 14 and 16 are `LAUNCH BUTTON`, `BUYIN BUTTON` and `CREDIT BUTTON`, the last being the manual's Start Button lamp.
- **Coils.** The Cycling Coils test fires, in this order and with these names: 1 `LOCK OUT`, 2 `BALL RELEASE`, 3 `AUTO LAUNCH 50V`, 4 `DROP TARGET`, 5 `SCOOP`, 6 `LEFT VUK 50V`, 7 `TOP VUK 50V`, 8 `KNOCKER`, 9 `DIVERTER`, 11 `G.I. RELAY`, 12-14 `NOT USED`, 15 `MOTOR UP/DWN`, 16 `SHAKER MOTOR`, 17 `LEFT TURBO`, 18 `BOTTOM TURBO`, 19 `RIGHT TURBO`, 20 `LEFT SLING`, 21 `RIGHT SLING`, 22 `LASER KICK 50V`. After each of drives 1-8 it fires the Left/Right relay (10) with the matching right-hand flash lamp, printed `FL:` with the bulb composition (`1-INS 2-PLFD 1-BP` for 1R and so on), and those compositions are exactly the Special Coil Wiring Diagram's. The test pulses 12-14 even though nothing is fitted: it sweeps every driver, and the wiring diagram's NO COIL AT THIS LOCATION decides.
- **Causal pairs.** In the Laser Kick Test, closing 17 (the left outlane) fires 22 immediately, and closing 38, 52 or 55 fires 6, 7 or 5 about 0.3 s later; closing 18, 25 or 26 fires nothing. In the Tombstone Test, holding Start drives 15, and closing 33, 36 or 37 turns the `UP`, `DOWN` or `TARGET` line ON.

## The tombstone, the VUKs and the other mechanisms

**Tombstone (gravestone).** A motor, cam and switch assembly (500-5742-01, below the playfield) raises and lowers a tombstone target in front of the Left VUK. The Gravestone Up & Down Test says the CPU senses the motor through two limit switches (addresses 33 and 36), each closing "just prior to the limit" of travel and never both at once, and that holding Start pulses the relay. The retained table models it as a one-solenoid reverse motor on drive 15 with 180 steps, switch 33 closing at positions 160-180 and 36 at 0-20, and pulses switch 37 (Tombstone Score) when a ball strikes it from below. The test text names Q23 as the relay driver, which is the transistor the same manual draws on drive 16; drive 15's drawn transistor is Q24. The two readings agree on the device and its address and differ only on a driver transistor designator, so both are kept in the device note. The Switch Part Numbers table asterisks 33, 36 and 37 (legend: location is in the cabinet) although the mechanism is on the playfield; the addresses have no callout on the location drawing, which is the only effect of the marking. The rules lower the tombstone when C-R-Y-P-T is spelled (multiball ready) and for the Monster Jackpot; shots into the Left VUK after it is lowered are "Robbing the Crypt".

**Left VUK and Super VUK.** The Left VUK (500-5306-03, switch 38, drive 6 at 50 V) throws the ball up from below the playfield. The Super VUK (500-5116-06, switch 52, drive 7) at the top right catches balls from the Trough Assembly (500-5652-00), whose Small Trough (53) and Large Trough (54) switches the retained table treats as a "Subway"; the Super VUK throws them back onto the playfield. The manual does not say which shots feed the subway.

**Power Scoop** (500-5741-00, switch 55, drive 5): collects a lit Creature Feature, starts the Crypt Jam six-ball play after every creature feature is complete, and pays the Electric Chair award (1 million times the balls in play).

**Drop-target bank.** Three Guillotine drop targets (41-43) reset together from drive 4. Super Guillotine starts them at 3 million and counts up while they are dropped. **Eyeball banks.** Two stand-up banks of three (20-22 left, 28-30 right) have no reset coil; the left return lane lights the Mystery Door values and a miss turns them off.

**Diverter** (drive 9, 27-1400): enabled by the ROM ("Use Diverter", Audit 92) and unsensed. **Laser Kickback** (drive 22, kickback and knocker assembly 500-5081-00): the Laser Kick Test text says rolling the ball over the left outlane switch fires it; the rules call it the Crypt Kicker. **Shaker** (drive 16): Adj. 48 turns the feature on or off.

**Ball handling.** Six pinballs serve the machine; seven trough switches (9-15) run along the slanted trough, with the shooter-lane switch at 16. Drive 1 fires the 6 Ball Ass'y Lockout (25-1240), a different coil type from every other drive, and drive 2 releases the ball. Drive 3 is the autoplunger (Ball Launch, 23-800 at 50 V). The player triggers it with the cabinet door handle, whose switch is the matrix's Launch Button (62, part 180-5073-00, the same part as the Buy-In Button at 8). The same handle opens the Door Prize doors and fires the Kill Shot: "Shoot the Lit Drop Target Bank with the door handle". The retained table's trough is a surrogate and its timing is not a measurement. The ROM's own serve, from a game start with balls on 9-14: nothing fires at power-up; after Start the ROM pulses the lockout (1) every two seconds until a ball closes 15, then pulses the ball release (2) every 2.3 s until 15 opens. With the ball resting on the shooter lane switch 16 nothing fires; pressing the launch button 62 fires the autoplunger (3) at once. Start also turns on the tombstone motor (15), which stayed on with neither limit switch closed, and fires the drop-target reset (4).

## Rules and flow, from the manual

Creature Features (collected at the scoop): Super Keeper Targets (K-E-E-P-E-R eyeball targets), Thunder Storm (left and right ramps), Skull Cracking (captive ball adds balls), Door Prize (pick a door with the flipper buttons, open it with the door handle; a zombie ends it), Frightmare (30 spins for 25 million), Psycho Pops (20 pop bumper hits), Robbing the Crypt, Lite Axe-tra Ball, Super Guillotine, Werewolf Countdown (30 million bonus counting down until the left spinner is hit), Video Mode and Electric Chair. A lit left or right spinner lights the scoop's Creature Feature; the right return lane lights it on a short timer and Chop Pops lights the turbo bumpers for 1 million per hit; Scared to Death pays a value as the ball leaves the right outlane. Play the Organ, Living Dead, Chainsaw Mode and Grave Digger start from the right ramp or when Bats, Rats, Ghosts and Gobblins (left ramp, 1 to 4 million) are complete. Shooting C-R-Y-P-T lowers the tombstone for multiball; shooting the tombstone starts it; the left ramp collects the Jackpot (raised by the left spinner), the right ramp then pays double (raised by the right spinner), and completing the Double Jackpot raises the tombstone again for the Monster Jackpot. The Crypt Jam is six-ball play at 1 million per switch.

## Cabinet, service and setup

Open the coin door, set FORWARD/REVERSE to REVERSE and press STEP to enter the diagnostics. The tests run in the order the manual lists: Easy Trough Clear, burn-in, sound tests, the dot-matrix display test, the Laser Kick Test, the Gravestone Up & Down Test, the Switch Test and Active Switches, the lamp tests and the flash lamp / coil tests. The switch test shows the switch name, row and column wire colours and number. The manual's adjustments include 48 SHAKER MOTOR and 61 PRINTER INTERFACE; its fuse chart lists a 50 V 5 A laser-kick fuse (PPB F9) and separate 32 V and 50 V coil fuses.

## Timing and tuning

None of the retained table's timings (trough kicker delays, VUK steps, tombstone acceleration, shaker nudge) is a machine fact; they are table-owned tuning. The manual gives no coil timing.

## Recreation guidance

Bind switches by public address with the controller's level as delivered, drive 84 and 82 for the flippers (the right button moves both right flippers), model GI as inverted solenoid 11, treat 25-32 as the right-hand flash lamps of drives 1-8, treat 12-14 as absent hardware, and bind 17-22 to the turbo bumpers, slingshots and laser kickback in the printed order the ROM's coil test confirms. Model the trough as a stack the lockout (1) feeds onto 15 one ball at a time and the release (2) kicks from 15 into the shooter lane, and fire the autoplunger (3) only from the launch button. Do not model a physical end-of-stroke on 63 and 64, and do not treat 37-44 as playfield outputs.

## Open items

- Spatial placement: all of it comes from one derivative lineage of recreations. The three factory location drawings (switches, lamps, flash lamps and coils) validate {validated} placements whose own callouts land within 0.07 normalized; the location drawings leave out the G.I., and they put switches 24, 47, 54 and 57, the scoop coil, one 1R flash lamp and the drive-15 box elsewhere than the table, or draw no callout of their own for 9, 37, the second 1R and 2R lamps and the 7R and 8R lamps at the Crypt Jam wheel (whose one combined `7R 8R` box marks the wheel, not either bulb), so those stay observed. The Lamp Bulbs & Sockets drawing (printed page 39) marks every playfield bulb, G.I. included, by bulb type only; measuring it against the lamp and flash-lamp sockets is the remaining factory route to the G.I. positions. Beyond that, flasher and GI placements are visual effects of the table, not a socket survey; the manual's #89 flash-lamp counts disagree by one (32 drawn on the wiring diagram, 31 listed in the bulb table); and coils 1 and 8 and switches 33 and 36 have no object in the retained table. Promotion needs a reproducible measurement of the three location drawings or an independent table.
- The W7 jumper (PinMAME DIP 0, PIA2 PA7): the manual's CPU jumper table does not list it and pinned PinMAME does not say what it selects; the harness cannot set a DIP, so no run has compared the two settings.
- The Green FORWARD/REVERSE button's contact polarity (above).
- The retained table runs the MOD ROM; a table that runs a factory ROM would be a better runtime witness. The committed game-start run covers the serve only, so the Door Prize and Kill Shot uses of the door handle are manual and script knowledge only.
- The service and ROM evidence is US 3.03; the other five drivers share the machine's hardware but were not re-run.
